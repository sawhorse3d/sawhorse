# Sawhorse 0.1.0-alpha.4

A pre-release: free, open source (MIT OR Apache-2.0), no account. Try it in the browser at https://sawhorse3d.com/app/ or install it below.

## What is new since 0.1.0-alpha.3

**A circle, an arc or a polygon is one curve** ([#3](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/3))
- **Select it whole.** Hovering any segment highlights the whole curve, and a click selects it. The status line says what it is, in your units: "Selected a circle (24 segments, radius 300 mm)". Shift and Ctrl add and toggle the whole curve. A window takes a curve only when all of it is inside; a crossing box takes it when it touches any segment. A click exactly on one of its points still selects that point.
- **Move, Rotate, Scale and Ctrl-copy it as one.** A circle that is moved, turned or scaled evenly is still a circle, with its new centre and radius ("Scaled a circle ×2: radius 600 mm."). Each copy is a new curve. Moving one point of a curve on its own is refused, and the message names Edit > Explode Curve. SketchUp bends the curve instead; this is a deliberate difference ([ADR-0013](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/adr/0013-curves.md)).
- **Erase it as one.** The Eraser takes the whole curve on a click or across a drag, in one undo step. **Edit > Explode Curve** turns the selected curves back into plain edges.
- **Drawing across a curve splits it.** A line across a circle leaves two arcs, and each one selects whole.
- **Rims stay curves after Push/Pull.** Pull a circle or a polygon into a solid and the curve becomes the rim of both ends: a click on a rim selects all of it. The walls of a pulled circle come out soft and smooth, so it reads as a cylinder; a polygon's walls stay hard. Push it back flat and the circle is a curve in the sketch again.
- **Centre inference.** Hover a segment of a circle, an arc or a polygon with a drawing or moving tool and its centre shows as a small blue dot labelled Centre. Move there and the cursor snaps to it.
- **Offset of a circle moves its radius**, as SketchUp does: a 200 mm circle offset 50 mm out is a 250 mm circle with the same 24 segments. An arc and a polygon offset the same way.
- Saved `.shz` files keep curves and rims, and older files open as before.

**Sketch geometry moves** ([#2](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/2))
- **Move, Rotate, Scale, Ctrl-copy and arrays** (`x3`, `/4`) work on lines, rectangles, circles, arcs and polygons before they are pulled into a solid. They use the same inference, arrow-key locks, typed distances and one undo step as a solid. "Sketch edges don't move yet" is gone.
- **Part of a shape moves within its plane.** Drag one corner of a rectangle and its two edges stretch to it; the rest stays put.
- **A whole shape moves anywhere.** Lift it off the ground and it lands on a new plane, ready to push/pull.
- **What it lands on joins it**, as drawing there would. Touching edges become one, crossing edges split both shapes, and a move that would twist a face into a bow-tie splits it in two.
- Moved and copied shapes push/pull like any other: the solid is exactly the shape's area times the distance.

**Fixed**
- **The Eraser dragged across a circle or a polygon** takes the whole shape and the face inside it, in one undo step; Ctrl+Z brings both back.
- **Messages read as sentences.** A refusal that names something no longer there says so in words ("that curve no longer exists"), for section planes, sheets and their items too. Move, Rotate and Scale count shapes, so a circle and its face are one ("Moved 2 circles 100 mm along +red"). The box beside the Scale factor no longer shows "mm" next to a factor of 2.
- **Drawings of soft edges.** Sheet viewports and SVG drawings draw a soft edge only where it is the outline. A cylinder's front elevation is two lines, its sides, not its 24 seams. Hidden edges are left out of sheet viewports, as they already were of SVG drawings.
- **A DXF's `CIRCLE` and `ARC` come in as curves**, and the import report counts them ("3 curves came in as curves").
- **Geometry far from the origin, or very thin.** The property tests and the fuzzer found several kernel bugs a user could have hit. A tiny part far from the origin could show the wrong volume: a 0.1 mm cylinder 6.6 km out, turned, measured 244 times its real volume. A very thin shape far out could leave the view unable to draw the model, for example a 0.07 mm band inside a 9 km circle; so could two holes in a face that touch at one point. Create Group from Slice could refuse a cap far along a tilted plane. Two edges crossing at an angle too small to see could put a point at infinity, which only reopening the saved file noticed. All of these are fixed, and each case found is now a test.

The full list is in the [changelog](https://sawhorse3d.com/changelog/).

## Not yet

- `.skp` export (you can open SketchUp files, not save them).
- Fillet and chamfer tools.
- Copy and paste in place.
- Back and Bottom views.
- Text, and radius or diameter dimensions in the model.
- Weld Edges: erasing the line that split a circle leaves two arcs.
- Signed installers (see below; the plan is in the [code signing policy](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/SIGNING.md)).
- Render credits: not launched. The app shows nothing about them unless you set a render service of your own in Edit > Settings.

## Known limits

- **A circle drawn on a solid's face is plain edges, not a curve.** It imprints or cuts the face as before, and pulling it gives walls without rims. To get a curve, draw the circle in a sketch (off the solid) and push it.
- **SketchUp files' curves come in as plain edges.** openskp 1.3.0, which reads `.skp` files, keeps no record of which edges make up a curve, and Sawhorse does not guess ([skp-compat.md](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/skp-compat.md#curves)). A SketchUp circle's segments move, erase and offset as edges; its centre still snaps.
- **Zoomed out on a large circle, a click in the middle of a segment can pick a point** instead of the circle, because short segments on screen leave little room between the points. Zoom in, or use a selection window ([#12](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/12)).
- **Moving or importing many curves is slow.** The time grows faster than their number: 200 circles took about 3 s to move and 400 about 10 s on the development machine ([#11](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/11)).
- **After a move that splits a shape where it lands**, for example a circle dropped across a rectangle's edge, the selection holds only one of the pieces. Click again to select what you want; the geometry itself is right ([#7](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/7)).
- **A Move drag in a big sketch stutters.** Each preview frame works out every edge of the sketch, not only the moving ones, so dragging keeps up only to about 1,500 to 2,000 sketch edges on the development machine ([#8](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/8)).
- **A scene saved with the default style does not bring the grid back.** Going to such a scene leaves the style as it is, so a grid hidden after the scene was saved stays hidden. Show it again with View > Grid. Updating the scene while the grid shows does not help, because it saves the default style again. A scene saved with any other style (X-ray faces, say, or the grid hidden) puts its grid back as it was saved. Tracked as [#6](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/6).
- Models of a few thousand faces are quick; a 13.5k-face SketchUp office opened in about 4.5 s on the development machine. [docs/LIMITS.md](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/LIMITS.md) has the measurements and the hard limits.
- openskp 1.3.0 refuses a real SketchUp 2018 save and files from SketchUp 2012 and older ([what comes across from the rest](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/skp-compat.md)).
- Parts sized from their bounds are exact for boards and generous for anything slanted or rounded: check them against what you mean to build.
- The installers are not code-signed yet (below).

## Unsigned installers

The installers are built in the open by GitHub Actions but are **not code-signed yet**, so the system warns before the first launch. To open them:

**Windows** (the `.exe` or the `.msi`)
1. Run the installer. Windows shows "Windows protected your PC".
2. Click **More info**.
3. Click **Run anyway**.

**macOS** (the `.dmg`, Apple Silicon)
1. Open the disk image and drag Sawhorse to Applications.
2. Open Sawhorse. macOS says it cannot verify the app; click **Done** (not Move to Trash).
3. Open **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, scroll to **Security**: "Sawhorse was blocked to protect your Mac". Click **Open Anyway**.
4. Confirm with your password or Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** once more. Later launches open normally.

**Linux**: the `.AppImage` needs to be made executable (`chmod +x Sawhorse_*.AppImage`); the `.deb` and `.rpm` install as usual.

To check a download against the release, put it next to `SHA256SUMS.txt` and run `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` (Linux; on macOS `shasum -a 256 -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt`; on Windows compare `Get-FileHash <file>` with its line).

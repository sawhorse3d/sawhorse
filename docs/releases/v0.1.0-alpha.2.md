# Sawhorse 0.1.0-alpha.2 (draft release notes)

Status: prepared 2026-10-12, **not tagged**. The maintainer tags the release (`v0.1.0-alpha.2`) after the planned repository recreation; GitHub Actions then builds the installers (`.github/workflows/release.yml`). These notes are the release page's text.

A pre-release: free, open source (MIT OR Apache-2.0), no account. Try it in the browser at https://sawhorse3d.com/app/ or install it below.

## What is new since 0.1.0-alpha.1

**For the shop**
- **Cut list from furniture that is not a solid.** Most furniture drawn in SketchUp is open faces. A group or component of them is now a part: Sawhorse tries to close it, and otherwise sizes it from the box around it and flags it "not solid (sizes from bounds)" in the pane, the CSV, the PDF, the labels and the nesting booklet. Loose faces are counted, and **Make parts from loose faces** turns each connected piece into a part. Untick a part to leave it out of the totals.
- **Board feet on the lumber you buy.** Board feet are counted on the rough (nominal) thickness: a 3/4 in shelf comes from 4/4, one inch. The stock library knows nominal and actual thickness (3/4 ply is 23/32, 4/4 S4S is 3/4, 5/4 is 1 1/16 ...), fills the actual one in from the nominal, and lets you type your supplier's.
- **Fractions typed every way**: `1-1/2"`, `1 1/2`, `1.5"`, `1½` and `1'-1-1/2"` all read the same in every length field.

**Modeling and looks**
- **Materials and textures** you can paint on faces, solids, groups and components and position with handles; **styles** (shaded, hidden line, X-ray, shop drawing ...) and **shadows** from a sun by date and time. (lane 10)
- **Soft and smooth edges**: the Eraser softens (Ctrl), hides (Shift) and unsoftens (Ctrl+Shift); Soften edges by angle in the Styles pane. Round parts read round, and SketchUp's soft edges come in as they were.
- **Section planes** with exact filled cuts, and **drawing sheets**: scaled viewports, dimensions, labels, a cut-list table and a title block, out as PDF, SVG, DXF and PNG, or printed. (lane 12)
- **Plugins**: tools, panels and commands in sandboxed JavaScript with declared permissions; three samples. (lane 11)

**Files**
- `.skp`: a definition someone named and placed once comes in as a component (a "Leg"), as SketchUp made it; an empty file opens as an empty model in its own units.
- `.shz` thumbnails are small WebP pictures.

**Fixed**: many, from two independent verifications; see the [changelog](https://sawhorse3d.com/changelog/).

**Security**: the plugin sandbox's three locks (an allowlist of globals, the page's content security policy, sources parsed before they run); a plugin could reach the network through `FontFace` before (fixed in lane 11, never in a release).

## Not yet

- `.skp` export (you can open SketchUp files, not save them).
- Fillet and chamfer tools.
- Copy and paste in place.
- Back and Bottom views.
- Text, and radius or diameter dimensions in the model.
- Signed installers (see below).
- Render credits: not launched; the app shows nothing about them unless you set a render service of your own in Edit > Settings.

## Known limits

- Models of a few thousand faces are quick; a 13.5k-face SketchUp office opened in about 4 s on the development machine. [docs/LIMITS.md](../LIMITS.md) has the measurements and the hard limits.
- openskp 1.3.0 refuses a real SketchUp 2018 save and files from SketchUp 2012 and older ([what comes across from the rest](../skp-compat.md)).
- Parts sized from their bounds are exact for boards and generous for anything slanted or rounded: check them against what you mean to build.

## Unsigned installers

The installers are built in the open by GitHub Actions but are **not code-signed yet**, so the system warns before the first launch. To open them:

**Windows** (the `.exe` or the `.msi`)
1. Run the installer. Windows shows "Windows protected your PC".
2. Click **More info**.
3. Click **Run anyway**.

**macOS** (the `.dmg`, Apple Silicon)
1. Open the disk image and drag Sawhorse to Applications.
2. Open Sawhorse. macOS says it cannot verify the app; click **Done** (not Move to Trash).
3. Open **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, scroll to **Security**: "Sawhorse was blocked to protect your Mac". Click **Open Anyway**.
4. Confirm with your password or Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** once more. Later launches open normally.

**Linux**: the `.AppImage` needs to be made executable (`chmod +x Sawhorse_*.AppImage`); the `.deb` and `.rpm` install as usual.

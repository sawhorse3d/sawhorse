# Sawhorse 0.1.0-alpha.7

A pre-release: free, open source (MIT OR Apache-2.0), no account. Try it in the browser at https://sawhorse3d.com/app/ or install it below.

## What is new since 0.1.0-alpha.6

**Glue-ups show in the cut list, and the narrowest strip is yours to set** ([#56](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/56), [#67](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/67), [#57](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/57), [#70](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/70))
- **A glued part's cut list row says what it is glued from.** In alpha.6 the Nesting section listed every glue-up, but the part's own row in the cut list said nothing. Now, while the Nesting section is open, a glued row's notes say "glued from 2 (4/4 x 12 x 96)", naming the board as the stock library does. After a change the note goes until the nesting is worked out again. The CSV, the printed cut list and the labels leave it out, so they read the same whether Nesting is open or not.
- **A board counts a glued part once.** A board holding both strips of one panel said "2 parts" while the totals said "1 of 1 part placed". Now it says "1 part in 2 strips", or "2 parts in 3 pieces" when it holds a whole part as well. When a part's strips are too long to share a board, each board names its share instead ("1 strip of 4a"), and the part counts once in the totals. The pane's caption and diagram label, the PDF booklet's Stock pieces list and each SVG's header say the same.
- **Minimum strip.** In alpha.6 a last strip narrower than 1 1/2 in, or than a quarter of the board when that is less, was evened out with the full strip before it, and that rule was fixed. Now **Minimum strip** sits beside Kerf, Trim and Joint loss in the Nesting section. It is empty, reading "auto", until you type a length, and auto is the alpha.6 rule. Type 2 in where every joint shows, say, and a last strip narrower than that is evened out the same way, never past half the board. 0 never evens it out, so a part auto would glue can be left on no stock when its last strip would be narrower than the kerf. Clearing the field goes back to auto. Each change is one undo step, and the footer under the diagrams names the setting ("min strip 2 in") when something is glued up.
- **Older files are unchanged.** The setting is saved with the file (`settings.minStrip`, 0 to 12 in) only when it is set, so a file that never had it, or had it cleared, is saved and hashed as before.

**The Nesting fields read and keep what you type** ([#89](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/89), [#90](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/90), [#92](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/92), [#95](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/95), [#93](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/93), [#96](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/96), [#98](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/98), [#101](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/101), [#102](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/102), [#100](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/100), [#106](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/106), [#88](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/88))
- **A length exactly halfway reads as the upper one.** The Kerf, Trim, Joint loss and Minimum strip fields, and the other places those lengths are written (the footer under the diagrams, a trim step in the cut sequence, a stock's actual thickness), could round a length exactly halfway down. In a millimetre document the built-in 1/8 in kerf and joint loss read 3.17 mm; they now read 3.18 mm, and 3/8 in reads 9.53 mm. In decimal inches 3/16 in read 0.187 in and now reads 0.188 in; in fractions 3/128 in now reads 1/32 in and 29/128 in reads 15/64 in. What is stored does not change.
- **What you are typing is left alone.** Text typed in a focused field stays until Enter, leaving the field or Escape, even when Undo, Redo or the pause after typing brings a new reading. Before, the pause turned 3.1 typed in a millimetre document into "3.1 mm" under the cursor, so the next key gave "3.1 mm5" and an error. Escape gives the text up for the value stored now.
- **The unit comes back.** A typed length that reads the same as the one shown (3.18 in a millimetre document, where the kerf reads 3.18 mm) used to stay on screen without its unit. Enter or leaving the field now shows "3.18 mm".
- **A red mark goes with its text.** A refused length stays as typed, marked in red with its reason in the tooltip. When the field has been left and Redo or a change of units brings a new reading, the mark and the reason now go with the old text.
- **A change right after Undo keeps what Undo did.** Set Kerf and Trim to 3.1 mm, Undo (Trim back to 0), then type 3.15 in Kerf and press Enter: Trim came back as 3.1 mm. Now each change is laid over the settings stored now. Undo and Redo put the fields back at once, and the diagrams and the line under them are dimmed until the nesting catches up.
- **Built-in stock says what it puts back,** in the document's units. In a millimetre document the status line reads "Stock back to the built-in sheets and boards: kerf 3.18 mm, no trim, joint loss 3.18 mm, minimum strip auto, table saw.", and the button's tooltip in the Stock dialog says the same. The built-in kerf is still 1/8 in there ([#58](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/58)).

**Scale, Move and Follow Me keep up with Undo, Delete and Hide** ([#76](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/76), [#82](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/82), [#85](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/85), [#105](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/105), [#111](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/111), [#114](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/114), [#86](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/86), [#104](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/104), [#81](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/81), [#83](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/83))
- **After Undo or Redo, Scale's box goes back round the shape.** In alpha.6 the dashed box and its grips stayed where the scaled shape had been. A value typed at that point was worse: it undid the step before the scale too (the Push/Pull that made the board) and then failed with "that solid no longer exists". Now a value typed after Undo or Redo scales the selection about its centre. In Move, Rotate and Offset a distance or angle typed right after Undo no longer undoes a further step; it says what to do first and changes nothing.
- **File > New, Close, Open and Edit > Restore Unsaved Work** no longer leave the old model's box over the new one, and drop a step half done in whatever tool is active.
- **Delete takes Scale's box with the solid.** Delete, Backspace, Edit > Delete and the Outliner's Delete left the box and its 26 grips over nothing. Now they go, and so they do after Explode Curve, Make Group, Make Component and Explode, and after a step that hides what was selected: Edit > Hide, the Outliner's eye, hiding a tag, putting the selection on a hidden tag, a scene that hides it, or Close Group / Component. In every tool, a step half done (a line started, a move picked up, a push under way) is dropped when what it works on is deleted or hidden. Showing things, or a scene that only moves the camera, leaves it alone.
- **Follow Me lets its path go when the model changes under it.** After Undo, a click on a profile could sweep it along a path that was gone and add it to the solid. Now Undo, Redo, New, Close, Open, Restore Unsaved Work, Delete and the steps above clear the path, and a click on a profile asks for one: select it and press W again.
- **Scale's status line says which key is held and which is tapped:** "Hold Shift: uniform. Tap Ctrl: switch between the opposite grip and the centre." What the keys do has not changed.

**Big sketches and many curves no longer wait** ([#11](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/11), [#61](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/61), [#64](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/64), [#87](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/87), [#65](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/65))
- **Importing a file of many circles and arcs** (a DXF, or a SketchUp file with loose curves) took time that grew with the square of the curves. Now they cost about what the same strokes cost plain: 400 DXF circles import in about 3.9 s, where they took about 7.5 s. An import leaves exactly what it left before.
- **A Move, Copy or Array of a big sketch selection** grows with the selection, not its square, curves or polylines. A typed Move of 400 circles of 48 segments takes about 0.22 s from Enter, where it took about 3.6 s, and the same rings as polylines about 0.27 s, where they took about 4.9 s. What a move, copy or array leaves is the same as before.
- **Starting a Move, Rotate or Scale drag of a big selection** pauses less. With everything selected, a Move's first preview comes about 0.15 s after the press for 1,000 rectangles (4,000 sketch edges), where it took about 0.28 s, and about 0.29 s for 400 circles, where it took about 3 s. Scale's grips come up in about 0.07 and 0.2 s, where they took about 0.2 and 2.5 s.
- **A long stroke goes in in time that grows with its length,** not its square, and leaves the same sketch. A stroke certain to take a sketch past its 20,000 edges is refused before it is drawn, with the same message as before ("a sketch would hold 23999 edges; at most 20000"), where it held the app for seconds first.
- These times were measured in the browser build on the development machine.

**Unsaved work is never hidden or lost behind an old offer** ([#94](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/94))
- In alpha.6 an unanswered "Restore unsaved work?" stayed the offer at every later start, so work drawn after it and left unsaved was never offered, and Restore of the old offer then wrote over that newer work for good.
- Now every set of unsaved work is kept until it is restored or discarded. A start offers the newest first and says how many older sets are set aside too ("1 older set of unsaved work is set aside too."). Discard offers the next at once. After Restore the others stay set aside, and Edit > Restore Unsaved Work brings them back one at a time, newest first.
- Restore sets the model you have open aside first when it holds unsaved changes, and says so ("The model you had open is set aside as unsaved work: Edit > Restore Unsaved Work brings it back after 1 other set."). Restored work comes back as an untitled model, so Save never writes it over an open file.
- While work is set aside and its offer is not on the status line, the line ends with "Unsaved work set aside. Click to restore or discard."; the click shows the offer again.
- At most 5 sets are kept, each up to 64 MB as before. A start that would keep a sixth deletes the oldest and says so on the status line. What older builds kept is read as it is.

**The cabinet generator's back takes a thickness** ([#60](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/60))
- The back was always thin sheet, 6 mm in a metric document and 1/4 in otherwise, so a cabinet with a 16 or 18 mm back had to be drawn by hand. Now the panel has a **Back thickness** field right after **Stock**, in the document's units. It starts at 6 mm or 1/4 in as before, so a thicker back is one number to type. It is greyed out while **Back panel** is off.
- The back stays in the same place, between the sides at the rear from the toe kick up, and the top, bottom and shelves are that much less deep: a 600 x 720 x 560 mm cabinet with an 18 mm back lists Back 18 x 564 x 620 and a top 542 deep. A back thicker than 1/4 in is listed as solid stock like the sides, unless you give it a material such as plywood.
- A back of no thickness says "The back needs a thickness.", one that leaves no depth says "The depth must be more than the stock and the back together", with the two added up, and the button waits. The line under the button names the back ("Adds a cabinet: 600 x 720 x 560, 1 shelf, 18 back, 100 toe kick.").
- A cabinet made with the defaults is the one made before. The sample plugin is version 1.1.0.

**An MCP server for agents** ([#14](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/14), [#63](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/63))
- `sawhorse-mcp <folder>` is a Model Context Protocol server on the same kernel as the app. An agent starts it and talks to it over stdin and stdout (MCP revision 2025-11-25). Its tools make, open and save a model; add a named solid board from a size, a corner and a material; apply the same ops a plugin sends; read a summary of the parts, the cut list and the nesting (the same JSON `sawhorse-cli` prints); and undo and redo. Every change is one undo step, all or nothing, and answers with the cut list rows it added, removed and changed. A `.shz` it saves opens in the app as it is.
- It reads and writes only `.shz` files under the folder it was started with. An absolute path, a path with `..`, a link that leads out of the folder, and a name that would mean something else on Windows are refused. A save is written to a new file beside the target, read back and checked before it replaces the target, so a failed save leaves the old file as it was. Nothing goes to the network.
- It is built from a checkout, like `sawhorse-cli` (`cargo build --release -p sawhorse-mcp`, in `crates/`), and it is not in the installers. Its tests run on Linux, Windows and macOS on every change, and on Windows and macOS the release binary is also run once on a fresh folder. After each change to the main branch, the Windows and macOS binaries that run made are kept for seven days on the run's page on GitHub Actions, unsigned and not a release, for trying the server without building it. The tools and a worked example are in [docs/MCP.md](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/MCP.md).
- @aramshaw reported on [#14](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/14) running such a Windows binary from a real MCP client on Windows 11, and from a folder on a network share; the one gap he found there is under Known limits.

**Linux: GNOME and Cinnamon name Sawhorse at logout while a model has unsaved changes** ([#73](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/73), [#119](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/119))
- On KDE Plasma the desktop app's own question already showed at logout. GNOME and Cinnamon end the session without closing the windows first, so nothing was asked. Now, while the model has unsaved changes, the desktop's Log Out, Restart and Power Off dialogs list Sawhorse with the reason "This model has unsaved changes". After a save Sawhorse is not listed.
- On GNOME this is a warning and a chance to cancel, not a block: GNOME still logs out when its dialog's 60 second countdown ends (seen by @akvalentine977 on GNOME 48), and the work is offered back at the next start.
- An AppImage installs no desktop file, so GNOME may not list it; that was not tried. On KDE Plasma the app asks as before, and Windows and macOS are not touched.
- Tried by hand by @akvalentine977 on 2026-11-19, on builds of the pull request: with the .deb and .rpm on GNOME 46, 48.7, 50.1 and 51.1 and Cinnamon 6.6.4, and on KDE Plasma 6.8.2 with an .rpm and an AppImage. Those builds gave each call to the session manager up after two seconds. What landed waits on the session manager's answer without a time limit, and that change was not tried on a desktop.

**Fixed**
- **A second rectangle stays on the ground** ([#9](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/9), [#69](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/69)). With a rectangle on the ground, a second one started level with a corner the cursor had rested on could stand up on a vertical plane, because its far corner caught the blue line through that corner. Now, while a rectangle's plane is still open, that blue line is skipped, as the blue line through its own first corner already was. Red and green from that corner stay, an arrow-key lock keeps every line, and a far corner on a real point, edge or face off the ground still stands the rectangle up.
- **Every menu title opens a menu, and the desktop app has Help** ([#97](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/97)). The menu bar showed Draw and Window, and Help in the desktop app, with nothing in them, so a click opened nothing. Now a title is in the bar only while its menu has an entry: File, Edit, View, Tools, Plugins and Help. In the desktop app Help holds User Guide, What's New and Source on GitHub, and each opens in the system's browser. It was checked in browser tests with the desktop app's bridge stood in for, on Windows and macOS.
- **A sheet or board the parts fill reads no waste, not -0.00.** With no kerf and no trim, parts that fill their stock exactly (nine 16 x 32 in parts on a 4 x 8 sheet, say) added up to a hair more than the stock by rounding, so the Nesting section, each SVG and the PDF booklet said "-0.00 sq ft waste". Now such a piece, its group and the totals read exactly full, "0.00 sq ft waste". Parts truly larger than their stock still show, and layouts do not change.
- **An extrude of exactly 0.01 mm is refused plainly** ([#66](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/66)). 0.01 mm is the shortest Push/Pull. An extrude of exactly that was rolled back as a kernel bug on some planes and went through on others. Now an extrude of 0.01 mm or less is refused on every plane with "an extrude must be more than 0.01 mm". A push/pull or offset just at the minimum that would put corners 0.01 mm or less apart is refused with a message that starts "too short here:" and ends "use a longer distance".

Behind these, more tests, time limits on the CI jobs, and setup docs that name Node 24.15 or later ([#108](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/108), [#113](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/113)). The full list is in the [changelog](https://sawhorse3d.com/changelog/).

## Not yet

- `.skp` export (you can open SketchUp files, not save them).
- Fillet and chamfer tools.
- Copy and paste in place.
- Text, and radius or diameter dimensions in the model.
- Weld Edges: erasing the line that split a circle leaves two arcs.
- Orbiting with two fingers on a trackpad, as SketchUp's trackpad mode does. Today a two-finger scroll zooms; use the Orbit tool or Option+drag ([#36](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/36)).
- A metric stock library and metric starting values. With millimetres set, the built-in stock is still US sheets and boards (4 x 8 sheets, 4/4 to 12/4 boards 8 ft long), the built-in kerf and joint loss are 1/8 in, and the nesting labels and totals stay in inches, board feet and 4 x 8 sheets. Stock… in the Nesting section lets you enter your own ([#26](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/26), [#58](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/58)).
- Cutting sizes: the cut list and the nesting use finished sizes, with no allowance for cutting a part long or wide and trimming it to size ([#54](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/54)).
- Joints that add to a part's length, such as a tenon or a half lap ([#25](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/25)).
- A minimum strip for one part: Minimum strip is one setting for the whole model ([#57](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/57)).
- The MCP server in the installers, and tools that measure the gap between two parts ([#14](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/14)).
- Double-clicking a `.shz` on a Mac or in a Linux file manager. On both, use File > Open, drag the file onto the window, or give its path on a command line.
- Signed installers (see below; the plan is in the [code signing policy](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/SIGNING.md)).
- Render credits: not launched. The app shows nothing about them unless you set a render service of your own in Edit > Settings.

## Known limits

- **On a Mac, Quit Sawhorse in the app menu (Cmd+Q) quits without asking** about unsaved changes; the red button and File > Quit Sawhorse ask. The browser build does not ask before its tab closes: it keeps your work as you go and offers it back the next time.
- **On GNOME, the logout dialog is a warning with a countdown, not a block** ([#73](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/73)). It lists Sawhorse while a model has unsaved changes, and GNOME still logs out when its 60 second countdown ends (seen by @akvalentine977 on GNOME 48). The work is offered back at the next start. The AppImage on GNOME was not tried.
- **On a Mac, a model saved with File > Save can be offered back as unsaved work at the next start** ([#103](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/103)). Nothing is lost: Restore gives back the model that was saved.
- **A double-clicked file waits behind a Restore unsaved work? offer,** and that offer is one line in the status line, with the file named there ("shelf.shz opens after you answer"), while the model area shows an empty model ([#115](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/115)). Answer the offer and the file opens.
- **Enter at the desktop app's questions chooses OK.** On Windows and macOS, pressing Enter at a question before discarding unsaved changes, or at "cabinet.shz already exists. Replace it?", discards the changes or replaces the file. Choose Cancel or press Escape to keep your work.
- **Two Sawhorse windows keep their unsaved work in the same place,** so after a crash only the window that changed last gets its work back. Save each window's model as a file to be safe ([LIMITS](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/LIMITS.md#the-desktop-app)).
- **Sketch edges that Undo removed stay selected** ([#110](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/110)), drawn in the selection's blue, and Follow Me's W reads them as a path, so a click on a profile then sweeps along edges the model no longer has.
- **A plugin command that deletes the selection while Scale is the tool leaves Scale's box** where the solid was.
- **Gluing adds only width.** A part longer than every board of its thickness goes in one piece on a thicker board long enough for it, when the library has one, and is otherwise listed under Parts on no stock, as is a part with its grain across.
- **A big drag still waits a moment as it starts:** about 0.3 to 0.4 s for 2,000 rectangles (8,000 sketch edges) on the development machine.
- **The MCP server makes every part a loose solid** ([#116](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/116)), so copies of a part are rows of their own in the cut list, not one row with a count, and do not move as one in the app.
- **The MCP server on a network share:** a first save through a link that the file server itself follows can land outside the server's folder; opening it there afterwards is refused ([#118](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/118)).
- **A SketchUp 5 file does not open** ([#15](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/15)): "legacy .skp parse failed: back-ref to unwalked slot 20617". Open it in SketchUp 2017 or later and save it again; the new save opens, as it did for the reporter's own model. Files from SketchUp 4 and older may fail the same way ([what comes across from the rest](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/skp-compat.md)).
- **SketchUp curves on faces that are not a solid come in as plain edges.** Most hand-made furniture arrives as open faces (amber meshes), and a mesh keeps no curves; the import report counts them. So does a circle drawn on a solid's face, and a loose curve inside a group or a component.
- **A circle drawn on a solid's face is plain edges, not a curve.** It imprints or cuts the face as before, and pulling it gives walls without rims. To get a curve, draw the circle in a sketch (off the solid) and push it.
- **Zoomed out on a large circle, a click in the middle of a segment can pick a point** instead of the circle. Zoom in, or use a selection window ([#12](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/12)).
- Models of a few thousand faces are quick; a 13.5k-face SketchUp office opened in about 4.5 s on the development machine. [docs/LIMITS.md](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/LIMITS.md) has the measurements and the hard limits.
- Parts sized from their bounds are exact for boards and generous for anything slanted or rounded: check them against what you mean to build.
- The installers are not code-signed (below).

## Unsigned installers

The installers are built in the open by GitHub Actions but are **not code-signed**, so the system warns before the first launch. To open them:

**Windows** (the `.exe` or the `.msi`)
1. Run the installer. Windows shows "Windows protected your PC".
2. Click **More info**.
3. Click **Run anyway**.

An antivirus may also hold an unsigned download for a short while to scan it before it lets you open it; a tester's ESET held alpha.4's for about 30 seconds and then released it.

**macOS** (the `.dmg`, Apple Silicon)
1. Open the disk image and drag Sawhorse to Applications.
2. Open Sawhorse. macOS says Apple could not verify that "Sawhorse.app" is free of malware; click **Done** (not Move to Trash).
3. Open **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, scroll to **Security**: "Sawhorse was blocked to protect your Mac". Click **Open Anyway**.
4. Confirm with your password or Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** once more. Later launches open normally.

That warning is what three people saw on Apple Silicon Macs with alpha.5, the first build signed ad hoc, and this build is signed the same way. Instead of steps 3 and 4, you can open **Terminal** after dragging Sawhorse to Applications and run

```
xattr -cr /Applications/Sawhorse.app
```

then open Sawhorse as usual. If macOS says instead that **"Sawhorse.app is damaged and can't be opened"**, as alpha.3 and alpha.4 did, the download is fine (its checksum matches): the same `xattr` command clears it, and if it still says damaged, give the app an ad-hoc signature on your Mac:

```
codesign --force --deep --sign - /Applications/Sawhorse.app
```

and open it again. If you see anything other than the four steps above, please say on [#27](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/27) which message you saw and what it took. A signed and notarised build needs none of this.

**Linux**: the `.AppImage` needs to be made executable (`chmod +x Sawhorse_*.AppImage`); the `.deb` and `.rpm` install as usual.

To check a download against the release, put it next to `SHA256SUMS.txt` and run `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` (Linux; on macOS `shasum -a 256 -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt`; on Windows compare `Get-FileHash <file>` with its line).

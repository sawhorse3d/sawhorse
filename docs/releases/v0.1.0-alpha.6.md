# Sawhorse 0.1.0-alpha.6

A pre-release: free, open source (MIT OR Apache-2.0), no account. Try it in the browser at https://sawhorse3d.com/app/ or install it below.

## What is new since 0.1.0-alpha.5

**The cut list plans real boards better** ([#49](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/49), [#53](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/53), [#62](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/62), [#50](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/50), [#51](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/51))
- **A solid part wider than every board is planned as a glue-up.** In alpha.5 a cabinet side in solid timber wider than any board, 17 in from 6 in stock say, was listed under Parts on no stock, and the boards it needed were never counted or drawn. Now it is planned as full-width strips of a board and a narrower last strip, ripped, jointed and edge-glued. Each joint costs the new **Joint loss** setting (1/8 in to start, beside Kerf and Trim in the Nesting section), and it is saved with the file.
- **The fewest joints, and no thin last strip.** The strips come from the board of that thickness that makes the part from the fewest strips. That 17 in side is 12 + 5 1/8 in from 4/4 x 12 when the stock library has it, one joint, and 6 + 6 + 5 1/4 in when 6 in boards are all there is. A last strip narrower than 1 1/2 in, or than a quarter of the board when that is less, is not left thin: it and the strip before it become two equal strips, so a 24 1/4 in top from 12 in boards is 12 + 6 1/4 + 6 1/4 in, not 12 + 12 + 1/2 in. A board shorter than the part is never used for it.
- **What you see.** The Nesting section lists every glue-up with its strips ("1 Top: glued up from 3 strips of 4/4 x 12 x 96, 12 + 6 1/4 + 6 1/4 in"), and the PDF booklet does too. Each strip is tagged after its part ("4a.1", "4a.2") and drawn at its own width, a glued part counts once in the totals, and hovering it in the model lights every strip. A part some board makes in one piece is never glued, and a part whose stock you set keeps that board.
- **Narrow parts share a board's width when that saves a board.** A 1 1/2 in rail on a 4 in board used to be charged the whole board's width. Now a board can be crosscut into sections, each ripped into parts side by side a kerf apart, so four 1 1/2 x 40 in rails come off one 4/4 x 4 x 96 board instead of two. Sawhorse uses this layout only when it saves a board, the grain stays along the board for every piece, and the cut order is still crosscut, then rip. The Nesting section, the buy totals, the SVG, the DXF and the PDF booklet all show the same boards.

**The desktop app asks before it closes over unsaved work** ([#44](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/44))
- In alpha.5 the window's close button and Alt+F4 closed the desktop app at once, and every change since the last save was gone. Now any close the system sends to the window (the X, Alt+F4, the taskbar's Close, the red button on a Mac) asks "Discard the unsaved changes to this model and close Sawhorse?" when there are unsaved changes. Cancel keeps the window and the model as they were; OK closes it. With nothing unsaved the window closes at once, as before.
- The question can never keep the window open: if it gets no answer, closing the window again two seconds later closes it, and the unsaved work is offered back the next time Sawhorse starts.
- **File > Close**, next to Open, closes the model and leaves Sawhorse running on an empty untitled one, asking first over unsaved changes. In the desktop app, **File > Exit** (Quit Sawhorse on a Mac, Quit on Linux) closes Sawhorse the way the window's X does, with the same question. The browser build has File > Close only, since a page cannot close its own tab.

**On Windows, a double-clicked .shz opens in Sawhorse, and the title bar names the model** ([#45](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/45))
- Both Windows installers now associate `.shz` files with Sawhorse: double-click one in File Explorer, or choose Sawhorse in its Open with list, and Sawhorse starts with that model. The setup `.exe` also gives the files the Sawhorse icon, and uninstalling removes the association. If Sawhorse is already open, the double-click starts a second window with that model and leaves the first one alone.
- On every system, a file's path after the program's name on a command line opens it the same way, for any file File > Open takes.
- The desktop window's title bar now shows the model's name, with a dot while it has unsaved changes ("• shelf - Sawhorse"), as a browser tab always did, so two Sawhorse windows can be told apart.
- The close question, File > Close and Exit, the double-click and the title bar were tried by hand on Windows 11 in a test build made before this release. Nobody has reported on the close question or the title bar from a Mac or Linux yet.

**The selection stays right after a move that splits, merges or breaks up shapes** ([#7](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/7), [#55](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/55))
- Move a circle so it lands across a rectangle's edge and it splits in two. In alpha.5 the selection was left on one half, so the next Move or Delete acted on that half alone. Now the selection is the circle's two halves, and the rectangle's remainder stays unselected. Rotate and Scale do the same.
- Undo of such a move selects the circle again, and Redo selects its two halves.
- A shape moved exactly onto an identical one is selected as the one shape they become; a shape lifted onto another sketch's plane across a shape there is selected as its own pieces; and a circle's rim selected on its own and moved across an edge is selected as every arc it became.
- Push/Pull follows: after a move that split a region into several pieces, P and a typed distance wait for a click on the piece to push, instead of pushing whichever piece the old selection pointed at.

**Dragging a big sketch keeps up** ([#8](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/8), [#59](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/59))
- With 1,000 rectangles selected (4,000 sketch edges), each step of a Move, Rotate or Scale drag took a quarter to a third of a second in alpha.5, so the shapes trailed behind the cursor. Now the preview works out once, as the drag starts, which end of each edge moves, and each frame moves only those ends. Measured on the development machine with 4,000 moving edges, the preview's own work went from about 210 ms to 0.5 ms a frame.
- Starting a Move of a selection that big still pauses once, for about 0.2 s, while the selected shapes' points are gathered ([#11](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/11)).

**macOS: Control+click is the right click** ([#40](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/40), [#41](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/41), [#42](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/42))
- On a Mac, Control+click is the right click in every app, and with a one-button mouse or a trackpad it is the one that comes easily. Sawhorse took it as a click with Ctrl held. Now Control+drag pans the view in every tool, the Orbit tool too, and pans a drawing sheet. Cmd is still the key for what the guide calls Ctrl with a click: Cmd+click toggles a selection or paints a whole solid. On Windows and Linux nothing changes.
- Nobody has reported this from a real Mac yet. If you use one, a comment on [#40](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/40) saying whether Control+click and Control+drag work for you would help.

**macOS: what alpha.5's signature did** ([#27](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/27))
- alpha.5 was the first build whose Mac app is signed ad hoc. On three Apple Silicon Macs it showed the usual warning that Apple could not verify "Sawhorse.app" is free of malware, and no longer said the app was damaged, as alpha.3 and alpha.4 did. Open Anyway in System Settings got past the warning, and so did `xattr -cr /Applications/Sawhorse.app` in Terminal.
- This build's Mac app is signed the same way. It is still not notarised by Apple, so the warning stays on the first open; the steps are under Unsigned installers, below.
- There is one Mac file, for Apple Silicon (M1 and later). There is no build for Intel Macs.

**A Save As name typed without its extension gets it** ([#29](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/29), [#31](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/31))
- In the desktop app, a name typed in Save As without its extension is saved with it: `cabinet` is saved as `cabinet.shz`, and an export named `part` as `part.stl`. The Windows and macOS dialogs did this already; on Linux the file was saved with no extension, and the Open dialog then hid it.
- If a file of the final name is already there, the app asks first: "cabinet.shz already exists. Replace it?"
- Nobody has reported this from the Linux desktop app yet. If you use it, a comment on [#31](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/31) saying whether Save As adds `.shz` for you would help.

**Fixed**
- **An STL or OBJ exported by Sawhorse comes back at its size** ([#47](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/47), [#48](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/48)). With File > Import units on Inches, alpha.5 read Sawhorse's own STL and OBJ files 25.4 times too big, so a 48 in board sent out and back came in about 1219 in long, and the Tape measured it that way. Sawhorse's own files are now read in millimetres, as they say inside, whatever File > Import units says, and the import report says so ("Sawhorse STL: read as millimetres, as its header says (File > Import units is for other STL files)"). A part that already came in too big stays that size: import the original export again.
- **The volume follows the units** ([#46](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/46)). With the units on mm, cm or m, the badge at the top left of the view gives the volume in cubic metres (a 300 mm cube reads "0.027 m³"), where it always read cubic inches.
- **A tiny solid in inches reads "< 0.1 in³".** A solid under half a tenth of a cubic inch, a 1/4 in cube say, read "0.0 in³" in the inch units, which is also what an empty model reads.
- **A rectangle drawn square on the ground stays a square** ([#23](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/23)). In a 3D view, a rectangle dragged out to 12 x 12 in on the ground stood up as a line reading "0 x 12" the moment it became square.
- **A right-drag pans a drawing sheet** ([#42](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/42)), as it pans the 3D view; before, a sheet panned only with the middle button or Space+drag.
- **A start-up path with `..` in it opens the file** ([#52](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/52)). Started from a command line with `..\box.shz`, the desktop app opened an empty model and showed a raw error; it opens `box.shz` in the folder above now.

The full list is in the [changelog](https://sawhorse3d.com/changelog/).

## Not yet

- `.skp` export (you can open SketchUp files, not save them).
- Fillet and chamfer tools.
- Copy and paste in place.
- Text, and radius or diameter dimensions in the model.
- Weld Edges: erasing the line that split a circle leaves two arcs.
- Orbiting with two fingers on a trackpad, as SketchUp's trackpad mode does. Today a two-finger scroll zooms; use the Orbit tool or Option+drag ([#36](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/36)).
- A metric stock library and metric starting values. With millimetres set, the built-in stock is still US sheets and boards (4 x 8 sheets, 4/4 to 12/4 boards 8 ft long), and the nesting labels and totals stay in inches, board feet and 4 x 8 sheets. Stock… in the Nesting section lets you enter your own ([#26](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/26), [#58](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/58)).
- Cutting sizes: the cut list and the nesting use finished sizes, with no allowance for cutting a part long or wide and trimming it to size ([#54](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/54)).
- Joints that add to a part's length, such as a tenon or a half lap ([#25](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/25)).
- A glue-up in the cut list: a glued part is one row there with no note that it is glued; the Nesting section lists the glue-ups ([#56](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/56)).
- A narrowest glue-up strip of your own choosing: the rule above, 1 1/2 in or a quarter of the board, is fixed ([#57](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/57)).
- A back thickness of your own in the cabinet generator: the back is 6 mm in a metric document and 1/4 in otherwise ([#60](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/60)).
- Double-clicking a `.shz` on a Mac or in a Linux file manager. On both, use File > Open, drag the file onto the window, or give its path on a command line.
- Signed installers (see below; the plan is in the [code signing policy](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/SIGNING.md)).
- Render credits: not launched. The app shows nothing about them unless you set a render service of your own in Edit > Settings.

## Known limits

- **On a Mac, Quit Sawhorse in the app menu (Cmd+Q) quits without asking** about unsaved changes; the red button and File > Quit Sawhorse ask. The browser build does not ask before its tab closes: it keeps your work as you go and offers it back the next time.
- **Enter at the desktop app's questions chooses OK.** On Windows and macOS, pressing Enter at a question before discarding unsaved changes, or at "cabinet.shz already exists. Replace it?", discards the changes or replaces the file. Choose Cancel or press Escape to keep your work.
- **Two Sawhorse windows keep their unsaved work in the same place,** so after a crash only the window that changed last gets its work back. Save each window's model as a file to be safe ([LIMITS](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/LIMITS.md#the-desktop-app)).
- **Gluing adds only width.** A part longer than every board of its thickness goes in one piece on a thicker board long enough for it, when the library has one, and is otherwise listed under Parts on no stock, as is a part with its grain across.
- **A SketchUp 5 file does not open** ([#15](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/15)): "legacy .skp parse failed: back-ref to unwalked slot 20617". Open it in SketchUp 2017 or later and save it again; the new save opens, as it did for the reporter's own model. Files from SketchUp 4 and older may fail the same way ([what comes across from the rest](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/skp-compat.md)).
- **SketchUp curves on faces that are not a solid come in as plain edges.** Most hand-made furniture arrives as open faces (amber meshes), and a mesh keeps no curves; the import report counts them. So does a circle drawn on a solid's face, and a loose curve inside a group or a component.
- **A circle drawn on a solid's face is plain edges, not a curve.** It imprints or cuts the face as before, and pulling it gives walls without rims. To get a curve, draw the circle in a sketch (off the solid) and push it.
- **Zoomed out on a large circle, a click in the middle of a segment can pick a point** instead of the circle. Zoom in, or use a selection window ([#12](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/12)).
- **Moving or importing many curves is slow.** The time grows faster than their number: 200 circles took about 3 s to move and 400 about 10 s on the development machine ([#11](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/11)). Starting a Move of a big sketch selection also pauses once, about 0.2 s for 4,000 sketch edges.
- **A rectangle drawn after another can now and then land on a vertical plane** instead of the ground. It has been seen only in scripted runs, with no steps that repeat it yet. Undo and draw it again ([#9](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/9)).
- Models of a few thousand faces are quick; a 13.5k-face SketchUp office opened in about 4.5 s on the development machine. [docs/LIMITS.md](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/LIMITS.md) has the measurements and the hard limits.
- Parts sized from their bounds are exact for boards and generous for anything slanted or rounded: check them against what you mean to build.
- The installers are not code-signed yet (below).

## Unsigned installers

The installers are built in the open by GitHub Actions but are **not code-signed yet**, so the system warns before the first launch. To open them:

**Windows** (the `.exe` or the `.msi`)
1. Run the installer. Windows shows "Windows protected your PC".
2. Click **More info**.
3. Click **Run anyway**.

An antivirus may also hold an unsigned download for a short while to scan it before it lets you open it; a tester's ESET held alpha.4's for about 30 seconds and then released it.

**macOS** (the `.dmg`, Apple Silicon)
1. Open the disk image and drag Sawhorse to Applications.
2. Open Sawhorse. macOS says Apple could not verify that "Sawhorse.app" is free of malware; click **Done** (not Move to Trash).
3. Open **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, scroll to **Security**: "Sawhorse was blocked to protect your Mac". Click **Open Anyway**.
4. Confirm with your password or Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** once more. Later launches open normally.

That warning is what three people saw on Apple Silicon Macs with alpha.5, the first build signed ad hoc, and this build is signed the same way. Instead of steps 3 and 4, you can open **Terminal** after dragging Sawhorse to Applications and run

```
xattr -cr /Applications/Sawhorse.app
```

then open Sawhorse as usual. If macOS says instead that **"Sawhorse.app is damaged and can't be opened"**, as alpha.3 and alpha.4 did, the download is fine (its checksum will match): the same `xattr` command clears it, and if it still says damaged, give the app an ad-hoc signature on your Mac:

```
codesign --force --deep --sign - /Applications/Sawhorse.app
```

and open it again. If you see anything other than the four steps above, please say on [#27](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/27) which message you saw and what it took. A signed and notarised build will need none of this.

**Linux**: the `.AppImage` needs to be made executable (`chmod +x Sawhorse_*.AppImage`); the `.deb` and `.rpm` install as usual.

To check a download against the release, put it next to `SHA256SUMS.txt` and run `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` (Linux; on macOS `shasum -a 256 -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt`; on Windows compare `Get-FileHash <file>` with its line).

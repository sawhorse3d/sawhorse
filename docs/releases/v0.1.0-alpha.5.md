# Sawhorse 0.1.0-alpha.5

A pre-release: free, open source (MIT OR Apache-2.0), no account. Try it in the browser at https://sawhorse3d.com/app/ or install it below.

## What is new since 0.1.0-alpha.4

**Save works after you delete, change or explode things** ([#21](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/21), [#34](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/34), [#35](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/35))
- This was the worst bug reported against alpha.4. Once something in a model had been deleted or replaced, alpha.4 could refuse to save it: "Could not save: the file did not read back as written: the model differs", and the same answer on every save after that. The reports came after using the cabinet generator, after exploding a cabinet or a component and grouping its parts, after erasing one copy of a component, and after other deletions. Your file on disk was never changed, but the work since the last good save could only be kept as an export, which keeps the shapes and not the editable model.
- Those models now save, open as the same model, and save again. Files saved by alpha.4 or earlier open as before, and nothing needs to be done to them.
- How it works now: Save still reads its own file back and refuses one that would not open as the model in the window. What was wrong was the reader, which numbered every part 1, 2, 3 again on open, so a model with a gap left by a deletion came back numbered differently and the check saw another model. The file now keeps every part's number exactly as it was.
- If Save ever does refuse, it says what to do: "Could not save: the saved copy would not have opened as the same model, so nothing was written; File > Export keeps the shapes. Please report this." Your model, its undo and the unsaved mark stay as they were.

**macOS: the app is signed ad hoc from this build** ([#27](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/27))
- alpha.3 and alpha.4 carried no signature at all, so on Apple Silicon macOS said "Sawhorse.app is damaged and can't be opened" and offered no way past it. From this build the release workflow signs the app ad hoc before it makes the disk image, then checks the signature of the app and of the app inside the `.dmg`, and signs and rebuilds them if either does not verify.
- What it does not change: an ad-hoc signature says nothing about who built the app, and the app is still not notarised by Apple, so macOS will still ask on the first open. The four steps under Unsigned installers, below, are the way through.
- alpha.5 is the first build that carries it, and it has not been tried on a real Mac yet. If you still see "damaged", the Terminal command under Unsigned installers clears it, and a comment on [#27](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/27) saying which message you saw tells us whether the signing worked.

**Orbit without a middle button** ([#36](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/36))
- On a MacBook with only its trackpad you could pan and zoom but not orbit. Now the **Orbit tool** (O, or View > Orbit) orbits with a plain drag, about the same point under the cursor as a middle-drag, and Shift+drag pans; Esc goes back to the tool you had. The **Pan tool** (H, or View > Pan) pans with a plain drag.
- **Option+drag** (Alt+drag on Windows and Linux) orbits in any tool without leaving it, even halfway through a line or a move, and Option+Shift+drag pans. Paint keeps Option for picking up a material and Section Plane for reversing the cut, so in those two press O. The guide's [Navigating](https://sawhorse3d.com/guide/#navigating) section has a paragraph for trackpads.

**Back, Left and Bottom views** ([#30](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/30))
- On the View menu as Back view, Left view and Bottom view, each a square, parallel view framed like the others. Bottom looks up from below with the front edge at the top and red running right, as a drawing sheet's Bottom view does. The top bar keeps its five buttons.

**The desktop app asks before it throws away unsaved changes** ([#28](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/28), [#32](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/32))
- In the Windows, macOS and Linux app, File > Open and File > New replaced a model with unsaved changes at once, without the question "Discard the unsaved changes to this model?". They now ask and wait for your answer, and so does opening an empty SketchUp file over unsaved work. The browser build always asked. Closing the window does not ask yet (see Known limits).

**SketchUp circles and arcs come in as curves, and older files bring their scenes**
- The `.skp` reader is openskp 1.4.0. A cylinder, a dowel or a rounded part that comes in as a solid has its circles and arcs as rims: click one and the whole circle selects, and its centre snaps. A loose circle at the top of the model comes in as a sketch with its face, ready to pull. The import report counts them ("60 curves came in as curves (12 circles, 48 arcs)" for one of the test cupboards, which brought none in alpha.4).
- Scenes from SketchUp 2015 to 2020 files now come in with their cameras and hidden tags, and a parallel scene shows the height SketchUp shows.
- A SketchUp 2018 file that alpha.4 refused now opens, and a component placed with its scale written into the placement (some exporters do this) no longer comes in 100 times too big.

**Units where the eye lands** ([#13](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/13))
- The unit chip beside the Value Control Box opens a list of the six units, each with a sample length, instead of stepping through them one click at a time. **Edit > Units** has the same list. On an empty model the status line says "Units: fractional inches. Click to change.", and that sentence is the click. The browser build shows a one-line hint about it on your first visit.

**A cut list without the app, for scripts** ([#14](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/14), [#33](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/33))
- `sawhorse-cli cutlist Bookshelf.shz` prints the cut list of a saved file as JSON, and `sawhorse-cli nest Bookshelf.shz` its nesting, exactly what the app's panes show for that file. It runs from a checkout of the source (`cargo run -p sawhorse-cli -- cutlist Bookshelf.shz` in its `crates` folder); the installers do not include it.

**Fixed**
- **Push/Pull no longer drops to 0 partway up a drag** ([#24](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/pull/24)). Pulling a rectangle up in the iso view, the distance could read 0 and the preview vanish for a stretch, then carry on.
- **A scene brings back the grid it was saved with** ([#6](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/6)). A known limit of alpha.3 and alpha.4; going to a scene now shows or hides the grid as it was when the scene was saved or updated.
- **A typed angle that turns nothing says so** ([#10](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/10)). In Rotate, `0` or `360` says "An angle of 0 turns nothing; type another angle or press Esc." and Rotate waits for another angle.
- **Quarter turns are exact** ([#10](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/10)). A shape stood up with a typed or snapped 90 degrees faces exactly along an axis, with no trace of rounding left in it.
- **Regions of two sketches are told apart** ([#10](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/10)). Moving region 1 of one sketch with region 1 of another said "Moved region 1 and region 1"; it now names each one's sketch.
- **A SketchUp import's curve count matches the model** ([#17](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/17)). The note counts every placed copy, as the model holds them.
- **Kerf and Trim keep what you set.** A length typed in the Nesting section's Kerf or Trim box and confirmed with Enter was committed twice (two undo steps for one change), and a Redo or a plugin that changed the kerf and trim while a typed length waited could be put back by it.

The full list is in the [changelog](https://sawhorse3d.com/changelog/).

## Not yet

- `.skp` export (you can open SketchUp files, not save them).
- Fillet and chamfer tools.
- Copy and paste in place.
- Text, and radius or diameter dimensions in the model.
- Weld Edges: erasing the line that split a circle leaves two arcs.
- Orbiting with two fingers on a trackpad, as SketchUp's trackpad mode does. Today a two-finger scroll zooms; use the Orbit tool or Option+drag ([#36](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/36)).
- A metric stock library. With millimetres set, the built-in stock is still US sheets and boards (4 x 8 sheets, 4/4 to 12/4 boards 8 ft long), and the nesting labels and totals stay in inches, board feet and 4 x 8 sheets. Stock… in the Nesting section lets you enter your own ([#26](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/26)).
- On a Mac, Control+click as the right click ([#40](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/40)). Use a two-finger click, or the right button.
- In the desktop app, double-clicking a `.shz` to open it, and Close or Exit on the File menu.
- Signed installers (see below; the plan is in the [code signing policy](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/SIGNING.md)).
- Render credits: not launched. The app shows nothing about them unless you set a render service of your own in Edit > Settings.

## Known limits

- **Closing the desktop app's window does not ask about unsaved changes.** File > Open and File > New ask; the window's close button closes at once. Save first.
- **Linux: a Save As name typed without `.shz` writes a file without the extension** ([#31](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/31)), and the Open dialog then does not list it. Type the `.shz` yourself; a file already saved without it opens once you rename it to end in `.shz`. The Windows and macOS dialogs add it for you. A fix is under review and is not in this release.
- **A SketchUp 5 file does not open** ([#15](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/15)): "legacy .skp parse failed: back-ref to unwalked slot 20617". Open it in SketchUp 2017 or later and save it again; the new save opens, as it did for the reporter's own model. Files from SketchUp 4 and older may fail the same way ([what comes across from the rest](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/skp-compat.md)).
- **SketchUp curves on faces that are not a solid come in as plain edges.** Most hand-made furniture arrives as open faces (amber meshes), and a mesh keeps no curves; the import report counts them. So does a circle drawn on a solid's face, and a loose curve inside a group or a component.
- **A circle drawn on a solid's face is plain edges, not a curve.** It imprints or cuts the face as before, and pulling it gives walls without rims. To get a curve, draw the circle in a sketch (off the solid) and push it.
- **Zoomed out on a large circle, a click in the middle of a segment can pick a point** instead of the circle. Zoom in, or use a selection window ([#12](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/12)).
- **Moving or importing many curves is slow.** The time grows faster than their number: 200 circles took about 3 s to move and 400 about 10 s on the development machine ([#11](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/11)).
- **After a move that splits a shape where it lands**, for example a circle dropped across a rectangle's edge, the selection is not updated to the pieces it leaves. Click again to select what you want; the geometry itself is right ([#7](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/7)).
- **A Move drag in a big sketch stutters.** Each preview frame works out every edge of the sketch, not only the moving ones, so dragging keeps up only to about 1,500 to 2,000 sketch edges on the development machine ([#8](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/8)).
- **A rectangle drawn after another can now and then land on a vertical plane** instead of the ground. It has been seen only in scripted runs, with no steps that repeat it yet. Undo and draw it again ([#9](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/9)).
- Models of a few thousand faces are quick; a 13.5k-face SketchUp office opened in about 4.5 s on the development machine. [docs/LIMITS.md](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/blob/main/docs/LIMITS.md) has the measurements and the hard limits.
- Parts sized from their bounds are exact for boards and generous for anything slanted or rounded: check them against what you mean to build.
- The installers are not code-signed yet (below).

## Unsigned installers

The installers are built in the open by GitHub Actions but are **not code-signed yet**, so the system warns before the first launch. To open them:

**Windows** (the `.exe` or the `.msi`)
1. Run the installer. Windows shows "Windows protected your PC".
2. Click **More info**.
3. Click **Run anyway**.

An antivirus may also hold an unsigned download for a short while to scan it before it lets you open it; a tester's ESET held alpha.4's for about 30 seconds and then released it.

**macOS** (the `.dmg`, Apple Silicon)
1. Open the disk image and drag Sawhorse to Applications.
2. Open Sawhorse. macOS says it cannot verify the app; click **Done** (not Move to Trash).
3. Open **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, scroll to **Security**: "Sawhorse was blocked to protect your Mac". Click **Open Anyway**.
4. Confirm with your password or Touch ID, then click **Open Anyway** once more. Later launches open normally.

This build is the first one signed ad hoc, which is what should let macOS offer these four steps; it has not been confirmed on a real Mac yet. If macOS says instead **"Sawhorse.app is damaged and can't be opened"**, the download is fine (its checksum will match). After dragging Sawhorse to Applications, open **Terminal** and run

```
xattr -cr /Applications/Sawhorse.app
```

then open Sawhorse as usual. Each of the four people who tried it on alpha.4 found that this alone opened the app. If it still says damaged, give it an ad-hoc signature on your Mac:

```
codesign --force --deep --sign - /Applications/Sawhorse.app
```

and open it again. Either way, please say on [#27](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/27) which message you saw and what it took: that is how we find out whether this build's signing works. A signed and notarised build will need none of this.

**Linux**: the `.AppImage` needs to be made executable (`chmod +x Sawhorse_*.AppImage`); the `.deb` and `.rpm` install as usual.

To check a download against the release, put it next to `SHA256SUMS.txt` and run `sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt` (Linux; on macOS `shasum -a 256 -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt`; on Windows compare `Get-FileHash <file>` with its line).

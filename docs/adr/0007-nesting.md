# ADR-0007: Nesting and cutting diagrams

- Status: Accepted and implemented, 2026-10-12 (lane 9)
- Deciders: the maintainer (the behaviour below was decided before the lane); the lane-9 builder (how the kernel computes it)
- Source: the lane-9 brief; ADR-0006 ("true nesting and cutting diagrams" listed as not yet); the approved mock's disabled "Nesting ›" button

## Context

The cut list (ADR-0006) says what to cut; a woodworker also needs to know what to buy and how to cut it: how many sheets of ply and boards of oak, where each part sits on them, the order of the cuts, and files for a CNC router or a laser. SketchUp users get this from paid extensions on top of a paid subscription. It has to give the same answer in the browser and on the desktop, be repeatable (the same model always gives the same layout), and never hide a part that does not fit.

## Decision

**Behaviour (decided before the lane):**

- **Stock library**: sheet goods (4 x 8, 5 x 5, custom; a thickness; grain along the length or none) and boards (a nominal thickness such as 4/4 with its actual thickness, a width and a length, grain along the length). Per-material default stock. Edited in a small Stock dialog; stored in `.shz`.
- **Nesting runs in the kernel** (Rust, deterministic, hashed). Parts come from the cut list: exact (unrounded) sizes, quantities, grain, sheet or lumber. Sheets nest with a guillotine algorithm first (clean table-saw cuts), a maximal-rectangles alternative selectable. Boards nest one-dimensionally (first-fit-decreasing plus a lookahead) on their lengths, with the width and thickness matched. The kerf (default 1/8 in, user-set) is applied to every cut. A part whose grain must run along the stock's grain is never turned; it turns 90 degrees only when it or the stock has no grain. A trim allowance per sheet edge. Parts larger than any stock are reported, never dropped.
- **Output**: a Nesting section in the Cut list pane (per stock piece: a diagram, part labels, the cut sequence, efficiency and waste; totals: sheets by kind, boards by nominal size, overall yield); SVG per stock piece (dimensions and the cut order), DXF (polylines per part, a layer per stock piece) and a PDF booklet (a page per stock piece plus a summary), through lane 6/7's save pickers and PDF writer.
- **Determinism**: same input, same layout; no seeds; goldens hash the layout.

**How it is built:**

- **`crates/kernel/src/stock.rs`**: `StockDef` (name, kind, length along the grain, width, actual thickness, nominal, grain), `NestSettings` (kerf, trim, algorithm), `StockLibrary` (stocks, material -> stock defaults, settings). A built-in library (4 x 8 sheets at 3/4, 1/2 and 1/4 in; 5 x 5 Baltic birch at 3/4 and 1/2; 4 x 8 MDF without grain; 4/4 boards 13/16 in thick at 4, 6, 8 and 12 in wide; 5/4, 6/4, 8/4 and 12/4 at 6 in; all 96 in long). The model's library is `None` until it is edited and goes back to `None` when it is edited back to the built-in one, so every older document and golden keeps its hash; it is hashed (at the end of the model hash) and saved only when set. Ops: `DefineStock` (add, or replace by name: a rename carries the defaults), `DeleteStock` (its defaults go), `SetMaterialStock`, `SetNestSettings`, `ResetStockLibrary`; all validated (names 1 to 64 characters, unique without case; sizes 1 mm to 10 m, not thicker than wide; kerf 0 to 1/2 in; trim 0 to 4 in), all undo steps, all typed refusals (`StockError`).
- **Which stock** (`nesting::choose_stock`): a part whose stock text names a library stock uses it (and is reported if the thickness is wrong); else its material's default when kind and thickness fit; else automatically. Sheets: within 1/16 in of the part's thickness (a part drawn 3/4 comes from 23/32 "3/4" ply), the one it fits first, then the closest thickness, then library order. Lumber of one material is grouped by thickness class (the thinnest board at least as thick) and the group comes from one board size, the narrowest as wide as its widest part, then the longest, so narrow parts ride on the offcuts of wide ones; a part no board of its class is wide enough for is placed on its own best board. Groups are (stock, material): oak and walnut never share a board.
- **Coordinates**: x along the stock's length (its grain), y across, from a corner; the usable area is inside the trim (sheets: every edge; boards: the two ends). A trim narrower than the kerf takes a kerf.
- **Guillotine** (`guillotine`): free rectangles, each part (largest first) on the first sheet with a fitting free rectangle, best area fit there; the rectangle splits into two by a cut a kerf wide, either crosscut first or rip first. Four variants run (sorted by area or by longest side; split to keep the largest offcut, or rips first for strips) and the one with the fewest sheets wins, then the emptier last sheet, then variant order.
- **Maxrects** (`maxrects`): maximal free rectangles, best short side fit; each part and the bin are grown by the kerf, so placed parts are a kerf apart and stay inside the usable area. Two variants (area, longest side).
- **Boards** (`ffd`, `lookahead`): one orientation per part (the one that fits the board's width, along it first). First-fit-decreasing; and a lookahead that fills one board at a time with the longest part left plus the subset of the rest that wastes least (depth-first, equal lengths tried once, at most 20,000 nodes a board and 400,000 in all). The one with fewer boards wins (the lookahead on a tie). A kerf goes between parts, not after the last.
- **Cuts** (`decompose`): found from the finished layout, not recorded while placing, so both algorithms get the same treatment. Trim cuts first; then, recursively, the first straight cut across the current offcut that crosses no part: before the first part, between two (the gap at least a kerf), or after the last. On a sheet, cuts parallel to the offcut's longer side are tried first (rips along a full sheet); on a board, crosscuts first (to length, then rip to width). The near side is cut up first. Each cut records where its kerf starts, its extent and the fence setting (the near piece's width or length). A guillotine layout always comes apart this way (a property test proves it for every layout the guillotine algorithm makes); a maxrects layout that does not is flagged `guillotine: false` with the cuts found, and the outputs say a track saw, jigsaw or CNC is needed.
- **Numbers**: areas and efficiency from exact sizes; efficiency = parts area / stock area (whole stock, trim included); board feet bought = nominal thickness x width x length / 144. Only `+ - * /`, `min`, `max` and comparisons; every sort has an input-order tie break; nothing is random. `Nesting::hash` (FNV over the library, the algorithm, every placement and cut at 1 nm, the parts left out) is checked WASM against native by `npm run golden:wasm` for every golden (each now records a `nesting` hash) and the new `nesting.json` golden.
- **Not placed**: `no_stock` (no sheet within 1/16 in, or no board thick enough), `thickness` (the stock a part names is the wrong thickness), `too_large` (larger than the stock inside the trim), `grain` (it would fit only turned against the grain). Each is checked to be true by the invariants.
- **The UI**: `Doc.nest(options)` in the worker (read-only, never an undo step), asked again 200 ms after the last edit while the Nesting section is open. Kerf, trim and the algorithm are document settings (`SetNestSettings`), committed on Enter, on leaving the field or 700 ms after typing stops. Diagrams are SVG in the pane, drawn from the same geometry (`src/nesting/diagram.ts`) as the SVG file and the PDF booklet; hovering a part lights it up in the model and the model's hovered part lights up in its diagram; a click selects it. SVG, DXF and the PDF are TypeScript (`src/nesting/`), from the kernel's result, through `saveFile` and the lane-7 PDF writer.

## Consequences

- A woodworker gets a buying list and cutting diagrams from the model with nothing extra to draw; naming stock (a part's stock text, a material's default, or the library) is the only input, and the built-in library covers the common cases.
- The layouts are good, not optimal: bin packing is NP-hard, and a deterministic heuristic that a person can predict was chosen over a search that changes with time budgets. The variants and the lookahead recover most of what a search would; the efficiency figure tells the truth either way.
- Boards are one-dimensional: two narrow parts are never ripped side by side from one wide board (each takes the full width of its length). This matches how a board is broken down (crosscut, then rip to width) and keeps the cut sequence simple; side-by-side rips are a later improvement.
- Every part is accounted for: placed once, or listed with the reason. The fuzzer checks that after every op (`testing::check_nesting`), with both algorithms.
- Not yet: rips of several narrow parts from one board, offcut reuse across stock pieces, edge banding, a cost per sheet or board foot, parts with true shapes (a part nests as the box it comes out of), multiple boards of a part glued up for width.

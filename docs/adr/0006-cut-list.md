# ADR-0006: The cut list

- Status: Accepted and implemented, 2026-10-12 (lane 7)
- Deciders: the maintainer (the behaviour below was decided before the lane); the lane-7 builder (how the kernel computes it)
- Source: the lane-7 brief; ADR-0005 ("lane 7's cut list reads parts from definitions"); the approved mock's Cut list pane

## Context

A woodworker draws a piece of furniture to find out what to cut. The mock promises a Cut list pane on day one: a table of parts with quantities and finished sizes in lumber terms, board feet, sheets, CSV, PDF and labels. Lane 5 gave the model components (definitions with instances), groups, tags and hidden items; the cut list has to read those the way a person reads their model, and give the same answer in the browser and on the desktop.

## Decision

**Behaviour (decided before the lane):**

- A part is a component definition; its quantity is its number of instances, multiplied through the components it is nested in. A plain solid, or a group placed once, is a part of its own, named as the Outliner names it. Mirrored copies count in the same row, noted "handed".
- Sizes are the minimal oriented bounding box of the part's solids (exact for rectilinear parts), reported T x W x L with T <= W <= L. Non-rectangular parts (a tapered leg, a moulding) are flagged "shaped" and show the box they come out of.
- Grain runs along L by default; a per-part override (along L, along W, none) is an op (`SetGrain`), shown as an arrow in the viewport while the Cut list pane is open.
- Material is the part's own (its first solid's), with a per-part override text (`SetStock`: the species or sheet stock). A part is sheet goods when its stock is marked or reads as sheet stock (ply, MDF), or when it is 1/4 in thick or less (its exact thickness, never the rounded one); everything else is solid lumber. Board feet = T x W x L / 144 (inches) for lumber; sheets report their area and a rough count of 4 x 8 sheets (true nesting: lane 9, ADR-0007).
- Finished sizes only; rounded to 1/16 in by default, 1/32 in selectable; metric rounds to 0.5 mm. (Lane 7b: the default is "Follow units", which is exactly that; decimal-inch and ft-in units write decimal inches; a pick lasts for the session unless pinned. Rounding is display only.)
- Hidden and tag-hidden parts are listed with a "hidden" note; "Visible only" leaves them out. Locked does not matter.
- Output: the pane (the mock's table and tiles), CSV, a printable report and a PDF of it, and labels laid out for Avery 5160-style sheets, all through lane 6's save pickers. Edge banding and nesting are out of scope, and the pane says so.

**How it is built (`crates/kernel/src/cutlist.rs`):**

- **Parts from the world tree.** The tree is walked in Outliner order. A group or instance holding solids and no nested group or instance is a *part container* (its solids together are one piece: a glue-up is one part). Anything else is an assembly and is walked into; a solid directly in an assembly is a part of its own. Inside an assembly *component*, a solid or solid-only group is a member part of that definition (one piece per copy). Because lane 5 materialises nested instances in the world, the pieces found are the instances, multiplied through nesting by construction; `Model::part_counts` rolls the same count up through the definitions, independently, and the fuzzer checks both against a brute-force count of the world.
- **Boxes.** For a rectilinear part (every face normal along one of at most three perpendicular directions) the box is exact along those directions, snapped to the part's own axes when it has them. Otherwise the smallest box over candidate directions: the part's own axes, its principal axes (cyclic Jacobi), and its face normals by area (at most 48), each with the minimum-area rectangle of the 2D convex hull across it (rotating calipers over the hull's edges). Only `+ - * /` and `sqrt` are used, never `sin`/`cos`/`atan2`, so native and WASM agree bit for bit (`npm run golden:wasm` compares cut list hashes too). A part box is computed once in definition coordinates and placed by each instance's placement when that is a similarity (rotation, mirror, uniform scale); a non-uniform scale is measured again in the world, and a copy scaled to another size gets a row of its own (noted "scaled").
- **Attributes.** `PartAttrs { grain, stock, sheet }` live on definitions, groups and solids. A solid's travel in its definition's template like its material (captured, re-derived, checked by the validator), so naming a member part inside one copy names it in all. An op naming an instance means its definition. Make Component on a group carries the group's attributes to the new component. They are hashed only when set (every older golden keeps its hash) and saved in `.shz` as optional `part` objects (`docs/format.md`).
- **Numbers.** Sizes are rounded to whole steps (sixteenths, thirty-seconds, half millimetres) for display, and the rows carry the step counts, so every output writes exactly the same fractions. Rounding is display only (amended 2026-10-12, lane 7b, after the verifier found a 0.26 in panel counted as sheet goods at 1/16 in and as lumber at 0.5 mm): sheet goods by thickness (exact thickness <= 1/4 in + 1 um), board feet, areas, the sheet grouping (by exact thickness) and the sheet count all come from the exact sizes, snapped only to a 2^-20 in grid (about 24 nm) to drop floating-point noise, so the rounding or the units never change a total (in f64: the fuzzer once found a 10 km solid whose step product overflowed an i64). Rows run in Outliner order, solid lumber before sheet goods. The list is deterministic and hashed (`CutList::hash`).
- **The UI** asks the worker for the cut list after every edit while the pane is open (read-only, never an undo step). CSV, the report, the PDF writer and the label sheet are TypeScript (`src/cutlist/`): the PDF writer is our own small PDF 1.4 writer (standard Helvetica, no embedding, exact cross-reference offsets) rather than a dependency.

## Amendment (lane 15, 2026-10-12): parts that are not solids

A competitive read of real SketchUp furniture found most of it is not watertight (benches, desks and side tables arrive as open meshes), so a cut list of solids only listed nothing for them. Now a group or instance holding non-solid meshes (and no nested group or instance) is a part container like one holding solids. `meshbuild::heal_check` tries the heal the importer's notes describe, more generously (welds within 0.1 mm instead of 0.01 mm, drops isolated faces under 0.1 % of the mesh's area, turns inconsistent faces); the part is flagged `not_solid` when that still does not close, and sized either way by the same minimal oriented box (`Shape::of_parts`: the meshes add their points and face directions). Nothing in the model changes. Faces outside part containers are counted (`totals.loose_faces`), never listed; `Op::GroupLoose` groups each edge-connected piece so it becomes a part. `PartAttrs::include` (`Op::SetInclude`) drops a part from the totals, the labels and the nesting while it stays listed. Every new field is hashed only when set (a flag bit that is 0 for older lists, `loose_faces` only when non-zero), so older goldens keep their hashes except `import_mixed.json`, whose cut list now counts its loose mesh. The fuzzer imports open, closed and nearly closed boxes in groups and loose, groups loose faces and drops parts; `check_cut_list` checks the flags and the loose-face count against a brute-force reading of the world.

## Consequences

- Every existing model gets a cut list with no extra modelling: plain solids are parts, components group the repeats. Making the repeats components is what turns six sides into "Side x 2".
- A woodworker's assembly (a drawer as a component holding two Drawer side instances and a bottom) lists its parts, not the assembly; three drawers give six sides and three bottoms.
- Shaped parts get the blank they come out of, which is what a cut list is for; their true shape needs the later nesting lane.
- Board feet are finished-size board feet; rough lumber allowances, kerf, waste factors and edge banding are not modelled yet (the pane and every output say so).
- Nesting and cutting diagrams arrived in lane 9 (ADR-0007). Not yet: edge banding, waste factors, one label per piece (labels are one per part), per-instance stock (stock belongs to the definition, like a material).

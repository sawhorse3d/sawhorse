# ADR-0010: Section planes and drawing sheets

- Status: Accepted and implemented, 2026-10-12 (lane 12); amended 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-13 (lanes 12c and 12d: the cap); amended 2026-10-13 (lane 17: one rule for what a cap is, for the cut and the slice)
- Deciders: the maintainer (the behaviour below was decided before the lane); the lane-12 builders (how the kernel, the drawings and the sheets hold it)
- Source: the lane-12 brief; ADR-0001 (every change is a transactional op), ADR-0005 (the tree, the editing context and scenes), ADR-0006 (the cut list), ADR-0008 (styles, whose "section cuts" switch waited for this lane)

## Context

A woodworker's model ends on paper: an elevation with the overall sizes, a plan, a section through the shelves at a larger scale, the cut list, a title block, printed at a scale someone can put a rule on. SketchUp users get there with section planes in the model and LayOut beside it. Sawhorse has one application, runs in a browser tab and on the desktop from the same build, and promises exact geometry: a drawing on paper must be the model's own lines, a dimension must read the model's length whatever the scale, and the same document must print the same sheet everywhere.

## Decision

**Behaviour (decided before the lane):**

- **Section planes** are entities of the tree, one per context (the model, a group, a component instance): a point, a normal, a name, an active flag, display flags. The Section Plane tool places one on any face with a hover preview, flips it (Alt), locks it to an axis (arrow keys) and accepts with a click or Enter. One plane is active per context; inactive planes show while the style's "Section planes" switch is on.
- The view **clips** each context by its active plane (correct inside groups), and fills the cut **exactly** from the kernel's cap polygons with holes, outlined in the style's section colour. The style's **Section cuts**, **Section planes** and **Section fill** switches work and are saved in scenes, with the active plane per context.
- Kernel ops create, place, flip, activate and deactivate, rename, hide, delete, move and copy planes (Move, Rotate, Scale and arrays carry them); a section query returns the caps and the outline, deterministic and hashed; **Create Group from Slice**; the slice exported as SVG and DXF. `.shz` stores the planes; `.skp` import maps the planes openskp exposes.
- **Sheets**: a Model / Sheets switch and a Sheets pane. A sheet is a paper (Letter, Legal, Tabloid, A4, A3, A2, custom), an orientation, margins, units, a title block (title, project, sheet n of N, date, scale note, drawn by, revision) and items in paper units: viewports (a scene, a standard view or a camera; orthographic views at a scale from the list, architect scales included; isometric and perspective allowed and marked NTS), dimensions (horizontal, vertical, aligned, angular, radius, diameter; snapped through inference to the viewport's lines; the model's true length at any scale; attached to model points, so they follow a change of scale or scene), labels with leaders, cut-list tables, images, lines and rectangles.
- Viewports draw the kernel's **exact hidden-line vector drawing**: what the canvas shows is what prints. Exports: PDF (vector, the real paper size), SVG (paper units), DXF (paper space; dimensions as lines and text), PNG at 150 and 300 dpi, and Print (the system dialog, desktop and browser). Everything in `.shz`, deterministic, hashed.

**How it is built:**

- **The plane and the cut** (`crates/kernel/src/section.rs`). A `SectionPlane { name, origin, normal, parent, active, hidden }` lives in a slot map beside the solids; `parent` is the group or instance holding it (a plane inside an instance belongs to that copy, not to the definition, like strokes drawn there, and moves with it). **The normal points at what stays**: a plane laid on a face, facing into the solid, cuts nothing until it is moved in, which is how a person expects the tool to behave. A solid is cut by every active plane of every context around it. `Model::section_cut` intersects each visible solid's B-rep with the plane in the plane's own frame: the crossings of every face boundary give the outline; the outline's loops, nested by containment, give the caps (outer rings and holes). A vertex exactly on the plane counts as kept (the tie rule), a crossing at a vertex on the plane is that vertex for every face that meets it, and rings drop zero-width spikes, so the result never depends on floating-point luck at the boundary (the fuzzer found three such cases; the repros are in `crates/kernel/tests/repro/2026-10-12-section-*`). *Amended 2026-10-12 (lane 12c):* the cut's pieces are chained **head to tail only**. Where the plane passes a vertex at which the surface crosses it twice (a folded solid: two lobes of the section, or a face lying on the plane and a fold, touching at a corner), four or more pieces meet at that node; the chain used to take any unused piece there, in either direction, so it could run one backwards, fold the ring and drop the cap (three finds of the snapping-off long fuzz, `2026-10-12-section-pinch-*`). Now each ring follows pieces forwards, takes the leftmost way on at such a node (it traces one patch of material at a time), and is split where it passes a node twice, so every cap ring is simple, with the material on its left. The fuzzer's invariant (`testing::check_sections`) gained two rules with it: **no cap ring passes a point twice**, and **each half is watertight**: what a plane keeps, its faces clipped to the kept side plus the cap, is a closed surface, so its vector area (Newell's sum over the clipped rings' 3D corners and crossings, plus the cap's area along -normal; `Model::kept_part`) cancels: to rounding for planar faces, and to within the largest distance of a corner from its face's plane times the cut's length for faces planar only to PLANARITY (a first, stricter version of the check fired on six such faces in the long fuzz, `2026-10-13-section-closure-on-a-bent-face-*`; the halves were right). A cap that is missing, folded or turned the wrong way leaves its whole area there. *Amended 2026-10-13 (lane 12d):* **the cap is no longer chained from its pieces; it is assembled on a planar arrangement** (`crates/kernel/src/arrangement.rs`), the Sketch tier's planar-graph machinery (the same robust predicates, the same insertion rules and the same face walk, `sketch::walk_circuits`) with a tolerance of a few hundred rounding errors instead of the sketch's merge distance, so no corner of a cap moves. (1) Every piece where the plane crosses a face, in face order, is laid in the plane's frame as a segment with its **material side** (the solid on its left: the face's normal crossed with the plane's says which way); a face that lies within its own bend of the plane (planar only to PLANARITY and tilted off the plane by about as much) meets it along a line its stored normal no longer points across, so its pieces are paired along their own spread and laid **without a vote**. (2) Segments are split where they cross or touch (an end within the tolerance of another segment splits it there; the segment bends, the point stays), coincident pieces merge into one edge that keeps the signed sum of their votes (a hole's edge lying along the outline and the outline itself cancel), zero-length ones go. (3) The faces of the arrangement are walked; a face is **material** when every edge around it that has a vote says so, empty when every one says not, and otherwise (votes that disagree, or none) the solid decides: a ray from a point inside the face, cast into the side that is cut away, counts the faces it passes (`section::inside_below`; a face the tie rule keeps whole is never met, rays that graze an edge are cast again another way); the unbounded face is never material. (4) The cap is the union of the material faces: its boundary is every edge with material on one side only, walked with the material on the left and the leftmost turn at a vertex (one face at a time where two touch), split where a ring passes a vertex twice, so outer rings come out counter-clockwise, holes clockwise, every ring simple, and every ring closes (around a vertex boundary edges alternate in and out). (5) Both halves are closed by that cap, facing opposite ways, so the halves make the whole by construction. Rings start in the order of the pieces that laid them, exactly as the chain started them, so every cut that the chain got right (every golden) hashes as before. Two finds of the lane-12c verification's long fuzz (seeds 20262101 and 20262303, both with snapping off) are what this fixes: **CX1**, a box sheared a micrometre and split near the plane, where two faces lying within their bend of the plane each had a 3 cm piece between two vertices on the plane whose ends sat at the same place along the face's line, so the vertex ids ordered them and one pointed the wrong way: the head-to-tail chain could not close the ring and dropped the whole cap (the kept half 6.6% short; `2026-10-13-section-cap-lost-after-split`); and **CX2**, a frame prism cut by a plane along the top edge of its hole, where the cut-away side's top face crosses the plane in one piece straight over the hole while the hole's wall lays its edge along it the other way, meeting it at no node: the hole's ring was never joined to the outline (it lay along the outline's edge instead of inside it) and the cap came out a plain rectangle (halves 24% short, with snapping on and off; `-section-frame-prism-along-a-hole-edge`). The tie rule's "a face with every corner within a nanometre of the plane lies on it" now reads within twice the face's own bend when that is more: a face is planar only to PLANARITY, so where it lies relative to a plane closer than that cannot be told, and classified corner by corner such a face can clip to a polygon no cap closes (the property tests found a box whose top corner was moved 0.3 micrometres up and in: the dented, bent top face clipped to a triangle over its dent; the chain failed it the same way). The watertight check also allows a face the tie rule lays on the plane (every corner within a nanometre of it) its tilt: such a face is kept or cut whole while the cap lies exactly in the plane, so the two differ by up to that tilt times its area, at most a nanometre times the cap's length (the property tests found a box face tilted 1.5e-8 rad, 1.2e-9 m2 apart). Other active planes clip the caps they reach. Hashed only when a plane exists or a scene stores section state, so every older document keeps its hash.
- **Ops** (`crates/kernel/src/model.rs`): `add_section` (active by default, which deactivates the others of its context), `place_section`, `flip_section`, `set_section_active`, `rename_section`, `set_section_hidden`, `delete_section`, `move_sections` and `copy_sections` (batched with `transform` when a selection holds solids and planes, so a move is one undo step), `slice_to_group` (the caps drawn into a sketch on the plane in a new group, closed loops becoming faces; lane 17: every ring of every cap the cut shows, welded, below). Scenes gain `sections: { active: [ids], hidden: [ids] }`; going to a scene restores exactly those flags.
- **The view** (`src/viewport/engine.ts`, `src/lib/sections.ts`): each part's triangles are grouped by the set of active planes that reach them, and each set gets a copy of the material with those three.js clipping planes (local clipping on), so a plane in a group clips only what that group holds; the caps arrive from the worker as triangulated polygons and are drawn as a filled mesh pulled toward the eye by a polygon offset, with the cut outline as fat lines in the style's colours. No stencil trick: the fill is the kernel's exact polygons, so a hole in a board is a hole in the fill. Planes draw as a framed translucent rectangle with arrows on the kept side.
- **Drawings** (`crates/export/src/drawing.rs`): exact vector hidden-line removal. Every edge (and every face triangle, which hides) is first clipped to the kept side of the active planes; the caps join the hiders and their outlines join the lines as cut lines. In view coordinates (parallel: right, up and depth; perspective: the projective map that keeps lines straight) a triangle hides at most one interval of an edge (inside its outline and behind its plane by a depth tolerance), found through a 2D BVH; the union of those intervals is hidden, the rest visible. No raster, no sampling: a box face-on is four lines. Profiles are marked for a heavier pen; hidden parts can be kept dashed. Every visible line remembers the model points it came from, which is what dimensions snap to (`crates/inference/src/flat.rs`).
- **Paper** (`crates/kernel/src/paper.rs`): `Scale` is `ratio { paper, model }`, `architect { inches_per_foot }` or `nts`; a model length times the factor is its paper length (30 in at 1:8 is 3 3/4 in; at 1/4" = 1'-0", 5/8 in). A dimension stores **model points** and its kind; `measure` reads them in the viewport's view plane, so its number is the same at every scale and after a scene changes; `format_length` writes shop fractions, decimal inches, feet and inches or millimetres. Only `+ - * /`, `sqrt` and `atan2`, compared at 1 nm in the hash: native and WASM agree.
- **Sheets** (`crates/kernel/src/sheets.rs`): plain data in an ordered slot map, changed only by ops (`add_sheet`, `set_sheet`, `delete_sheet`, `move_sheet`, `add_sheet_item`, `set_sheet_item`, `move_sheet_items`, `delete_sheet_items`, `order_sheet_item`), validated on every commit (paper 50 mm to 2 m, margins that leave room, fields at most 500 characters, items inside reach, dimensions on a viewport of the same sheet). A scene renamed carries its viewports along; a scene deleted leaves its viewports on its camera; a viewport deleted takes its dimensions with it. Hashed only when a sheet exists.
- **One picture for every output** (`crates/export/src/sheet.rs`): `render_sheet` lays a sheet out as primitives in paper metres (lines with pens and dashes, filled rings, rectangles, text with anchors and widths, images): viewports centred on their drawing or on the point they keep (Alt+drag slides it), clipped to their rectangle, captioned; dimensions computed from their points every time; labels, tables (`crates/export/src/table.rs`, the cut list's own words and fractions), the margin frame and the title block (bottom right). The sheet canvas (SVG in the page), the PDF (`src/sheets/exports.ts` over the lane-7 PDF writer, vector, `/MediaBox` at the paper size, Helvetica), the SVG (in paper inches or millimetres), the DXF (`crates/export/src/sheet_dxf.rs`: LINE, LWPOLYLINE, TEXT on one layer per kind; dimension values as text, never DIMENSION entities) the PNG (the same drawing rasterised on a canvas at 150 or 300 dpi, the resolution written in a `pHYs` chunk) and Print (the same SVG, a page per sheet, `@page` at the paper size, through the system print dialog) all draw that one list, so they agree by construction; it is hashed at 1 nm.
- **The UI** (`src/sheets/`): `SheetsWorkspace` (state, tools, ops), `SheetCanvas` (the paper, zoom and pan, select / move / resize / slide, the placement tools with the kernel's snap points), `SheetsPane` (the sheet list, the tool's options, the selected item's properties, paper, title block, print and export), `SheetTabs` with the Model / Sheets switch. The canvas never edits anything itself: each gesture is one op, one undo step. A pane edit is sent as the whole setup or item; edits still on their way are merged so two fields committed in quick succession keep each other (`useInFlight`).
- **Import**: openskp exposes each section plane as `a, b, c, d` with a name and a hidden flag, but not which plane was active. Planes come in at their place in their group or component (the kept side opposite `(a, b, c)`), inactive, with a note; top-level ones are `add_section` ops batched after the import, one undo step.

**What a cap is (amended 2026-10-13, lane 17).** Create Group from Slice and the cut disagreed about it, and the lane-12d verification's long property run (`PROPTEST_CASES=50000`, run 1 of 2) found both ways: the cut showed a cap the slice then refused (a vertex of a folded box poking 4 micrometres through the plane: one five-point ring of 4.8e-12 m2, every edge shorter than POINT_MERGE, which the slice skipped edge by edge, leaving nothing; `tests/repro/2026-10-13-section-slice-refused-a-speck-of-cap.json`), and the cut showed a cap the slice drew open (a 0.0221 m2 hexagon whose far tip had three points within about ten micrometres: the slice skipped the two short edges and the ends left were 11 micrometres apart, more than the sketch merges, so the loop never closed while the app said the faces were ready to push; `-section-slice-left-open-at-a-tip.json`). Both were the same on the kernel before lane 12d. Now there is one rule, `section::cap_ring_counts`, which `Model::section_cut` shows by and `Model::slice_to_group` draws by. A ring of the cut (an outer ring, or a hole) is a cap's ring only when (a) not every one of its edges is POINT_MERGE (10 micrometres) or shorter, (b) **welded** it keeps three corners or more (`section::weld_ring`: walking round, a point within POINT_MERGE of the last one kept is dropped, and the last when within POINT_MERGE of the first, so every edge left is longer than the distance at which the sketch merges points), and (c) its area, welded and as cut, is more than `AREA_EPS` (POINT_MERGE squared, 1e-10 m2) per metre of the solid's size (`cap_area_floor`; the size is the largest coordinate the solid reaches, at least 1 m, the same scale the arrangement's tolerance uses). It was `AREA_EPS * 1e-6` for the whole cap, a floor no rounding speck stayed under. A hole that fails is left out of its cap; a cap whose outer ring fails, or whose area with its holes is not over the floor, is not shown. The cut keeps each ring as it is (not welded: a cap's corners never move, so the cross-section check stays exact), and the slice draws every ring of every cap shown, welded: three corners or more, every edge longer than the sketch merges, so each closes into a face. The slice no longer draws the outline: the outline leaves out edges along another active plane, so a cap bounded there was drawn open, and one bounded only by other planes would have been refused while it showed. **So the slice never refuses a cap the cut shows, and never draws one the cut dropped**; when the cut shows none, it says "the section plane cuts nothing here, so there is no slice to make", as before. No check was loosened: the cross-section property compares the cut with the independent cross-section within its old allowance (1e-9 m2 plus the bend allowance), which a dropped ring (at most 1e-10 m2 per metre of a solid under a metre and a half in those tests) fits inside; the slice property's allowance for the sketch's merging (POINT_MERGE times the outline's length) is unchanged and covers the welding, which moves a corner by at most POINT_MERGE. Both finds are named repros, replayed by `the_section_finds_cut_cleanly` with every property and asserted exactly by `the_lane_12d_verification_finds`.

## Consequences

- Every older document, golden and corpus file reads, hashes and renders as before: planes, scene section state, the new style switches and sheets are hashed only when present, and the `.skp` corpus has no planes.
- A sheet is exactly reproducible: the same document lays out the same primitives (hashed) natively and in WASM, so a golden (`sheet.json`, `section.json`) pins the drawing, the dimensions' text and the table's numbers.
- Dimensions can never disagree with the model: they hold no paper length, and (amended 2026-10-12, after the lane's verification found a widened cabinet still dimensioned 30") **they follow the geometry**. A point snapped on a solid's edge stores a `ModelAnchor` beside its model point: the solid and the persistent ids of the faces meeting at each corner of the edge, and how far along it the point is. Push/pull, Move, Rotate, Scale and splits keep face ids (paint already relied on it), so every layout finds the corner again where those faces meet now (`Model::anchor_point`; the stored point breaks a tie between corners with the same faces). Corners are named by faces, not vertex ids, because B-rep edits rebuild vertices and keep faces. A point on a sketch, an imported mesh or a section cut, a corner an edit took away, and every dimension saved before anchors stay at their stored points (the layout marks such a dimension `detached` when an anchor no longer resolves, and the pane says which). Anchors are hashed only when present, so older sheets keep their hashes.
- (Amended 2026-10-12, lane 12c, from the lane-12 re-verification.) **A viewport keeps its scale through an isometric view**: sending it to Isometric or a perspective camera makes it NTS and records the scale it had in an optional `ortho_scale` (a scene made perspective does the same in the kernel); going back to a square view gives that scale back. Hashed only when present. **A dimension in a view that is not to scale reads the model**, never a foreshortened length: aligned the true distance, an angle the true angle, a radius or diameter the true circle (`paper::measure_true`); horizontal and vertical have no model direction there and read "NTS". **The sheet picks what is drawn under the cursor**: a dimension, a label, a line or a rectangle's outline takes a click only on its lines, text boxes and arrowheads (within 4 px), a viewport, a table or an image anywhere in its frame, the topmost drawn first; it used to pick the smallest bounding box, so a dimension's box stole clicks inside a viewport (`src/sheets/render.ts`, `hitItem`). A pane edit that reads a field it changes (a table's columns) reads it from the edit still on its way, so two quick ticks keep each other.
- Hidden-line removal is exact but quadratic in the worst case; the 2D BVH keeps the bookshelf and the goldens quick. The sheet is laid out again after every edit; very large models on a sheet will want the drawing cached per viewport.
- Not yet: section planes from `.skp` knowing which was active; a viewport clipped to a polygon; leaders with several segments; dimension styles beyond arrows, ticks and dots; text fonts other than Helvetica; hatch patterns in section fills (solid fill only); a DXF of several sheets; printing on the desktop goes through the webview's print dialog, not a native print path.
- Clean room: SketchUp's section planes and LayOut's viewports, dimensions and title blocks were described from memory as a user knows them; no SketchUp or LayOut source, documentation or file was read. See `docs/CLEANROOM.md`.

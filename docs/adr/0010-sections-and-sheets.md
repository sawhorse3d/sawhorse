# ADR-0010: Section planes and drawing sheets

- Status: Accepted and implemented, 2026-10-12 (lane 12); amended 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-13 (lanes 12c and 12d: the cap); amended 2026-10-13 (lane 17: one rule for what a cap is, for the cut and the slice); amended 2026-10-13 (lane 18: the cut keeps every cap; one rule for what the slice can draw); amended 2026-10-13 (lane 19: the tie rule decided once per surface; a weak face cut along its own triangles); amended 2026-10-14 (lane 19b: every face cut by the distances its corners carry; the parts of a re-planed face one surface)
- Deciders: the maintainer (the behaviour below was decided before the lane); the lane-12 builders (how the kernel, the drawings and the sheets hold it)
- Source: the lane-12 brief; ADR-0001 (every change is a transactional op), ADR-0005 (the tree, the editing context and scenes), ADR-0006 (the cut list), ADR-0008 (styles, whose "section cuts" switch waited for this lane)

## Context

A woodworker's model ends on paper: an elevation with the overall sizes, a plan, a section through the shelves at a larger scale, the cut list, a title block, printed at a scale someone can put a rule on. SketchUp users get there with section planes in the model and LayOut beside it. Sawhorse has one application, runs in a browser tab and on the desktop from the same build, and promises exact geometry: a drawing on paper must be the model's own lines, a dimension must read the model's length whatever the scale, and the same document must print the same sheet everywhere.

## Decision

**Behaviour (decided before the lane):**

- **Section planes** are entities of the tree, one per context (the model, a group, a component instance): a point, a normal, a name, an active flag, display flags. The Section Plane tool places one on any face with a hover preview, flips it (Alt), locks it to an axis (arrow keys) and accepts with a click or Enter. One plane is active per context; inactive planes show while the style's "Section planes" switch is on.
- The view **clips** each context by its active plane (correct inside groups), and fills the cut **exactly** from the kernel's cap polygons with holes, outlined in the style's section colour. The style's **Section cuts**, **Section planes** and **Section fill** switches work and are saved in scenes, with the active plane per context.
- Kernel ops create, place, flip, activate and deactivate, rename, hide, delete, move and copy planes (Move, Rotate, Scale and arrays carry them); a section query returns the caps and the outline, deterministic and hashed; **Create Group from Slice**; the slice exported as SVG and DXF. `.shz` stores the planes; `.skp` import maps the planes openskp exposes.
- **Sheets**: a Model / Sheets switch and a Sheets pane. A sheet is a paper (Letter, Legal, Tabloid, A4, A3, A2, custom), an orientation, margins, units, a title block (title, project, sheet n of N, date, scale note, drawn by, revision) and items in paper units: viewports (a scene, a standard view or a camera; orthographic views at a scale from the list, architect scales included; isometric and perspective allowed and marked NTS), dimensions (horizontal, vertical, aligned, angular, radius, diameter; snapped through inference to the viewport's lines; the model's true length at any scale; attached to model points, so they follow a change of scale or scene), labels with leaders, cut-list tables, images, lines and rectangles.
- Viewports draw the kernel's **exact hidden-line vector drawing**: what the canvas shows is what prints. Exports: PDF (vector, the real paper size), SVG (paper units), DXF (paper space; dimensions as lines and text), PNG at 150 and 300 dpi, and Print (the system dialog, desktop and browser). Everything in `.shz`, deterministic, hashed.

**How it is built:**

- **The plane and the cut** (`crates/kernel/src/section.rs`). A `SectionPlane { name, origin, normal, parent, active, hidden }` lives in a slot map beside the solids; `parent` is the group or instance holding it (a plane inside an instance belongs to that copy, not to the definition, like strokes drawn there, and moves with it). **The normal points at what stays**: a plane laid on a face, facing into the solid, cuts nothing until it is moved in, which is how a person expects the tool to behave. A solid is cut by every active plane of every context around it. `Model::section_cut` intersects each visible solid's B-rep with the plane in the plane's own frame: the crossings of every face boundary give the outline; the outline's loops, nested by containment, give the caps (outer rings and holes). A vertex exactly on the plane counts as kept (the tie rule), a crossing at a vertex on the plane is that vertex for every face that meets it, and rings drop zero-width spikes, so the result never depends on floating-point luck at the boundary (the fuzzer found three such cases; the repros are in `crates/kernel/tests/repro/2026-10-12-section-*`). *Amended 2026-10-12 (lane 12c):* the cut's pieces are chained **head to tail only**. Where the plane passes a vertex at which the surface crosses it twice (a folded solid: two lobes of the section, or a face lying on the plane and a fold, touching at a corner), four or more pieces meet at that node; the chain used to take any unused piece there, in either direction, so it could run one backwards, fold the ring and drop the cap (three finds of the snapping-off long fuzz, `2026-10-12-section-pinch-*`). Now each ring follows pieces forwards, takes the leftmost way on at such a node (it traces one patch of material at a time), and is split where it passes a node twice, so every cap ring is simple, with the material on its left. The fuzzer's invariant (`testing::check_sections`) gained two rules with it: **no cap ring passes a point twice**, and **each half is watertight**: what a plane keeps, its faces clipped to the kept side plus the cap, is a closed surface, so its vector area (Newell's sum over the clipped rings' 3D corners and crossings, plus the cap's area along -normal; `Model::kept_part`) cancels: to rounding for planar faces, and to within the largest distance of a corner from its face's plane times the cut's length for faces planar only to PLANARITY (a first, stricter version of the check fired on six such faces in the long fuzz, `2026-10-13-section-closure-on-a-bent-face-*`; the halves were right). A cap that is missing, folded or turned the wrong way leaves its whole area there. *Amended 2026-10-13 (lane 12d):* **the cap is no longer chained from its pieces; it is assembled on a planar arrangement** (`crates/kernel/src/arrangement.rs`), the Sketch tier's planar-graph machinery (the same robust predicates, the same insertion rules and the same face walk, `sketch::walk_circuits`) with a tolerance of a few hundred rounding errors instead of the sketch's merge distance, so no corner of a cap moves. (1) Every piece where the plane crosses a face, in face order, is laid in the plane's frame as a segment with its **material side** (the solid on its left: the face's normal crossed with the plane's says which way); a face that lies within its own bend of the plane (planar only to PLANARITY and tilted off the plane by about as much) meets it along a line its stored normal no longer points across, so its pieces are paired along their own spread and laid **without a vote**. (2) Segments are split where they cross or touch (an end within the tolerance of another segment splits it there; the segment bends, the point stays), coincident pieces merge into one edge that keeps the signed sum of their votes (a hole's edge lying along the outline and the outline itself cancel), zero-length ones go. (3) The faces of the arrangement are walked; a face is **material** when every edge around it that has a vote says so, empty when every one says not, and otherwise (votes that disagree, or none) the solid decides: a ray from a point inside the face, cast into the side that is cut away, counts the faces it passes (`section::inside_below`; a face the tie rule keeps whole is never met, rays that graze an edge are cast again another way); the unbounded face is never material. (4) The cap is the union of the material faces: its boundary is every edge with material on one side only, walked with the material on the left and the leftmost turn at a vertex (one face at a time where two touch), split where a ring passes a vertex twice, so outer rings come out counter-clockwise, holes clockwise, every ring simple, and every ring closes (around a vertex boundary edges alternate in and out). (5) Both halves are closed by that cap, facing opposite ways, so the halves make the whole by construction. Rings start in the order of the pieces that laid them, exactly as the chain started them, so every cut that the chain got right (every golden) hashes as before. Two finds of the lane-12c verification's long fuzz (seeds 20262101 and 20262303, both with snapping off) are what this fixes: **CX1**, a box sheared a micrometre and split near the plane, where two faces lying within their bend of the plane each had a 3 cm piece between two vertices on the plane whose ends sat at the same place along the face's line, so the vertex ids ordered them and one pointed the wrong way: the head-to-tail chain could not close the ring and dropped the whole cap (the kept half 6.6% short; `2026-10-13-section-cap-lost-after-split`); and **CX2**, a frame prism cut by a plane along the top edge of its hole, where the cut-away side's top face crosses the plane in one piece straight over the hole while the hole's wall lays its edge along it the other way, meeting it at no node: the hole's ring was never joined to the outline (it lay along the outline's edge instead of inside it) and the cap came out a plain rectangle (halves 24% short, with snapping on and off; `-section-frame-prism-along-a-hole-edge`). The tie rule's "a face with every corner within a nanometre of the plane lies on it" now reads within twice the face's own bend when that is more: a face is planar only to PLANARITY, so where it lies relative to a plane closer than that cannot be told, and classified corner by corner such a face can clip to a polygon no cap closes (the property tests found a box whose top corner was moved 0.3 micrometres up and in: the dented, bent top face clipped to a triangle over its dent; the chain failed it the same way). The watertight check also allows a face the tie rule lays on the plane (every corner within a nanometre of it) its tilt: such a face is kept or cut whole while the cap lies exactly in the plane, so the two differ by up to that tilt times its area, at most a nanometre times the cap's length (the property tests found a box face tilted 1.5e-8 rad, 1.2e-9 m2 apart). Other active planes clip the caps they reach. Hashed only when a plane exists or a scene stores section state, so every older document keeps its hash. *(Lane 19, below: the tie rule is decided once per surface, and a face within its bend of the plane, a weak face, is cut along its own triangles with a vote; the 16-times-its-bend test and the pairing along the crossings' spread are gone.)*
- **Ops** (`crates/kernel/src/model.rs`): `add_section` (active by default, which deactivates the others of its context), `place_section`, `flip_section`, `set_section_active`, `rename_section`, `set_section_hidden`, `delete_section`, `move_sections` and `copy_sections` (batched with `transform` when a selection holds solids and planes, so a move is one undo step), `slice_to_group` (the caps drawn into a sketch on the plane in a new group, closed loops becoming faces; lane 18: every representable cap, its rings merged by the one rule, below). Scenes gain `sections: { active: [ids], hidden: [ids] }`; going to a scene restores exactly those flags.
- **The view** (`src/viewport/engine.ts`, `src/lib/sections.ts`): each part's triangles are grouped by the set of active planes that reach them, and each set gets a copy of the material with those three.js clipping planes (local clipping on), so a plane in a group clips only what that group holds; the caps arrive from the worker as triangulated polygons and are drawn as a filled mesh pulled toward the eye by a polygon offset, with the cut outline as fat lines in the style's colours. No stencil trick: the fill is the kernel's exact polygons, so a hole in a board is a hole in the fill. Planes draw as a framed translucent rectangle with arrows on the kept side.
- **Drawings** (`crates/export/src/drawing.rs`): exact vector hidden-line removal. Every edge (and every face triangle, which hides) is first clipped to the kept side of the active planes; the caps join the hiders and their outlines join the lines as cut lines. In view coordinates (parallel: right, up and depth; perspective: the projective map that keeps lines straight) a triangle hides at most one interval of an edge (inside its outline and behind its plane by a depth tolerance), found through a 2D BVH; the union of those intervals is hidden, the rest visible. No raster, no sampling: a box face-on is four lines. Profiles are marked for a heavier pen; hidden parts can be kept dashed. Every visible line remembers the model points it came from, which is what dimensions snap to (`crates/inference/src/flat.rs`).
- **Paper** (`crates/kernel/src/paper.rs`): `Scale` is `ratio { paper, model }`, `architect { inches_per_foot }` or `nts`; a model length times the factor is its paper length (30 in at 1:8 is 3 3/4 in; at 1/4" = 1'-0", 5/8 in). A dimension stores **model points** and its kind; `measure` reads them in the viewport's view plane, so its number is the same at every scale and after a scene changes; `format_length` writes shop fractions, decimal inches, feet and inches or millimetres. Only `+ - * /`, `sqrt` and `atan2`, compared at 1 nm in the hash: native and WASM agree.
- **Sheets** (`crates/kernel/src/sheets.rs`): plain data in an ordered slot map, changed only by ops (`add_sheet`, `set_sheet`, `delete_sheet`, `move_sheet`, `add_sheet_item`, `set_sheet_item`, `move_sheet_items`, `delete_sheet_items`, `order_sheet_item`), validated on every commit (paper 50 mm to 2 m, margins that leave room, fields at most 500 characters, items inside reach, dimensions on a viewport of the same sheet). A scene renamed carries its viewports along; a scene deleted leaves its viewports on its camera; a viewport deleted takes its dimensions with it. Hashed only when a sheet exists.
- **One picture for every output** (`crates/export/src/sheet.rs`): `render_sheet` lays a sheet out as primitives in paper metres (lines with pens and dashes, filled rings, rectangles, text with anchors and widths, images): viewports centred on their drawing or on the point they keep (Alt+drag slides it), clipped to their rectangle, captioned; dimensions computed from their points every time; labels, tables (`crates/export/src/table.rs`, the cut list's own words and fractions), the margin frame and the title block (bottom right). The sheet canvas (SVG in the page), the PDF (`src/sheets/exports.ts` over the lane-7 PDF writer, vector, `/MediaBox` at the paper size, Helvetica), the SVG (in paper inches or millimetres), the DXF (`crates/export/src/sheet_dxf.rs`: LINE, LWPOLYLINE, TEXT on one layer per kind; dimension values as text, never DIMENSION entities) the PNG (the same drawing rasterised on a canvas at 150 or 300 dpi, the resolution written in a `pHYs` chunk) and Print (the same SVG, a page per sheet, `@page` at the paper size, through the system print dialog) all draw that one list, so they agree by construction; it is hashed at 1 nm.
- **The UI** (`src/sheets/`): `SheetsWorkspace` (state, tools, ops), `SheetCanvas` (the paper, zoom and pan, select / move / resize / slide, the placement tools with the kernel's snap points), `SheetsPane` (the sheet list, the tool's options, the selected item's properties, paper, title block, print and export), `SheetTabs` with the Model / Sheets switch. The canvas never edits anything itself: each gesture is one op, one undo step. A pane edit is sent as the whole setup or item; edits still on their way are merged so two fields committed in quick succession keep each other (`useInFlight`).
- **Import**: openskp exposes each section plane as `a, b, c, d` with a name and a hidden flag, but not which plane was active. Planes come in at their place in their group or component (the kept side opposite `(a, b, c)`), inactive, with a note; top-level ones are `add_section` ops batched after the import, one undo step.

**What a cap is (amended 2026-10-13, lane 17).** Create Group from Slice and the cut disagreed about it, and the lane-12d verification's long property run (`PROPTEST_CASES=50000`, run 1 of 2) found both ways: the cut showed a cap the slice then refused (a vertex of a folded box poking 4 micrometres through the plane: one five-point ring of 4.8e-12 m2, every edge shorter than POINT_MERGE, which the slice skipped edge by edge, leaving nothing; `tests/repro/2026-10-13-section-slice-refused-a-speck-of-cap.json`), and the cut showed a cap the slice drew open (a 0.0221 m2 hexagon whose far tip had three points within about ten micrometres: the slice skipped the two short edges and the ends left were 11 micrometres apart, more than the sketch merges, so the loop never closed while the app said the faces were ready to push; `-section-slice-left-open-at-a-tip.json`). Both were the same on the kernel before lane 12d. Now there is one rule, `section::cap_ring_counts`, which `Model::section_cut` shows by and `Model::slice_to_group` draws by. A ring of the cut (an outer ring, or a hole) is a cap's ring only when (a) not every one of its edges is POINT_MERGE (10 micrometres) or shorter, (b) **welded** it keeps three corners or more (`section::weld_ring`: walking round, a point within POINT_MERGE of the last one kept is dropped, and the last when within POINT_MERGE of the first, so every edge left is longer than the distance at which the sketch merges points), and (c) its area, welded and as cut, is more than `AREA_EPS` (POINT_MERGE squared, 1e-10 m2) per metre of the solid's size (`cap_area_floor`; the size is the largest coordinate the solid reaches, at least 1 m, the same scale the arrangement's tolerance uses). It was `AREA_EPS * 1e-6` for the whole cap, a floor no rounding speck stayed under. A hole that fails is left out of its cap; a cap whose outer ring fails, or whose area with its holes is not over the floor, is not shown. The cut keeps each ring as it is (not welded: a cap's corners never move, so the cross-section check stays exact), and the slice draws every ring of every cap shown, welded: three corners or more, every edge longer than the sketch merges, so each closes into a face. The slice no longer draws the outline: the outline leaves out edges along another active plane, so a cap bounded there was drawn open, and one bounded only by other planes would have been refused while it showed. **So the slice never refuses a cap the cut shows, and never draws one the cut dropped**; when the cut shows none, it says "the section plane cuts nothing here, so there is no slice to make", as before. No check was loosened: the cross-section property compares the cut with the independent cross-section within its old allowance (1e-9 m2 plus the bend allowance), which a dropped ring (at most 1e-10 m2 per metre of a solid under a metre and a half in those tests) fits inside; the slice property's allowance for the sketch's merging (POINT_MERGE times the outline's length) is unchanged and covers the welding, which moves a corner by at most POINT_MERGE. Both finds are named repros, replayed by `the_section_finds_cut_cleanly` with every property and asserted exactly by `the_lane_12d_verification_finds`. *(Superseded by the lane-18 amendment below: the cut no longer drops rings, and the welding rule above was not enough.)*

**The cut keeps every cap; one rule for what the slice can draw (amended 2026-10-13, lane 18).** The lane-17 rule made the cut drop any ring that welded to fewer than three corners, and the lane-17 verification's long property runs proved that wrong twice and found a leftover. (1) **Real slivers were dropped**: folded boxes cut so that the cap is a sliver 0.125 to 0.5 m long and under 10 micrometres wide (5.5e-7 to 2.4e-6 m2 before shrinking, 1.6e-7 and 2.8e-7 m2 after; 500 to 2,000 times the cross-section check's allowance) welded to two corners, so the cut showed nothing while the faces clipped to the plane closed that area (proptest seeds `c7f65cce...` in `folded_solids_cut_into_halves_that_make_the_whole` and `8248c33f...` in `planes_through_one_to_four_vertices`; both deterministic, both passing on the kernel before lane 17, whose cut kept them). (2) **The weld depended on the plane's orientation**: the lane-12d verification's unshrunk seed `5daea986...` (`planes_through_one_to_four_vertices`) cuts a 0.0226 m2 cap whose tip has edges of 1.0e-5 and 1.5e-6 m. Walking round one way, `weld_ring` kept the tip corner 1.017e-5 m from the one before it (just over POINT_MERGE) and dropped the next; that corner lay 9.9 micrometres from the edge before it, so the sketch snapped it onto that edge, into the corner before, and refused the edge between them as "that shape is too small (under 0.01 mm)". Flipped, the walk dropped the other tip point and the slice worked. Welding only neighbours is not what the sketch merges: the sketch merges a point within POINT_MERGE of another point **or of an edge**.

So the two questions are now separate. **The cut keeps every cap exactly** (`Model::section_cut`): every ring the arrangement gives, clipped by the other active planes, dropping only a ring left with fewer than three points and a cap whose area is no more than 1e-16 m2 (`RING_FLOOR`, the arrangement's own rounding floor, the same one `solid_rings` and so `Model::kept_part` apply), exactly as before lane 17. The halves, the watertight check, the viewport's fill, the exports and the cross-section property all read those unwelded rings, so none of them can disagree about what is material: a sliver a few micrometres wide is material, and dropping it leaves a half open by its area. **What the slice can draw is one rule, `section::representable`** (`crates/kernel/src/section.rs`): a ring's loops after merging points closer than POINT_MERGE (`weld`, the ring alone), kept when they have three corners or more and an area above `cap_area_floor` (AREA_EPS, 1e-10 m2, per metre of the solid's size) turning the ring's own way. The merge is the sketch's, applied first (for one ring, as here; the slice also welds across rings, below): (a) two neighbours within the weld distance become their midpoint; (b) a corner so sharp that a neighbour lies within it of its other edge is a spike the sketch would fold flat, and goes; (c) a corner within it of an edge that is neither its own nor next to it pinches the ring there, which is split into the loops either side (they share that corner). The closest merge goes first, again until none is left, so the result depends on the distances alone, never on where the ring starts or which way round it runs: a plane and its flip merge a cap alike. After it every corner is more than the weld distance from every other corner and every edge it is not on, so the sketch, whose snapping and repair look only within POINT_MERGE, takes each loop exactly as merged. The weld distance is POINT_MERGE plus a part per million (`WELD`), so a corner the sketch measures at POINT_MERGE with its own rounding is merged here first: if the sketch ever refuses a merged loop, that tolerance is the bug, not the ring. Each `Cap` carries `representable` (its outer ring's loops are not empty); that flag is all the slice and every oracle read. **Create Group from Slice** draws every representable cap: each ring's merged loops (a hole that is not representable is a slit the face covers), and then all those loops welded together by the same merges across loops (a corner within the weld distance of another loop's corner moves onto it, the later loop's onto the earlier's; one within it of another loop's edge is put into that edge, so the loops share it), because the sketch merges across loops too: lane 18's own fuzz (seed 20263608, snapping off) cut two boxes a micrometre apart, each cap fine alone, and drawn one after the other the sketch merged a corner of one into the other's edge and refused what was left (`tests/repro/2026-10-13-section-slice-two-caps-a-micrometre-apart.json`). It refuses none; a plane that cuts nothing still says "the section plane cuts nothing here, so there is no slice to make", and a plane whose caps are all not representable says, in its own words, "the section cut here is thinner than 0.01 mm; nothing to slice" (`SectionError::SliceTooThin`). The lane-12d speck (4.8e-12 m2) is again shown by the cut, and the slice now gives that message for it instead of "cuts nothing here".

The oracles follow the split: the cross-section property compares **every** cap with the faces clipped to the plane, unchanged in its allowance; the slice property is now "the slice's faces cover every representable cap's outer ring" (allowance unchanged: POINT_MERGE times the outline's length covers a folded spike) and "a section with caps but none representable is refused as too thin, and only then"; the halves and closure are unchanged. `testing::check_sections`, which the fuzzer runs after every accepted op, gained the same rule: slicing a copy of the model from its root context succeeds exactly when some cap is representable, says "too thin" exactly when there are caps and none is, and "cuts nothing" exactly when there are none. No tolerance was loosened. All three seeds are in `crates/kernel/tests/section_props.proptest-regressions`, and `the_lane_17_verification_finds` asserts them shrunk and exactly, on both sides of each plane (it fails on the lane-17 kernel: the slivers are missing from the cut, and the tip is refused on one side). The runs are in the fuzz record (`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`).

**The tie rule is decided once per surface; a weak face is cut along its own triangles (amended 2026-10-13, lane 19).** The lane-18 verifier's snapping-on fuzz found seed 27182803 (about one sequence in 130,000; `tests/repro/2026-10-13-section-tie-rule-on-an-imprinted-tilted-face.json`, failing on the lane-18 kernel): an inactive plane at z = -1.75 lying on a box's bottom face, the face tilted by moving one corner a micrometre, then a rectangle drawn across its edge. `check_sections` found the cut-away half open by the whole of a 0.0313 m2 triangle, with no cap. The tie rule decided each face on its own and moved the corners of a face it laid on the plane to that face's mean distance. The drawn part (bent 1.1e-7 m off the face's stored plane, which it shares with the rest of the face) was within twice its own bend of the plane, so it was laid on it and its corners moved to their mean, 9.1e-8 m off; the rest of the face (bent 2.5e-7 m, one corner 1e-6 m off) was not, so it was cut corner by corner. Two of the moved corners were on the box's side face, which then no longer met the flipped plane along its bottom edge, so its piece of the cap was gone, while the bottom face, touching the flipped plane at its three other corners, kept the triangle between them: a face of the half with nothing to close it. Fixing the classification alone showed a second fault under it: the bottom face, cut corner by corner, had five crossings scattered over it (a face within its bend of the plane meets it along no line), and pairing them along their spread joined the wrong two, so the cap lost a third of its triangle. And the lane's own property tests found the same family twice more (below). The rules are now:

1. **One classification per surface** (`section::sides`). Faces with the same stored plane are one surface (a face split by Draw, Imprint or Split Face keeps its parent's plane in every part, so the parts can never be decided apart), and each surface is classified once, the same for both halves: **on the plane** when every corner of every face of the surface is within `max(ON_FACE, 2 x bend)` of the plane (*bend*: the farthest any of those corners sits off the surface's stored plane), and then every one of its corners is on the plane, distance exactly zero, whatever other faces it is a corner of; **parallel** (normals within 1e-12) and not on it, every corner at the surface's mean distance; otherwise every corner where it is. Nothing is moved to a mean that another face then reads as a side.
2. **A surface on the plane belongs to the half whose material is behind it, and the other half's cap is the face itself.** Zero counts as kept, so both halves keep the surface whole. In the half it belongs to, the faces around it run off the plane on the kept side and there is no cap there; in the other half they run off it on the side cut away, their pieces run along the surface's own edges between its own corners (a crossing at a corner on the plane is that corner), and the cap they enclose is the surface exactly: the surface and its cap close each other. The cap is built from those pieces only. Laying the surface's outline into the arrangement as well (which the lane's brief proposed) counts it twice wherever a face cut corner by corner lies beside it on the plane: the property tests found a 5 mm square drawn on a box's top face, which was then bent by a corner nudged 0.1 micrometre: the square stayed on the plane while the rest of the face, cut corner by corner, kept a 0.0078 m2 triangle across half of it, and with the square's outline laid as well the cap came out as the 25 mm2 square alone. Every piece is exactly an edge along which the kept half clips a face, so the pieces are the whole boundary of the kept faces and the cap closes the half edge for edge.
3. **A weak face is cut along its own triangles** (`section::weak`, `section::clip_rings`). A face is *weak* against a plane when its bend, over the sine of the angle between the two planes, is more than a nanometre (`WEAK_LINE`, LENGTH_EPS): its crossings can then sit that far off the line where its plane meets the section plane, and the sides of its corners do not split it along a line. Such a face is clipped triangle by triangle, the tessellator's triangles in its own frame (the ones `Object::tri_solid` measures the solid by), by the cut (`cut_pieces`) and by the halves (`Model::kept_part`) alike; on a triangle the corners' distances are exactly those of a plane, so each crossing triangle has one piece, from where its boundary leaves the kept side to where it comes back, and the kept part of the face is the part of its own tessellated surface on the kept side. Those pieces have the kept part of the triangle on their left, which puts the material on their left seen from the plane, so they are laid with a vote. Lane 12c's weak faces (tilted within 16 times their bend across their size) and their unvoted pairing along the crossings' spread are gone: a face bent a micrometre by two nudged corners and tilted 1.2e-5 rad off the plane was not weak by that rule, yet its crossings sat centimetres off the line, and pairing along the line joined them across a drawn rectangle (4.1e-5 m2 of cap out of place); a bottom face bent by a corner nudged 1e-7 m, with rectangles drawn on it, clipped loop by loop, ran its pieces round a 2.2e-5 m2 region the wrong way, a cap no material can be. A face parallel to the plane is clipped loop by loop the same way (its corners are on one side, or on the plane, unless one is also a corner of a surface lying on the plane), a face that is neither is cut as before (its crossings sorted along the line and paired, which every golden hashes), and a face the tessellator refuses keeps its loops.

*(Lane 19b, below: rule 3's "a face that is neither is cut as before" is gone; every face is cut by the chords of its clipped rings under the distances its corners carry, and a surface also takes in faces that meet within the merge distance of each other's planes.)*

The oracles restate the rules and nothing else changed: the independent cross-section (`tests/section_props.rs`) classifies surfaces and clips weak faces by their triangles the same way, and `testing::check_sections`, its closure allowance and every other tolerance are as they were. A new generator, `planes_on_a_nudged_face_drawn_on`, puts a plane on a face of a box, nudges up to three corners by 0.1 to 5 micrometres in any direction (autofold on and off), draws one to three rectangles and circles on the face (some across its edges, before or after the nudges), and cuts it both ways with plane snapping on and off, checking the halves, closure, simple rings, the caps against the faces clipped to the plane, and the slice. The find is replayed by `the_section_finds_cut_cleanly`, asserted by `the_lane_18_verification_find` (the cut-away half closed by a cap, both ways; it fails on the lane-18 kernel) and, for the classification, by `section::tests::the_parts_of_a_drawn_on_face_are_one_surface`; the property tests' three finds are in `section_props.proptest-regressions`. Every golden hashes as before. The runs are in the fuzz record (`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`).

**Every face is cut by the distances its corners carry; the parts of a re-planed face are one surface (amended 2026-10-14, lane 19b).** The lane-19 verifier's first 100,000-case run of `planes_on_a_nudged_face_drawn_on` found one case in about 700,000 (deterministic, plane snapping off; failing on the lane-18 and lane-19 kernels; `tests/repro/2026-10-14-section-poked-corner-facets.json`, its seed in `section_props.proptest-regressions`): a 0.125 x 0.125 x 0.5 m box with a 13-gon and a rectangle drawn on its +x face, then one corner poked 2.5 micrometres out of the face and another dented 1.4 micrometres in, with autofold, and a plane on the face facing into the box. The kept half was open by 0.0235 m2 with no cap. Autofold had left the face as nine facets with nine stored planes. The big facet was on the plane by its own bend (9.8e-7 m), so its dented corner, 1.4 micrometres off, was put on the plane; five exact triangles around the poked corner were not on it, and one of them had the dented corner. That triangle's corners now carried distances (two on the plane, the poked one 2.5 micrometres beyond) that meet the plane along the line between its two corners on it, but the cut paired its crossings along the line where its stored plane meets the section plane, nearly at right angles to that one, and directed the piece by its stored normal: backwards. Nothing closed there, and the five facets around the poked corner (0.0235 m2) were lost from the kept half. The surface rule decided the corner once; the cut then read the face by something else. The rules are now:

1. **One distance per corner, and nothing else** (`section::sides`). The distances the tie rule gives (on the plane by its surface, a parallel surface's mean, zero within ON_PLANE, or where the corner is) are the *effective distances*; the cut, the kept halves, the ray and the oracles read only those. No face is cut by its stored plane.
2. **Every face is cut by the chords its clipped rings run along** (`section::cut_pieces`, `section::clip_rings`). Each ring a face is clipped by (its loops, or a weak face's triangles) is walked in order; where an edge leaves the kept side the ring departs, where one comes back it returns (at the crossing on that edge, interpolated on the effective distances, or at the vertex on the plane), and each departure pairs with the return after it. That chord is the piece, laid with a vote: the kept part of the ring is on its left. These are exactly the chords along which `Model::kept_part` clips the face under the same distances (`kept_under`), so every other edge of a clipped ring is part of an edge of the solid that the face beside it clips at the same crossing and walks the other way; the chords of all the faces are the whole boundary of the kept faces along the plane, and the cap the arrangement encloses with them closes the half. **This holds under any distances, one per vertex**: along the normal to rounding, and across it to within the farthest a corner sits off the plane at the distance it was given, times the seam. Lane 12's sort along the stored plane's line, its pairing and its direction by the stored normal are gone; for a face whose corners carry the distances of its plane they give the same pieces, and every golden hashes as before. `section::tests::the_cut_closes_the_half_under_any_distances` replays the find's solid under the tie rule's distances, with the dented corner forced onto the plane as lane 19 forced it, and with every corner within 5 micrometres of the plane forced onto it, both ways: the half is closed along the normal within 1e-12 m2. With the lane-19 rule for the pieces put back it fails with exactly the find's 0.0235 m2.
3. **Weak counts the forcing** (`section::weak`). A face is cut along its own triangles when its bend **plus the farthest the tie rule moved one of its corners' distances**, over the sine of the angle between the face's plane and the section plane, is more than a nanometre: the distances its corners carry are then not those of one plane through it, and only on a triangle are any three distances those of a plane. A face whose corners are all kept, or all cut away, keeps its loops (its triangles clip the same).
4. **A surface also takes in the faces that meet within the merge distance** (`section::surfaces`). Besides faces with the same stored plane, two faces that meet along an edge, face the same way and have every corner within POINT_MERGE (10 micrometres) of each other's stored planes are one surface, decided once; its bend is the farthest any of its corners sits off the stored plane of its own face, and it is parallel when every face of it is. Edits re-plane parts of one face apart: autofold splits a face into facets with planes of their own, and a corner of a drawn-on face moved without autofold re-fits the plane of the part holding it while the parts a stroke split off keep the old one. The model cannot hold a fold between such faces (it merges points closer than POINT_MERGE), so the tie rule does not tell them apart either. In the find, the nine facets are one surface again, not on the plane (the poked corner is 2.5 micrometres off, more than twice the bend), every corner where it is: the cap is the 0.0170 m2 of the face that lies beyond the plane, and both halves close to 1e-17 m2.

The lane took the brief's options (1) and (2) both, because they answer different questions. Rule 2 is what makes the invariant provable: every corner has one distance, every face is cut consistently with the distances of its corners, and both halves see the same pieces, whatever the classification decides. Rule 4 makes the classification match what the model can draw. Without it, the lane's widened generator (below) found, on the interim build with rules 1 to 3 only, two cases where parts of one face on different stored planes were classified apart (`tests/repro/2026-10-14-section-plane-through-a-poked-corner-of-a-drawn-on-face.json` and `-beside-a-drawn-part.json`; `PROPTEST_RNG_SEED` 2026101411 and 2026101412, both seeds in the regressions): a box's -x face drawn on with two rectangles, one corner moved 0.06 or 0.22 micrometres without autofold, and a plane through that corner. The bent part holding the corner was on the plane by its bend, and the parts beside it, on the old plane 0.06 or 0.22 micrometres off, were parallel and not, so the corners they share were on the plane for one surface and at the other's mean for the other. The first left the kept half open by the bent part's whole area (0.0053 m2: the cap enclosed it while the half also kept it); the second folded a side face's triangles around a shared corner into a 7.8e-8 m2 seam the cap cannot close (over the bend allowance, which counts the cap's own length, 0.24 m, while the folded seam ran 0.55 m). The lane-19 kernel passes both (its sorted pairing does not fold them); they were the lane's own regressions, and both pass with rule 4 (`the_lane_19b_property_finds`). Corners are not moved onto the plane (the cap's corners stay where the solid's are), so across the normal a half still closes only to the bend allowance; `testing::check_sections` and every tolerance are unchanged.

The oracles restate the rules: the independent cross-section in `tests/section_props.rs` groups surfaces by key and by meeting within 1e-5 of each other's planes, and counts the forcing in its weak test. **The generator was widened** (`planes_on_a_nudged_face_drawn_on`): an optional fourth nudged corner and fourth stroke, and half the planes through a nudged corner (where it is after the nudges: along the face, either way, or turned to a direction of its own) instead of on the face. They are drawn after the lane-19 fields, so the verifier's seed still draws its box, nudges and strokes (with whatever the new fields add; the find itself is the named repro). Nudges stay 0.1 to 5 micrometres in any direction, autofold on and off, on all six faces. The finds are replayed by `the_section_finds_cut_cleanly` and asserted by `the_lane_19_verification_find` and `the_lane_19b_property_finds`. The runs are in the fuzz record (`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`).

## Consequences

- Every older document, golden and corpus file reads, hashes and renders as before: planes, scene section state, the new style switches and sheets are hashed only when present, and the `.skp` corpus has no planes.
- A sheet is exactly reproducible: the same document lays out the same primitives (hashed) natively and in WASM, so a golden (`sheet.json`, `section.json`) pins the drawing, the dimensions' text and the table's numbers.
- Dimensions can never disagree with the model: they hold no paper length, and (amended 2026-10-12, after the lane's verification found a widened cabinet still dimensioned 30") **they follow the geometry**. A point snapped on a solid's edge stores a `ModelAnchor` beside its model point: the solid and the persistent ids of the faces meeting at each corner of the edge, and how far along it the point is. Push/pull, Move, Rotate, Scale and splits keep face ids (paint already relied on it), so every layout finds the corner again where those faces meet now (`Model::anchor_point`; the stored point breaks a tie between corners with the same faces). Corners are named by faces, not vertex ids, because B-rep edits rebuild vertices and keep faces. A point on a sketch, an imported mesh or a section cut, a corner an edit took away, and every dimension saved before anchors stay at their stored points (the layout marks such a dimension `detached` when an anchor no longer resolves, and the pane says which). Anchors are hashed only when present, so older sheets keep their hashes.
- (Amended 2026-10-12, lane 12c, from the lane-12 re-verification.) **A viewport keeps its scale through an isometric view**: sending it to Isometric or a perspective camera makes it NTS and records the scale it had in an optional `ortho_scale` (a scene made perspective does the same in the kernel); going back to a square view gives that scale back. Hashed only when present. **A dimension in a view that is not to scale reads the model**, never a foreshortened length: aligned the true distance, an angle the true angle, a radius or diameter the true circle (`paper::measure_true`); horizontal and vertical have no model direction there and read "NTS". **The sheet picks what is drawn under the cursor**: a dimension, a label, a line or a rectangle's outline takes a click only on its lines, text boxes and arrowheads (within 4 px), a viewport, a table or an image anywhere in its frame, the topmost drawn first; it used to pick the smallest bounding box, so a dimension's box stole clicks inside a viewport (`src/sheets/render.ts`, `hitItem`). A pane edit that reads a field it changes (a table's columns) reads it from the edit still on its way, so two quick ticks keep each other.
- Hidden-line removal is exact but quadratic in the worst case; the 2D BVH keeps the bookshelf and the goldens quick. The sheet is laid out again after every edit; very large models on a sheet will want the drawing cached per viewport.
- Not yet: section planes from `.skp` knowing which was active; a viewport clipped to a polygon; leaders with several segments; dimension styles beyond arrows, ticks and dots; text fonts other than Helvetica; hatch patterns in section fills (solid fill only); a DXF of several sheets; printing on the desktop goes through the webview's print dialog, not a native print path.
- Clean room: SketchUp's section planes and LayOut's viewports, dimensions and title blocks were described from memory as a user knows them; no SketchUp or LayOut source, documentation or file was read. See `docs/CLEANROOM.md`.

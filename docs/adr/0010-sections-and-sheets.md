# ADR-0010: Section planes and drawing sheets

- Status: Accepted and implemented, 2026-10-12 (lane 12). The cut was revised six times as hostile fuzzing found faces near the plane it got wrong (lanes 12c, 12d, 17, 18, 19, 19b) and was **rebuilt on 2026-10-14 (lane 19c)**: one sign per vertex, the solid's own triangles, a cap that closes the half by construction. This record describes the design as it now is; "History of the cut" below keeps what each revision did and what found it wrong.
- Deciders: the maintainer (the behaviour below was decided before the lane); the lane-12 builders (how the kernel, the drawings and the sheets hold it)
- Source: the lane-12 brief; ADR-0001 (every change is a transactional op), ADR-0005 (the tree, the editing context and scenes), ADR-0006 (the cut list), ADR-0008 (styles, whose "section cuts" switch waited for this lane)

## Context

A woodworker's model ends on paper: an elevation with the overall sizes, a plan, a section through the shelves at a larger scale, the cut list, a title block, printed at a scale someone can put a rule on. SketchUp users get there with section planes in the model and LayOut beside it. Sawhorse has one application, runs in a browser tab and on the desktop from the same build, and promises exact geometry: a drawing on paper must be the model's own lines, a dimension must read the model's length whatever the scale, and the same document must print the same sheet everywhere.

## Decision

**Behaviour (decided before the lane):**

- **Section planes** are entities of the tree, one per context (the model, a group, a component instance): a point, a normal, a name, an active flag, display flags. The Section Plane tool places one on any face with a hover preview, flips it (Alt), locks it to an axis (arrow keys) and accepts with a click or Enter. One plane is active per context; inactive planes show while the style's "Section planes" switch is on.
- The view **clips** each context by its active plane (correct inside groups), and fills the cut **exactly** from the kernel's cap polygons with holes, outlined in the style's section colour. The style's **Section cuts**, **Section planes** and **Section fill** switches work and are saved in scenes, with the active plane per context.
- Kernel ops create, place, flip, activate and deactivate, rename, hide, delete, move and copy planes (Move, Rotate, Scale and arrays carry them); a section query returns the caps and the outline, deterministic and hashed; **Create Group from Slice**; the slice exported as SVG and DXF. `.shz` stores the planes; `.skp` import maps the planes openskp exposes.
- **Sheets**: a Model / Sheets switch and a Sheets pane. A sheet is a paper (Letter, Legal, Tabloid, A4, A3, A2, custom), an orientation, margins, units, a title block (title, project, sheet n of N, date, scale note, drawn by, revision) and items in paper units: viewports (a scene, a standard view or a camera; orthographic views at a scale from the list, architect scales included; isometric and perspective allowed and marked NTS), dimensions (horizontal, vertical, aligned, angular, radius, diameter; snapped through inference to the viewport's lines; the model's true length at any scale; attached to model points, so they follow a change of scale or scene), labels with leaders, cut-list tables, images, lines and rectangles.
- Viewports draw the kernel's **exact hidden-line vector drawing**: what the canvas shows is what prints. Exports: PDF (vector, the real paper size), SVG (paper units), DXF (paper space; dimensions as lines and text), PNG at 150 and 300 dpi, and Print (the system dialog, desktop and browser). Everything in `.shz`, deterministic, hashed.

**How it is built:**

- **The plane** (`crates/kernel/src/section.rs`). A `SectionPlane { name, origin, normal, parent, active, hidden }` lives in a slot map beside the solids; `parent` is the group or instance holding it (a plane inside an instance belongs to that copy, not to the definition, like strokes drawn there, and moves with it). **The normal points at what stays**: a plane laid on a face, facing into the solid, cuts nothing until it is moved in, which is how a person expects the tool to behave. A solid is cut by every active plane of every context around it. `Model::section_cut` gives, for each visible solid a plane reaches, its caps (outer rings counter-clockwise and holes clockwise, in the plane's frame) and the outline around them, made as **The cut** below says; other active planes that reach the same solid clip its caps (ring by ring, as Sutherland and Hodgman clip, where that leaves simple rings, which it always does for a convex cap; otherwise, since lane 19c, the cap is cut again on a planar arrangement with each other plane's line laid across it without a vote, keeping the material faces every half-plane keeps: a hole the line passes through opens into a notch, a ring it crosses four times comes out in two pieces), and the outline leaves out edges lying only along another plane's cut. Deterministic, and hashed only when a plane exists or a scene stores section state, so every older document keeps its hash.
- **Ops** (`crates/kernel/src/model.rs`): `add_section` (active by default, which deactivates the others of its context), `place_section`, `flip_section`, `set_section_active`, `rename_section`, `set_section_hidden`, `delete_section`, `move_sections` and `copy_sections` (batched with `transform` when a selection holds solids and planes, so a move is one undo step), `slice_to_group` (the caps drawn into a sketch on the plane in a new group, closed loops becoming faces; lane 18: every representable cap, its rings merged by the one rule, below). Scenes gain `sections: { active: [ids], hidden: [ids] }`; going to a scene restores exactly those flags.
- **The view** (`src/viewport/engine.ts`, `src/lib/sections.ts`): each part's triangles are grouped by the set of active planes that reach them, and each set gets a copy of the material with those three.js clipping planes (local clipping on), so a plane in a group clips only what that group holds; the caps arrive from the worker as triangulated polygons and are drawn as a filled mesh pulled toward the eye by a polygon offset, with the cut outline as fat lines in the style's colours. No stencil trick: the fill is the kernel's exact polygons, so a hole in a board is a hole in the fill. Planes draw as a framed translucent rectangle with arrows on the kept side.
- **Drawings** (`crates/export/src/drawing.rs`): exact vector hidden-line removal. Every edge (and every face triangle, which hides) is first clipped to the kept side of the active planes; the caps join the hiders and their outlines join the lines as cut lines. In view coordinates (parallel: right, up and depth; perspective: the projective map that keeps lines straight) a triangle hides at most one interval of an edge (inside its outline and behind its plane by a depth tolerance), found through a 2D BVH; the union of those intervals is hidden, the rest visible. No raster, no sampling: a box face-on is four lines. Profiles are marked for a heavier pen; hidden parts can be kept dashed. Every visible line remembers the model points it came from, which is what dimensions snap to (`crates/inference/src/flat.rs`).
- **Paper** (`crates/kernel/src/paper.rs`): `Scale` is `ratio { paper, model }`, `architect { inches_per_foot }` or `nts`; a model length times the factor is its paper length (30 in at 1:8 is 3 3/4 in; at 1/4" = 1'-0", 5/8 in). A dimension stores **model points** and its kind; `measure` reads them in the viewport's view plane, so its number is the same at every scale and after a scene changes; `format_length` writes shop fractions, decimal inches, feet and inches or millimetres. Only `+ - * /`, `sqrt` and `atan2`, compared at 1 nm in the hash: native and WASM agree.
- **Sheets** (`crates/kernel/src/sheets.rs`): plain data in an ordered slot map, changed only by ops (`add_sheet`, `set_sheet`, `delete_sheet`, `move_sheet`, `add_sheet_item`, `set_sheet_item`, `move_sheet_items`, `delete_sheet_items`, `order_sheet_item`), validated on every commit (paper 50 mm to 2 m, margins that leave room, fields at most 500 characters, items inside reach, dimensions on a viewport of the same sheet). A scene renamed carries its viewports along; a scene deleted leaves its viewports on its camera; a viewport deleted takes its dimensions with it. Hashed only when a sheet exists.
- **One picture for every output** (`crates/export/src/sheet.rs`): `render_sheet` lays a sheet out as primitives in paper metres (lines with pens and dashes, filled rings, rectangles, text with anchors and widths, images): viewports centred on their drawing or on the point they keep (Alt+drag slides it), clipped to their rectangle, captioned; dimensions computed from their points every time; labels, tables (`crates/export/src/table.rs`, the cut list's own words and fractions), the margin frame and the title block (bottom right). The sheet canvas (SVG in the page), the PDF (`src/sheets/exports.ts` over the lane-7 PDF writer, vector, `/MediaBox` at the paper size, Helvetica), the SVG (in paper inches or millimetres), the DXF (`crates/export/src/sheet_dxf.rs`: LINE, LWPOLYLINE, TEXT on one layer per kind; dimension values as text, never DIMENSION entities) the PNG (the same drawing rasterised on a canvas at 150 or 300 dpi, the resolution written in a `pHYs` chunk) and Print (the same SVG, a page per sheet, `@page` at the paper size, through the system print dialog) all draw that one list, so they agree by construction; it is hashed at 1 nm.
- **The UI** (`src/sheets/`): `SheetsWorkspace` (state, tools, ops), `SheetCanvas` (the paper, zoom and pan, select / move / resize / slide, the placement tools with the kernel's snap points), `SheetsPane` (the sheet list, the tool's options, the selected item's properties, paper, title block, print and export), `SheetTabs` with the Model / Sheets switch. The canvas never edits anything itself: each gesture is one op, one undo step. A pane edit is sent as the whole setup or item; edits still on their way are merged so two fields committed in quick succession keep each other (`useInFlight`).
- **Import**: openskp exposes each section plane as `a, b, c, d` with a name and a hidden flag, but not which plane was active. Planes come in at their place in their group or component (the kept side opposite `(a, b, c)`), inactive, with a note; top-level ones are `add_section` ops batched after the import, one undo step.

**The cut** (`crates/kernel/src/section.rs`, `crates/kernel/src/arrangement.rs`; lane 19c). A cap must close the half it caps: what a plane keeps of a solid (its faces clipped to the kept side) and the cap are one closed surface, and the two halves of a plane add up to the solid. Six revisions decided which side of the plane a vertex near it is on by the faces, surfaces and bends around it, and cut each face along a line its stored plane gives; each fixed the case a verifier's fuzz had found, and the hostile generator found the next one (History, below). The cut is now built so that the cap closes the half **whatever the floating-point geometry**:

1. **One sign per vertex** (`section::distances`). Every vertex's signed distance from the plane is worked out once; a vertex whose distance is `ON_FACE` (1 nm, the kernel's length noise floor `LENGTH_EPS`) or less in size is on the plane, distance exactly zero, and **zero counts as kept**. That is the whole tie rule: no face, surface or bend changes a vertex's side. A plane laid on an exact face therefore cuts nothing facing into the solid and, facing out of it, has the face as its cap; a face nudged, bent or drawn on so that its corners sit more than a nanometre off the plane is cut where its corners are (a plane a few micrometres inside a face cuts a sliver that thin off it, and the cap is the face).
2. **The solid's own triangles.** A face with no corner below the plane is kept whole and one with every corner below it is cut away whole; a face the plane splits is triangulated by the kernel's tessellator in the face's own frame, the very triangles `Object::tri_solid` measures the solid by (`object::face_triangles`; a face the tessellator refuses, which a committed solid never has, is fanned from the first corner of each loop: still a 2-chain whose boundary is the face's loops). **No triangle stands off its face** (since the lane's resumed run): a face is planar only to within its tolerance, and where its corners lie nearly in a line in its own frame the tessellator can clip an ear a nanometre wide there whose corners lie tens of nanometres apart across the face; that triangle stands across its face and can lie folded flat over the face beyond the edge, so the solid's triangles cover a sliver of the plane twice or once the wrong way. A triangle leaning more than 60 degrees off its face has its diagonals flipped wherever the flip is legal (both new triangles counter-clockwise in the face's frame, by the robust orientation) and lays the pair down further; a face flat to rounding keeps the tessellator's triangles exactly. Each triangle is clipped by the classic table, its corners in its own turn: no corner below, the whole triangle; no corner above and one below, nothing; otherwise the polygon of its kept corners and the crossings of its edges from a corner above to one below (`section::clip_triangle`). A crossing is worked out once from its segment's two vertices (interpolated from the lower vertex id), so both triangles that share a segment, in one face or in two, have the same point; and since a plane's flip gives every vertex the same distance with its sign turned, the half it keeps and the half it cuts away have the same crossings to the bit, and the same on-plane vertices (`section::tests::both_halves_share_every_point_of_the_cut`).
3. **The chords, and why the cap closes.** The triangles of all the faces are a closed oriented surface: every edge of a triangle is an edge of exactly one other, walked the other way (a diagonal by the face's next triangle, an edge of the solid by the triangle of the face across). What a triangle keeps of an edge depends on the edge's two distances alone: all of it when neither end is below the plane, the part from the end above to the crossing when one end is above and one below, nothing else of any length. So both triangles of an edge keep the same part and walk it opposite ways, and it closes, except along an edge with both ends on the plane, which a triangle keeps whole when its third corner is not below the plane and not at all when it is. **What the kept triangles leave open is exactly the chords**: the chord of every triangle the plane splits (from where its boundary leaves the kept side to where it comes back, the kept part on its left) and every edge with both ends on the plane kept by one of its two triangles only, walked as that triangle walks it. `section::tests::the_kept_triangles_leave_open_exactly_the_chords` walks the kept triangles of hostile solids (both lane-19b finds, the lane-19 and lane-18 finds, folded boxes, a box, a frame) under the tie rule's distances, with every corner within 10 nm to 5 micrometres of the plane forced onto it, and under random distances that no geometry gives, and matches every open segment to a chord, one to one, then does the same for the merged faces (below).
4. **Faces, not triangles.** The kept triangles of each face are merged back into the face's kept rings: segments its triangles walk both ways (its diagonals) close, and the chords that meet at a crossing on one of its diagonals are joined into one run, from where the run enters the face to where it leaves (a run can also go round a hole of a bent face, crossing only diagonals). A run that lies within the cap's arrangement tolerance (below) of a straight line is laid as that line (Douglas and Peucker, the ends always kept), so a planar face the plane crosses gives exactly the chord between the crossings on its own edges, the cap's corners are the crossings on the solid's edges, and the caps, the slices and the hashes see faces, not triangles: every golden hashes as it did before lane 19c. The merged rings and the chords take the same straightened runs, so they still close each other.
5. **The cap from the chords** (`crates/kernel/src/arrangement.rs`). The chords are laid in the plane's frame on a planar arrangement, the Sketch tier's planar-graph machinery (the same robust predicates and face walk) with a tolerance of a few hundred rounding errors (`2e-13` m per metre of the solid's largest coordinate) instead of the sketch's merge distance, so no corner of a cap moves by more than that. Every distinct end of a chord is made a vertex once (one within the tolerance of a vertex already there is that vertex), so two chords that share an end share its vertex; a chord is then laid between its two vertices, split where existing vertices lie on it and where it properly crosses an edge (which is split too), chords laid along one edge add their votes (opposite ones cancel), and what single insertions leave (vertices within the tolerance, a vertex on an edge, a crossing) is settled. Each of these steps keeps the chords a cycle, as they are by construction (they are the boundary of the kept surface), so **every face of the arrangement has one winding number**, worked out face to face from the unbounded face (0) across each edge (the face on an edge's left has the one on its right's number plus the votes along it). A face is material when its number is 1 or more: no ray is cast and no oracle asked (lane 12d asked a ray cast into the solid about faces whose votes disagreed; for a cycle they cannot). Only for votes that do not close, which the cut never lays, are the numbers inconsistent, and then each face goes by the majority of its own votes. The cap is the union of the material faces: its boundary is every edge with material on one side only, walked with the material on the left and the leftmost turn at a vertex (one face at a time where two touch), split where a ring passes a vertex twice, so outer rings come out counter-clockwise, holes clockwise, every ring simple and closed; rings start in the order of the chords that laid them. The rings of a solid's caps are then tidied together (a repeated point, or one in line with its neighbours, goes), keeping every point that more than one of them passes: a hole that touches its outer ring meets it at a corner of both, never with one ring's straight edge running past the other's corner a rounding error to one side.
6. **What that gives.** Where the chords wind round no point of the plane more than once or less than none (true when the solid's triangles are an embedded surface near the plane, as a committed solid's are once no triangle stands off its face; it could fail only where two sheets of the surface lie within about a nanometre of each other and of the plane, folded over one another, as the standing triangle of the resumed run's find did), the cap's boundary is the chords seen in the plane, walked the other way, so each half closes **along the normal to rounding** and **across it to within how far a chord's end sits off the plane** (a corner on the plane by the tie rule is up to ON_FACE off it) times the seam's length. No face's stored plane, bend or neighbours enter that argument. Where chords did wind twice, or round a sliver the wrong way, the cap is the region they wind round once or more, not their sum, and the half would be open by the difference; the oracles below would say so (they did, for the standing triangle).
7. **The cut keeps every cap exactly** (lane 18): every ring the arrangement gives, clipped by the other active planes, dropping only a ring left with fewer than three points or with no area at all (1e-16 m2, `RING_FLOOR`, the arrangement's rounding), and a cap whose area is no more. The halves, the viewport's fill, the exports and the cross-section all read those unwelded rings, so none of them can disagree about what is material: a sliver a few micrometres wide is material.

**What Create Group from Slice can draw** is one rule, `section::representable` (lanes 17 and 18): a ring's loops after merging points closer than POINT_MERGE as the sketch merges them (`weld`; the merge distance plus a part per million, so a corner the sketch measures at POINT_MERGE with its own rounding is merged here first): (a) two neighbours within it become their midpoint; (b) a corner so sharp that a neighbour lies within it of its other edge is a spike the sketch would fold flat, and goes; (c) a corner within it of an edge that is neither its own nor next to it pinches the ring there, which is split into the loops either side. The closest merge goes first, again until none is left, so the result depends on the distances alone, never on where the ring starts or which way round it runs: a plane and its flip merge a cap alike. A loop is kept when it has three corners or more and an area above `cap_area_floor` (AREA_EPS, 1e-10 m2, per metre of the solid's size) turning the ring's own way; each `Cap` carries `representable` (its outer ring keeps a loop), and that flag is all the slice and every oracle read. **Create Group from Slice** draws every representable cap: each ring's merged loops (a hole that is not representable is a slit the face covers), and then all of them welded together across loops by the same merges (a corner within the distance of another loop's corner moves onto it, one within it of another loop's edge goes into that edge), because the sketch merges across loops too. It refuses none: a plane that cuts nothing says "the section plane cuts nothing here, so there is no slice to make", and one whose caps are all thinner than the sketch can hold says "the section cut here is thinner than 0.01 mm; nothing to slice". The slice draws the caps, not the outline (the outline leaves out edges along other planes' cuts).

**How the cut is checked.** `testing::check_sections`, which the sequence fuzzer runs after every accepted op and every repro replays, holds every section of every model to: the cut never fails and is deterministic; every cap ring is closed, simple (no point twice) and turns the right way, holes inside their outer, and no two edges of a cap cross; the areas are the rings' sums; the outline has no more edges than the rings; Create Group from Slice succeeds exactly when some cap is representable, says "too thin" exactly when there are caps and none is, and "cuts nothing" exactly when there are none; the two halves of the plane add up to what it reaches, measured over the faces' planes; each half is closed, its vector area (every kept face ring's Newell sum over its 3D points plus the cap's area along `-normal`, `Model::kept_part`) at most `1e-9 x (1 + area) + 2 x LENGTH_EPS x` the seam's length (before lane 19c the allowance also had twice the faces' bend times the seam: faces were cut along their stored planes, so a bent face met its cap along chords up to its bend apart); and, for every solid whose caps no other active plane clips, its caps' area equals the cross-section of its own triangles worked out without the cut (`testing::cross_section`: `tri_solid`'s triangles clipped by the documented tie rule, the kept part's vector area along the normal), within `1e-9 x (1 + its surface) + 2 x LENGTH_EPS x` the caps' length. `crates/kernel/tests/section_props.rs` cuts hostile solids both ways with the same checks plus no two cap edges crossing and a slice that covers every representable cap: folded boxes (Move with autofold) at planes through their own vertices, along their faces and at random; frame prisms cut through an edge of their hole at any angle; two boxes touching at a corner; faces sheared, tilted or bent within a micrometre of the plane and sometimes split; planes through one to four vertices of a folded box; a pocket round a post with a hole of its own; planes exactly on a face; and `planes_on_a_nudged_face_drawn_on` (a plane on a box face, or through one of its corners, up to four corners nudged by 0.1 to 5 micrometres in any direction with autofold on and off, up to four rectangles and circles drawn on the face, some across its edges, before or after the nudges, cut with plane snapping on and off). Every find of every lane is a named repro (`crates/kernel/tests/repro/*-section-*`), replayed by `the_section_finds_cut_cleanly` and `tests/repro.rs` and, where a test names it, asserted exactly. The runs are in the fuzz record (`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`). `crates/kernel/tests/section_perf.rs` times the cut (ignored by default; release): timed back to back with the lane-19b kernel on the development machine, the bookshelf's two planes take 0.044 and 0.020 ms (0.033 to 0.037 and 0.025 ms before), and a 13,562-face SketchUp office fit-out (48,612 faces in 275 solids once imported) 0.3 to 1.8 ms a plane, where the lane-19b cut took 54 to 57 ms (it grouped every face of every solid into surfaces first), with the same caps and hashes on all six planes (`scripts/skp-op.mjs` writes the import op the harness reads from a `.skp`).

## History of the cut

Each revision fixed what a verifier's fuzz or the property tests had found; every find is a named repro in `crates/kernel/tests/repro/` (or a seed in `section_props.proptest-regressions`) and passes on the current cut. The runs are in the fuzz record (`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`).

- **Lane 12 (2026-10-12).** Each face's boundary crossings, sorted along the line where its stored plane meets the section plane, paired into pieces and directed by its stored normal; the pieces chained into rings, nested by containment. A vertex exactly on the plane counted as kept (within 1e-12), a crossing at a vertex on the plane was that vertex for every face, rings dropped zero-width spikes (`2026-10-12-section-cap-on-a-tilted-face`, `-through-a-reflex-vertex`, `-along-a-face-tilted-a-micrometre`).
- **Lane 12c.** Pieces chained head to tail only, the leftmost way on at a node where the surface crosses the plane twice, rings split where they pass a node twice (three snapping-off finds, `2026-10-12-section-pinch-*`); `check_sections` gained simple rings and closed halves (vector area within the faces' bend times the cut's length; `2026-10-13-section-closure-on-a-bent-face-1..6`, `-closure-under-another-plane`, `-halves-on-bent-faces`).
- **Lane 12d.** The cap assembled on a planar arrangement from the pieces with their material side, faces decided by their votes or, where they disagreed, by a ray cast into the solid; a face within its own bend of the plane laid without a vote; a face with every corner within twice its bend of the plane lay on it. Finds: a split face's piece pointing the wrong way dropped the whole cap (CX1, `2026-10-13-section-cap-lost-after-split`), a hole's edge along the outline lost the notch (CX2, `-frame-prism-along-a-hole-edge`), and the property tests' dented top face (now `a_dented_face_near_the_plane_is_cut_along_its_triangles`).
- **Lanes 17 and 18.** One rule for what a cap is, then two: the cut keeps every cap exactly, and `representable` decides what the slice draws, merged as the sketch merges, welded across loops (`2026-10-13-section-slice-refused-a-speck-of-cap`, `-slice-left-open-at-a-tip`, `-slice-two-caps-a-micrometre-apart`; seeds `c7f65cce`, `8248c33f` and `5daea986` in the regressions).
- **Lane 19.** The lane-18 verifier's find (seed 27182803, about one sequence in 130,000, `2026-10-13-section-tie-rule-on-an-imprinted-tilted-face`): faces decided one by one moved shared corners to their own mean distance, and a side face lost its piece; the cut-away half kept a 0.0313 m2 triangle with no cap. The tie rule was decided once per surface (faces sharing a stored plane), a surface on the plane exactly zero, and a weak face (bend over the sine of its angle with the plane over a nanometre) cut along its triangles. The lane's new generator, `planes_on_a_nudged_face_drawn_on`, found three more of the family (seeds in the regressions).
- **Lane 19b.** The lane-19 verifier's find (about one case in 700,000, `2026-10-14-section-poked-corner-facets`): an exact autofold facet with a corner another facet forced onto the plane was still cut along its stored plane's line and ran backwards; the kept half lost 0.0235 m2. Every face was then cut by the chords of its clipped rings under the forced distances, the forcing counted in the weak test, and faces meeting within the merge distance of each other's planes made one surface (two interim-build finds, `-plane-through-a-poked-corner-of-a-drawn-on-face`, `-beside-a-drawn-part`).
- **Lane 19c (2026-10-14).** The lane-19b verifier's find (`2026-10-14-section-top-face-poked-beyond-its-bend-snap-off` and `-snap-on`, failing with plane snapping on and off, and on the lane-19 kernel): a box top whose poked corner, 1.8 micrometres off the plane, was beyond twice the face's 0.65 micrometre bend, two n-gons larger than the face drawn on it, and a plane at the original top facing down; the cut-away half kept 0.0251 m2 of the top with no cap. Rather than a seventh rule, the cut was rebuilt as described above: one distance per vertex, the solid's own triangles, the cap from exactly the chords the kept triangles leave open, material by winding number. The surfaces, the bend rule, the parallel means, the weak faces, the stored-plane pieces and the ray oracle are gone. Every earlier find passes, and every named test's assertions hold unchanged but one: in the lane-19b property finds a plane through a corner moved 0.06 or 0.22 micrometres into a face now cuts that sliver off the face, the cap the whole face on both sides, where lane 19b had laid the face on the plane (`the_lane_19b_property_finds` asserts it). The lane-19b verifier's find has the 0.0251 m2 of the top above the plane as the cap of both halves (`the_lane_19b_verification_find`). The lane's own first round of property runs on its first commit (five fresh seeds of `planes_on_a_nudged_face_drawn_on` at 100,000 cases, the whole file at 100,000) found one case, with `PROPTEST_RNG_SEED` 2026101953 after 35,548 cases: both halves were closed, but a hole of the flipped plane's cap touched its outer ring at a crossing on that ring's straight edge, tidying dropped the corner from the outer ring only, and the hole's first edge crossed it by a rounding error. The rings of a solid's caps are now tidied together, and `check_sections` checks that no two edges of a cap cross (`2026-10-14-section-hole-touching-its-cap-on-a-straight-edge`, its seed in the regressions; the lane-19b kernel's caps cross there too). With that check the lane's 100,000-sequence fuzz with snapping on found two caps clipped by another active plane that crossed, the same way on the lane-19b kernel (the ring-by-ring clipping is lane 12's): a hole the active plane's line passes through, whose clipped edge lay along the outer ring's, and a ring the line crosses four times, which ran back along it past its own corners (`-section-hole-opened-by-another-plane`, `-section-ring-crossed-four-times-by-another-plane`); such a cap is now cut again on a planar arrangement (above). **Resumed after an outage** (2026-10-14): the shared crossings were checked directly (`both_halves_share_every_point_of_the_cut`), and the first of the resumed run's five 100,000-case runs of `planes_on_a_nudged_face_drawn_on` (`PROPTEST_RNG_SEED` 2026102001) failed after 48,791 cases: a box's end face split by a rectangle drawn across its edge, one corner pushed 1 micrometre into the box and another moved 74 nm and 0.88 micrometres along the face, no autofold, a plane on the face. The side face's tessellation had an ear 0.34 nm wide in its frame across a nearly straight run of corners, standing across the face (cosine 0.005 with it) and lying folded flat over the end face; the flipped plane's chords wound -1 round it and the cut-away half was open by 3.1e-9 m2. The kernel's one triangulation of a face (`object::face_triangles`, for `tri_solid` and the cut alike) now lays such triangles down (above; `2026-10-14-section-side-face-sliver-folded-over-the-plane`, `the_lane_19c_resumed_find`, `no_triangle_stands_off_its_face`, its seed in the regressions).

## Consequences

- Every older document, golden and corpus file reads, hashes and renders as before: planes, scene section state, the new style switches and sheets are hashed only when present, and the `.skp` corpus has no planes.
- A sheet is exactly reproducible: the same document lays out the same primitives (hashed) natively and in WASM, so a golden (`sheet.json`, `section.json`) pins the drawing, the dimensions' text and the table's numbers.
- Dimensions can never disagree with the model: they hold no paper length, and (amended 2026-10-12, after the lane's verification found a widened cabinet still dimensioned 30") **they follow the geometry**. A point snapped on a solid's edge stores a `ModelAnchor` beside its model point: the solid and the persistent ids of the faces meeting at each corner of the edge, and how far along it the point is. Push/pull, Move, Rotate, Scale and splits keep face ids (paint already relied on it), so every layout finds the corner again where those faces meet now (`Model::anchor_point`; the stored point breaks a tie between corners with the same faces). Corners are named by faces, not vertex ids, because B-rep edits rebuild vertices and keep faces. A point on a sketch, an imported mesh or a section cut, a corner an edit took away, and every dimension saved before anchors stay at their stored points (the layout marks such a dimension `detached` when an anchor no longer resolves, and the pane says which). Anchors are hashed only when present, so older sheets keep their hashes.
- (Amended 2026-10-12, lane 12c, from the lane-12 re-verification.) **A viewport keeps its scale through an isometric view**: sending it to Isometric or a perspective camera makes it NTS and records the scale it had in an optional `ortho_scale` (a scene made perspective does the same in the kernel); going back to a square view gives that scale back. Hashed only when present. **A dimension in a view that is not to scale reads the model**, never a foreshortened length: aligned the true distance, an angle the true angle, a radius or diameter the true circle (`paper::measure_true`); horizontal and vertical have no model direction there and read "NTS". **The sheet picks what is drawn under the cursor**: a dimension, a label, a line or a rectangle's outline takes a click only on its lines, text boxes and arrowheads (within 4 px), a viewport, a table or an image anywhere in its frame, the topmost drawn first; it used to pick the smallest bounding box, so a dimension's box stole clicks inside a viewport (`src/sheets/render.ts`, `hitItem`). A pane edit that reads a field it changes (a table's columns) reads it from the edit still on its way, so two quick ticks keep each other.
- Hidden-line removal is exact but quadratic in the worst case; the 2D BVH keeps the bookshelf and the goldens quick. The sheet is laid out again after every edit; very large models on a sheet will want the drawing cached per viewport.
- Not yet: section planes from `.skp` knowing which was active; a viewport clipped to a polygon; leaders with several segments; dimension styles beyond arrows, ticks and dots; text fonts other than Helvetica; hatch patterns in section fills (solid fill only); a DXF of several sheets; printing on the desktop goes through the webview's print dialog, not a native print path.
- (Lane 19c.) **A face is cut where its corners are.** A plane laid on a face whose corners an edit moved more than a nanometre off it (a nudge, a Move without autofold, a bent face) cuts the slivers that lie beyond it: they show as section fill over the face, and the slice draws them only where they are 0.01 mm or more across. The earlier revisions laid such a face on the plane, which is what each of their finds had got wrong. The cut reads each solid's vertices once and triangulates only the faces the plane splits, so it takes about a millisecond on a model of tens of thousands of faces.
- Clean room: SketchUp's section planes and LayOut's viewports, dimensions and title blocks were described from memory as a user knows them; no SketchUp or LayOut source, documentation or file was read. See `docs/CLEANROOM.md`.

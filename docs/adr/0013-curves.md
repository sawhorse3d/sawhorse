# ADR-0013: Curves

- Status: accepted (lane 23, 2026-10-19)
- Issue: [#3](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/3), "Clicking a circle, arc or polygon selects one segment or vertex, not the whole curve"
- Source: the lane-23 brief; ADR-0001 (the sketch tier), ADR-0011 (edge flags keyed by face-id pairs), ADR-0012 (moving sketch geometry)

## Context

The Circle (C; C again for Polygon) and Arc tools drew a run of independent sketch edges: a click on a circle's rim selected one of its 24 segments or one of its vertices, the Eraser took one segment, and Move on what was clicked bent the circle. In SketchUp a shape drawn by those tools is **one curve**: any segment selects the whole thing, Move, Rotate and Scale act on it whole, the Eraser removes it whole, a line drawn across it splits it into two arcs, Explode Curve turns it back into plain edges, and its centre is an inference point. The kernel had no such entity; the inference engine found arcs among edges by their shape alone.

## Decision

### The entity (crates/kernel/src/curve.rs, crates/kernel/src/sketch.rs)

A sketch keeps, beside its graph, its **curves**: each an ordered chain of vertex ids (a closed chain does not repeat its first vertex), a **kind** (circle and polygon closed, arc open, polyline either), the number of segments it was drawn with, and for a round kind the circle it lies on (`Round`: centre and radius in the sketch frame, the angle of its first point and the signed angle its points turn through, segment by segment, counter-clockwise about the plane's normal). An edge belongs to at most one curve. Ids come from a counter the sketch keeps and saves (`next_curve`), so a curve's id is the same after undo, redo, a `.shz` round trip and an in-plane move.

The graph's own rules (ADR-0001: merges, T-junctions, crossings, welds, repairs) never look at curves. After every sketch edit the curves are **settled** against the graph (`Sketch::settle_curves`):

1. A curve segment another edge split is taken into the chain: the vertices along the old segment, within twice the merge distance of it, in order (`Sketch::link`). The segment is still the curve's, as a run of pieces; the new vertex sits on a chord, not on the circle.
2. At a **junction** (a chain vertex where an edge that is not the curve's meets it) the curve **splits**: an open curve into two; a closed curve from its **second** junction on (one line ending on a circle leaves the circle whole, as in SketchUp; two make two arcs). A circle or an arc splits into arcs on the same circle (centre and radius kept, the angles read from the piece's points); a polygon or a polyline into polylines. A figure eight's lobes are closed pieces.
3. A curve that **lost** an edge or a point (erased by the plain `EraseSegment`, consumed by a push, a point merged away, an edge now claimed by an older curve it coincides with) becomes **polyline pieces** of what is left: never a chain with a hole in it, never a dangling reference. Pieces too short to be curves (a polyline of one edge) go.

The first piece keeps the curve's id; the others take new ones. A curve's point is never healed away (an erase that leaves it joining two collinear edges keeps it: the chain names it), and a repair merging a curve's point with a plain point keeps the curve's.

The **validator** (`Sketch::check`) holds: every curve's id below the counter; its chain of distinct live vertices joined by edges in order (and back round for a closed one); no edge in two curves; circles and polygons closed, arcs open, round kinds with a circle and polylines without; and every round curve's points on its circle within `CURVE_FIT` (four merge distances plus a part in 1e9 of its size, `tolerance.rs`), or on the segment joining its two neighbours (a chord point), or at an open end no further out than the radius; its start angle pointing at its first point; its points turning one way, by the stored sweep, a closed one once round (`curve::check_round`).

### Ops

- `Draw { curve: Option<CurveSpec> }`: the tools say what a stroke is (`{kind, centre}`). The kernel checks it (a circle or polygon closed with three points or more, an arc open with two or more, every point at one distance from the centre within two merge distances; else `NotACurve`, typed), draws the stroke with the insertion rules as before, then makes it a curve (`Sketch::add_curve`): its points found where the insertion put them, its radius the farthest drawn point, settled at once (a circle drawn across a line is two arcs at once). A stroke that did not land whole, or whose landed points do not fit the circle, stays plain edges, never a curve that lies. On a solid's face the spec is ignored: the stroke cuts or imprints plain edges (below).
- `EraseCurve { sketch, curve }`: every edge of the curve goes, junctions it leaves joining two straight edges heal, regions are re-detected. One undo step. Inside a `Batch` (lane 23b), a curve that was there when the batch began and that an earlier op of the same batch erased (an `EraseRegion` of the face it bounds takes its ring, and so the curve) counts as already erased and the op does nothing, as an `EraseSegment` of an edge already gone does; a curve that was never there is refused as before.
- `ExplodeCurve { sketch | object, curve }`: the curve goes, its edges stay. One undo step.
- `TransformSketch { curves }` (ADR-0012): whole curves by id; their points move and their edges are carried. A curve whose every edge is carried moves **as one**: within its plane its centre is mapped and its radius scaled when the map, projected into the plane, is a similarity (a move, a turn, a mirror, a uniform scale; `curve::similarity_scale`), and its angles are read again from its points; any other map (a non-uniform scale, a tilt projected into the plane) leaves it one curve, a polyline. Lifted off its plane it is made a curve again in the sketch it lands on (a new id); a copy is a new curve with a new id. A curve only some of whose points move is bent: it becomes a polyline. Outcomes say the (sketch, curve) pairs: `SketchMoved { curves }`, `SketchCopied { curves }`.
- `Offset`: a region's or a chain's round curves offset to curves of their kind: an offset loop with a closed source's point count, all at one distance from its centre, is a circle (or polygon) about the same centre; a run of an arc's points (or two fewer, cut to meet the lines beside it) at one distance from its centre is an arc. `Drawn { curve }` names the first. **A lone circle, arc or polygon offsets its radius** (lane 23b): when the source is exactly one round curve (a region or a solid's face bounded by it alone, or its own chain; a sketch's curve or a solid's rim), every point moves along its radius by the distance, so the result has the radius plus or minus the distance, the same centre, kind and segment count (`Model::round_offset`). The polygon offset of everything else moves the edges by the distance, which for a 24-segment circle of 200 mm offset 50 mm gave 249.57 mm (the apothem moved, not the radius); SketchUp, and a woodworker, expect 250 mm. For a polygon that means the corners move the distance (its edges a little less), and an inward offset past its apothem but short of its radius is still a polygon. Closed: a positive distance grows; an arc: positive is to the left of its way (towards the centre for a counter-clockwise arc), as for any chain; past the centre is refused (`Vanishes`).
- `Import { strokes: [{ curve }] }`: a file's stroke that is one curve (a DXF `CIRCLE` or `ARC`) comes in as a curve when its points land as drawn; the import notes count them ("3 curves came in as curves").

### The one deliberate divergence: a curve's point moved on its own is refused

SketchUp lets you move one point of a circle and bends the curve. Sawhorse refuses it, typed (`TransformError::CurvePoint`: "that point is on a curve: moving it alone would bend the curve. Select the whole curve, or use Edit > Explode Curve first"); the Move, Rotate and Scale tools refuse it before the gesture starts, with the same words (`xform::curvePointRefusal`), for a sketch's curves and for a solid's rims. A curve's points stay individually selectable (a click exactly on one selects that point: curve points are points), and moving an edge or a region that shares a point with a curve still bends the curve (it becomes a polyline, still one curve). The reason: a bent circle silently stops being a circle (its centre, radius and Centre inference are gone), and the commonest way to do it is a stray drag on a rim point while meaning to move the shape. Explode Curve is one click away for the rare deliberate case.

### Solids: rims (keyed like lane 15's edge flags)

Pushing a region into a solid (`Extrude`) gives the solid the **rims** of every curve whose every edge bounds the region: one on the base and one on the cap (`RimCurve`: kind, closed, segment count, and its edges in order, each named by the **pair of persistent face ids** it lies between: the cap, 1 or 2, and the side wall that edge raised; `Object::extrude` numbers the walls ring by ring from 3). As with ADR-0011's flags, a face-id pair follows its edge through moves, copies, mirrors, component definitions and instances, and pushes of other faces with no bookkeeping. After every edit (`Object::settle_curves`, run with `settle_edges`) a rim edge whose key no longer names exactly one edge drops out, and the rim splits there: circle and arc rims into arcs, polygon and polyline rims into polylines. A rim's circle is **read from its points** when asked for (`Object::curve_info`: three points spread along it, every point within `CURVE_FIT` of the circle and its plane), so a scaled solid's rim needs nothing, and a rim scaled out of round reads as a polyline. The validator holds that every key names one edge, the edges meet in order (round the loop for a closed rim), no edge is in two rims.

Pulling a circle or an arc also makes the walls between its consecutive segments **soft and smooth** (SketchUp: a pulled circle is a smooth cylinder). This was not lane 15's doing: the brief asked to verify that lane 15 already made them so, and it did not (lane 15's softening is by angle, on request); with curves the kernel knows which walls belong to one curve. A polygon's and a polyline's walls stay hard (a hexagonal prism has six faces). A solid pushed back flat onto its source sketch gives its base rims back to the sketch as curves.

A circle drawn **on a solid's face** imprints or cuts plain edges: its edges would share one face-id pair (the face and the imprint), so lane 15's keying cannot tell them apart, and no rim is kept (`docs/LIMITS.md`).

### Selection, the Eraser, the transform tools, inference (src/)

The view mesh names each edge segment's curve (`edge_curve`, an index into `curves`: owner, id, kind, closed, segments, centre, radius, sweep, normal); a pick of an edge carries its curve's id. The Select tool takes a curve's segment as the whole curve on hover and click (Shift adds, Ctrl toggles the whole curve; a double-click still selects the solid); a window takes a curve when all its segments are inside, a crossing box when it touches any; the status line says "Selected a circle (24 segments, radius 300 mm)", "an arc (12 segments, radius 1 1/2 in, 90 degrees)", "a polygon (6 sides, ...)" in the model's units. The Eraser erases a sketch's curve whole on a click or across a drag (one undo step); with Ctrl or Shift it flags every segment of a solid's rim. Edit > Explode Curve explodes the selected curves, one undo step (there is no viewport context menu; the Edit menu is the one place). Move, Rotate, Scale and Offset carry a selected curve as one (a mixed selection of curves, edges, regions and solids moves together, one undo step). An inference on a circle's, an arc's or a polygon's segment names its centre (the dot labelled "Centre"); moving there snaps to it, on sketches and on solid rims; the curves' exact centres join the shape-found arcs among plain edges.

### What `.skp` import keeps

(Superseded by the lane-25 addendum at the end: openskp 1.4.0 gives the curve record, and SketchUp curves come in as curves.) As written in lane 23: nothing new: openskp 1.3.0's parse output does not say which edges make up a SketchUp curve, in either file family (the classic 2013-2020 reader reads each `CEdge`'s curve pointer and each `CArcCurve`'s parameters and builds edges without them; the 2021+ reader's edges carry their vertices and flags only). Sawhorse never guesses curves from the edges' shape, so SketchUp's circles, arcs, polygons and welded curves come in as plain edges. The import report said so on every file with an edge until lane 23b; since openskp cannot tell a file with curves from one without, the note was noise, and the fact now lives in the docs only. `docs/skp-compat.md` has the detail; `docs/upstream/openskp-curves.md` asks for it upstream. `.skp` export is not shipped.

### Saved and hashed only when present

`.shz` (`docs/format.md`): a sketch's `curves` (id, kind, vertex indices, closed, segments, and centre / radius / start / sweep for a round kind) and `next_curve` (once a curve was ever made); a B-rep's `curves` (id, kind, closed, segments, keys). The model hash includes a sketch's curves and counter only when it ever had a curve, and a solid's rims only when it has any, so every older document and every golden keeps its hash. A file is refused when a curve breaks the validator's rules or names edges that are not there.

## What the property tests found (and the three kernel fixes)

The hostile property test (`crates/kernel/tests/curve_props.rs`) found three faults, none of them in the curve logic; each also failed without curves, each is fixed where it was, never by loosening a tolerance, and each is kept as a named test:

- **The tessellator's area check trusted the robust predicate's values** (seed 2026102300): a 9 km nonagon and its copy turned 1e-7 degrees bound 0.14 m2 slivers 1.5e-5 m wide bent round the corners; earcut's triangles were right (checked in exact arithmetic), but the check measured them with `robust::orient2d`, whose value is only sign-exact, and the ring as a fan of 5e6 m2 triangles either way: 1.2e-8 off, refused, and the view could not be built. Both sides of the check are now worked out to their own size: products split exactly (`two_product`, a fused multiply-add) and summed with Neumaier's compensated sum (`sawhorse_tessellate::ring_area2`). `a_sliver_bent_round_a_far_corner_triangulates`, `a_huge_nonagon_copied_a_hair_turned_still_draws`.
- **The self-intersection test called a valid solid self-intersecting** (seed 2026102301): a 9 km 67-gon's copy turned 1e-7 degrees leaves slivers whose tips are 1.3e-8 rad wide; pulled, the edge leaving a tip was tested from a point a merge distance along it, 1.3e-13 m off the other side's wall, under the 1e-12 m its coordinates round to, so it fell on the wrong side. An edge from a point of a face's plane meets that plane nowhere else unless it lies in it: such an edge is now tested only when its far end is within the planarity tolerance of the face (`intersect.rs`). `a_wedge_whose_tip_is_a_hair_wide_far_out_is_no_contact`, `a_huge_67_gon_copied_a_hair_turned_pushes_into_solids`.
- **An extruded sliver's faces were fitted, not built** (seed 2026102302): on a tilted plane 9 km out, a sliver's Newell normal was 1e-8 rad off and its own points read 5.6e-5 m off it. `Object::extrude` now gives every face the plane it is built in (the sketch's plane, it moved by the distance, each wall across its edge and the extrusion), origins at the ring's centroid as a fitted plane has them, so ordinary faces keep their triangles. `a_huge_circle_on_a_tilted_plane_copied_a_hair_turned_pushes_flat`.

No golden changed its hash.

## What the fuzzer found (four more fixes)

The op-sequence fuzzer's lane-23 intents found four faults, each kept as a named repro sequence (`crates/kernel/tests/repro/`, replayed on every run). The last two fail the same way on `main` with the curve moved as a region (lane 22's move), so they were older than curves; curves only made a tiny shape far out easier to reach:

- **A heal could lay an edge onto a point** (seed 20269003, `2026-10-19-erase-curve-heals-an-edge-onto-a-copy.json`): erasing a curve left a vertex joining two near-collinear edges, and healing it joined their far ends with one edge that passed within the merge distance of a copy's point, which the validator rightly refuses (a point lying on an edge it is not an end of). A heal now happens only when no other point is within the merge distance of the new edge and no edge properly crosses it; otherwise the two edges stay. This was not curve logic: any erase could reach it, a curve erase only reached it first.
- **Settling could keep a bent piece round** (seeds 20269104 and 20269107, `2026-10-19-tiny-triangle-curve-copied-a-hair-over-itself.json`): a 0.1 mm three-segment circle copied 0.15 mm over itself; a corner of the copy lay 1.1e-5 m from one of the original's chords, inside the twice-merge reach a split segment is linked through, so the original's piece ran out along the copy's edges and back, turning back on itself, and kept its circle; the validator refused it. A piece whose points no longer fit its circle under the validator's own rules (`check_round`) now settles as a polyline, so settling never leaves a curve the validator refuses.
- **A solid's volume was summed about the world origin** (seed 20269306, `2026-10-19-tiny-cylinder-6km-out-turned-measures-its-volume.json`): a 0.1 mm cylinder moved 6.9 km out and copied turned 45 degrees measured 3.5e-6 m3 by its triangles, 244 times its 1.4e-8, because each triangle's term is a product of 6.6 km coordinates; the section invariant caught it. `TriSolid::volume` (and the mesh importer's orientation test) now sum about the solid's own first corner, the same volume for a closed surface and exact to its own size. `a_tiny_cube_far_out_measures_its_volume`.
- **A section half's vector area was summed about the world origin** (seed 20269303, `2026-10-19-tiny-cap-9km-out-closes.json`): a 2.5e-5 m2 cap 9 km out read 3.1e-9 m2 open (Newell's sum over 9 km corners), and the watertightness invariant refused a correct cut. `KeptPart`'s Newell sum is now about each ring's own first corner.

## What the second verifier found (lane 23b)

An independent verifier returned not-ready on `main` 1baa37d; every item fixed, each with a test that would have caught it:

- **An Eraser drag across a closed curve erased nothing** (a regression of lane 23): a drag over the face and the rim sent `Batch[EraseRegion, EraseCurve]`; the region's erase took the curve, the curve's erase was refused, and the batch rolled back with "no curve that one there". The batch tolerates the curve already gone (above), and `eraseOps` leaves out a curve that is exactly the outline of a face it erases (`curvesRingingRegions`: the face's triangles' boundary segments are the curve's; a face with a hole, or a curve that is only part of the outline, keeps its op). Every kernel refusal that names an id now reads as a sentence (`friendlyError`; a test formats every such message of the kernel and the wasm bridge).
- **A section across a hair-thin fin kept a half below zero** (fuzz seed 20271003, plane snapping on; also on `76ecce0`): the kept volume summed each kept face's stored plane times its area there, about the world origin. The fin's faces sit within tolerance of their planes but are a few hundredths of a millimetre wide and 2 m tall, so their stored normals are not their corners'; the half's surface closed (its Newell sum cancels) while the stored normals' sum did not, and 1.5 m from the origin that read as -4.5e-8 m3 of a 9.7e-8 m3 solid. The kept volume is now the cone from the solid's box centre over each kept face ring's own corners (`KeptPart::cone`), which for a closed surface does not depend on the apex; the oracle holds the halves to the whole measured the same way, within twice the faces' bend times their area (`testing::check_sections`). `2026-10-20-section-across-a-fin-pulled-up-from-a-tiny-cylinder.json`, `a_section_across_a_hair_thin_fin_keeps_two_halves_above_zero`. (Superseded by the second fix pass, below: the rings' fans and that allowance are gone.)
- **A rim id of u64::MAX overflowed** in a debug build while a hostile `.shz`'s rims were settled. Curve ids and a sketch's counter above 2^53 - 1 (`curve::MAX_CURVE_ID`, the largest JavaScript reads exactly) are refused on open, and `settle_curves` allocates ids with checked arithmetic. The cases (u64::MAX and 2^53 rims, a rim on an edge that is not there, an edge in two rims, a sketch curve and counter of u64::MAX, a circle with a point off its circle, a radius that is not its points') are in `tampered_snapshots_are_refused`.
- **Scale refused a lone curve point silently** (no grips, no words); it now says why when the tool starts and on a press, in the words Move and Rotate use, so the three tools agree with "The one deliberate divergence" above. Rotate already refused it at its first click, before the gesture starts.
- The friction items: the `.skp` note dropped (above); Scale's status says the new radius; Move, Rotate and Scale count a curve and its face as one shape; the sheet viewport draws soft edges only as the outline (ADR-0010's drawings, lane 15's soft edges); Offset moves a lone curve's radius (above). Filed, not fixed: [#11](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/11) (moving or importing many curves is quadratic), [#12](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/12) (zoomed out, a mid-segment click on a large circle picks a vertex).

## What lane 23b's verifier found (the second fix pass)

The verifier returned not-ready on `main` bc2b393: every by-hand check agreed, and the fuzzer found two section failures, one a regression of lane 23b; it also called lane 23b's bend allowance in the oracle a loosening the evidence did not need. Each fixed with a named repro and a test that fails on bc2b393:

- **The halves were fans over rings, the whole fans over whole rings** (fuzz seed 20281103, plane snapping on, sequence 17,214; passes on 1baa37d): a 700 m 14-gon offset 0.25 m in and the ring pulled 0.25 m down, the four corners of one outer wall moved up a micrometre, a plane through the centre. A fan from a ring's first corner spans the ring's hull, so for the ring round the hole of a 1,063 m2 top face it spans 1.5e6 m2, and on a face bent within tolerance a fan is not the face's triangles: the whole read 266.19 m3 against 265.72 tessellated, beyond lane 23b's twice-bend-times-area slack (0.01 m3). `KeptPart::volume` is now the cone from the solid's box centre over the solid's own triangles (`face_triangles`, the ones `tri_solid` measures it by), each clipped to the kept side (`clip_face` records what each triangle keeps); a triangle's kept and cut-away pieces tile it through the same crossings, so the halves make the tessellated volume to rounding (9e-14 of it here; 1.5e-14 on the fin). `testing::check_sections` holds them to the cone over every triangle within 1e-6 x (1 + whole), as before lane 23b; the bend allowance is gone. `2026-10-20-section-of-a-700m-ring-wall-with-two-corners-nudged-a-micrometre.json`, `a_section_of_a_bent_ring_wall_keeps_halves_that_make_its_tessellated_volume`; the fin test's bound went from 1e-4 to 1e-11 of the whole.
- **The oracle read a far solid's stored planes about the world origin** (fuzz seed 20281207, plane snapping off, sequence 6,000; fails on 1baa37d too): a 700 m arc drawn as 0.34 mm segments and a chord, copied turned 45 degrees, a 3.8 mm sliver pulled 0.5 m down. The measure over the stored planes is now taken about the solid's box centre, and its allowance is derived instead of assumed: a face's triangles and their projection onto its plane enclose at most its bend times its area, and the projections of neighbouring faces leave a strip along every edge, at most the bend wide, through which the integrated field `(x - c) / 3` flows, at most the bend times the face's perimeter times its farthest corner's distance from the centre over three. The old allowance (PLANARITY times the area) left the strips out: the new property test's first find, a ring wall 50 m across and 0.2 mm wide with one corner nudged a micrometre, read 0.012742 m3 over its planes and 0.011888 tessellated about its own centre. A face flat to rounding is allowed nothing beyond the 1e-6 floor, so the check is tighter than before wherever faces are flat. `2026-10-20-section-of-a-sliver-of-a-700m-circle-snap-off.json`, `a_sliver_of_a_700m_circle_measures_its_planes_about_itself`.
- **Tests that pinned the old measure**: the lane-19 section finds now hold each half to its volume worked out without the cut, the cap or any reference point (`section_props::volume_kept`: the flux of the distance beyond the plane through the solid's triangles, clipped by the tie rule) and the halves to the whole to 1e-12 (it was 1e-7). The lane-19b verifier's sliver is 8.354e-9 m3 by both; it had been asserted between 1e-8 and 1.5e-8, its bent top face read through its stored plane. The new property `bent_ring_walls_cut_into_halves_that_make_their_volume` cuts ring walls from 5 cm to 1 km across, up to 5 km out, with a face's corners or any few nudged by up to 5 micrometres, anywhere, with snapping on and off. Its first long run found its own bound short, not the kernel: a half the tie rule leaves open across the normal (up to twice LENGTH_EPS times its seam, as this ADR's cut allows) has a cone that moves with its apex by up to its size times that gap over three (a 5 cm ring cut along a bottom nudged 1e-10 m up read 1.2e-13 m3 below it); the property now allows that term.
- Friction: `srgb_to_linear`'s doc comment is back above it. Not changed here: the commit trailers of lane 23b (history is public), Polygon offset moving the radius (as ADR-0013 says), Ctrl+A selecting faces only, Rotate refusing a curve's point at its first click rather than at tool start, and no on-screen note for a `.skp` circle that came in as edges: design calls left to the owner.

## What the verifier found (the fix pass, three more fixes)

The lane's verifier ran the property test at 100,000 cases with its own seeds and the fuzzer on its own bases; one seed of each failed, both on the triangulator and both the same on `main` before curves (`76ecce0`, curves stripped). Fixed in `crates/tessellate`, never by loosening the 1e-9 check:

- **The area check measured a band's area as a difference of two huge ones** (seed 7303): a 9 km 15-gon offset 6.8e-5 m in bounds a 3.84 m2 band between rings of 2.47e8 m2. Each ring's area was right to the ring's size, so their difference was 2.3e-8 m2 off, 5.9e-9 of the band; earcut's 30 triangles sum to the band exactly (checked in rational arithmetic) and were refused. `polygon_area2` now sums every ring's exactly split products, the holes' negated, in one compensated sum, right to the polygon's own size; the zero-area test uses it too. `a_thin_band_round_a_far_circle_triangulates`, `thin_bands_round_far_polygons_triangulate` (property), `a_huge_circle_offset_a_hair_in_still_draws`.
- **Rings that touch at a point broke both triangulators** (seed 20267102, `2026-10-19-circle-drawn-over-slivers-of-a-moved-quad.json`): two slivers sharing a corner inside a 700 m circle. earcut drops the shared point's second copy and covers the second hole (0.54 m2 too much); the fallback bridged each hole to the ring on its own, so the outline passed through the point twice with wedges that overlap (each hole's outside holds the other hole), which is not a weakly simple polygon, and it clipped a triangle of -80 m2. `ear_clip` now merges rings that share a point by swapping their successors there (one ring round both holes, its two passes through the point bounding the two gaps), decides every turn with `robust::orient2d`, refuses an ear when another pass through one of its corners opens into it or an edge crosses its diagonal, and checks each bridge exactly (out of the hole, into the ring's wedge where it lands, crossing nothing), trying every ring vertex nearest first when the classic choice fails. `cover_all_vertices` leaves a point that is a second copy of a used one alone (it used to split the nearest triangle "on" it, making a clockwise triangle). `holes_that_touch_at_a_point_triangulate`, `holes_touching_at_points_triangulate` (property).

The fix pass's own fuzz run then found a third fault, outside curves and outside the triangulator (a 9 km slab's wall offset 5 km, then cut): **Create Group from Slice drew its caps in a sketch with the section's point as origin** (seed 20269807, plane snapping off, `2026-10-19-slice-of-a-wall-offset-14km-out-along-a-tilted-plane.json`), and a sketch holds points within MAX_COORD of its origin; along a tilted plane through the world's origin the cap lay 10.6 km from it, and the sketch refused a representable cap. The slice now moves its sketch's origin along the plane to where the loops fit (`section::slice_shift`, ADR-0010), and refuses caps more than 20 km apart along the plane in their own words (`SectionError::SliceTooLarge`); the section oracle accepts that refusal only when a representable cap does not fit the section's own frame. `a_slice_far_along_a_tilted_plane_fits_its_sketch`, `a_slice_wider_than_a_sketch_is_refused`. It fails the same way on `a22ca1f`.

The verifier also tampered saved curves by hand and every case was refused; those cases, and the rims', are now a test (`snapshot::tests::tampered_curves_are_refused`). Clean room: nothing was read for the fix pass; Neumaier's sum, Shewchuk's predicates (the `robust` crate the kernel already used) and ear clipping of weakly simple polygons are textbook.

## Consequences

- Every rule above has a kernel test (`crates/kernel/tests/curves.rs`), the hostile property test (`curve_props.rs`: circles and polygons of 3 to 96 segments at radii from 0.1 mm to 9 km, arcs of 0.001 to 359.999 degrees either way, lines ending on a curve's point and on a segment's middle, crossing a segment, crossing two curves at once, along a segment, erases of one segment and of a whole curve, explodes on sketches and solids, offsets of regions and chains, `transform_sketch` of whole curves, regions and lone points by moves, landings, turns, tilts, uniform and non-uniform scales, mirrors and lifts, copies and arrays, pushes and pushes back flat, on the ground and on tilted planes; after every op the validator's curve rules, `check_model`, undo and redo exact, the snapshot through JSON with the same curves; then `check_sequence`), and the op-sequence fuzzer's lane-23 intents (`CurveDraw`, `CurveLine`, `CurveErase`, `CurveExplode`, whole curves in `SketchMove`). See `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md` for the runs.
- The inference engine's cost is the same (a curve's index on each segment, the hint read from the nearest segment within reach): `crates/inference/tests/perf.rs` times it on 9,600-face models (`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`).
- Issue [#7](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/7) (the selection goes stale when a moved region splits) gains a curve form, not fixed here: select the circle itself (its rim) and move it across a rectangle, and the circle splits into arcs where it crosses; the selection keeps the circle's id, which the first arc now carries (`SketchMoved.curves` names first pieces only), so it shrinks to that one arc, highlighted as such, until the next click. Selecting the region inside the circle and moving it behaves exactly as #7 describes.
- The plugin API names edges, not curves: a plugin sees a selected curve as nothing (`itemOfEntity`) and cannot learn a curve's id, though its batches may hold the curve ops and a `draw` with a `curve` like any op (`docs/LIMITS.md`).
- Clean room: the behaviour was taken from SketchUp as a user sees it (Entity Info, the Eraser, Explode Curve, the centre inference); no SketchUp, Hew or hew3d/openskp source was read. The installed openskp package (MIT) was read to establish what its parser exposes.

## Addendum (lane 25, 2026-10-28): curves from `.skp` files

openskp 1.4.0 says which edges make up a SketchUp curve (`Edge.curveId`) and gives every arc's and circle's exact circle (`Definition.arcs`), from classic and 2021+ files. Lane 25 brings them in **through the curve entity this ADR defines, with no second representation**:

- **Sketch curves, through the existing import path.** At the top of the model, a curve lying loose (flat, three points or more) or bounding a flat, unpainted set of faces that holds it whole goes into a sketch as an `Import { strokes: [{ curve }] }` stroke, exactly as a DXF circle does; the faces go with it, each loop a closed stroke, so the sketch finds the face itself. A SketchUp disc is a sketch circle and its region, and pulled it is a cylinder with two circle rims (`Extrude`, above).
- **Rims, through a new field of the import item.** Every other curve rides on its import item as `ImportItem.curves: [{ kind, points, closed }]` (point indices, in order; `crates/kernel/src/meshbuild.rs` `ImportCurve`). After `build_item` builds the item's solids, `apply_curves` matches each curve segment by segment to edges of one solid by their end points (within twice the weld distance, as edge flags are matched) and, when every segment is exactly one edge of that solid lying between two different faces and no pair of faces comes twice (in the curve or in a rim already made), adds a `RimCurve` keyed by those face-id pairs, as `Object::extrude` does. The rim must pass `Object::check_curves`, and a circle's or an arc's points on the solid must read round (`rim_info` within `CURVE_FIT`), or the rim is removed again: the edges stay plain. A curve on a non-solid mesh or on loose lines, or one whose edges share a pair of faces (a circle drawn on a solid's face, the limit above), stays plain edges. A malformed curve (a point index out of range, a circle or polygon not closed, an arc closed, too few points, a point twice) refuses the import, as a malformed edge flag does. Template solids of component definitions take their rims the same way, so every instance derives them; nothing about rims after import is new (they settle, save and hash as in lane 23).
- **One note for both.** The import tallies what became of every curve it was given (`CurveTally`): "3 curves came in as curves (2 circles, 1 arc)", with the kind breakdown when there is more than one kind (a single kind keeps lane 23's wording, "1 curve came in as curve"); strokes said to be curves that are not ("N as plain edges: their points did not land as drawn", now also counting a stroke whose curve spec the kernel refuses, which lane 23 drew silently as plain edges); "N curves on faces that are not a solid (or on no face) came in as plain edges: a mesh keeps no curves"; "N curves drawn on a face of a solid came in as plain edges"; "N round curves whose points on the solid are not on one circle came in as plain edges". A component's curves are counted once, for its definition.
- **Nothing is guessed and no tolerance moves.** The mapping (`src/io/skpCurves.ts`) trusts an arc record only when every point of its chain lies on the record's circle and plane within `CURVE_FIT` and rounding, or where the kernel's own round-curve rule allows a point off it (a split segment's chord point, an open end inside the circle), within the record's angles; a record that disagrees leaves the edges plain with a note naming the piece. A point the kernel's rule allows but its import paths cannot keep round (an end cut on a chord) makes the curve a polyline; an ellipse record (an arc scaled unevenly) makes a polyline; a curve with no record is a polyline.

Kernel tests: `crates/kernel/tests/import_curves.rs` (a cylinder's two circles as rims, through undo, redo and a snapshot; an arc and a closed polyline; a curve on a mesh, drawn on a face and off its circle staying plain, each with its note; malformed curves refused; a component's rim in every copy, riding up when one copy's cap is pulled; a disc as a curve stroke pulled into a solid with rims; strokes and rims counted in one note by kind). No golden hash changed: an import without curves serialises and builds as before.

# ADR-0013: Curves

- Status: accepted (lane 23, 2026-10-19)
- Issue: [#3](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/3), "Clicking a circle, arc or polygon selects one segment or vertex, not the whole curve"
- Source: the lane-23 brief; ADR-0001 (the sketch tier), ADR-0011 (edge flags keyed by face-id pairs), ADR-0012 (moving sketch geometry)

## Context

The Circle (C; C again for Polygon) and Arc tools drew a run of independent sketch edges: a click on a circle's rim selected one of its 24 segments or one of its vertices, the Eraser took one segment, and Move on what was clicked bent the circle. In SketchUp a shape drawn by those tools is **one curve**: any segment selects the whole thing, Move, Rotate and Scale act on it whole, the Eraser removes it whole, a line drawn across it splits it into two arcs, Explode Curve turns it back into plain edges, and its centre is an inference point. The kernel had no such entity; the inference engine found arcs among edges by their shape alone.

## Decision

### The entity (crates/kernel/src/curve.rs, crates/kernel/src/sketch.rs)

A sketch keeps, beside its graph, its **curves**: each an ordered chain of vertex ids (a closed chain does not repeat its first vertex), a **kind** (circle and polygon closed, arc open, polyline either), the number of segments it was drawn with, and for a round kind the circle it lies on (`Round`: centre and radius in the sketch frame, the angle of its first point and the signed angle its points turn through, segment by segment, counter-clockwise about the plane's normal). An edge belongs to at most one curve. Ids come from a counter the sketch keeps and saves (`next_curve`), so a curve's id is the same after undo, redo, a `.shz` round trip and an in-plane move.

The graph's own rules (ADR-0001: merges, T-junctions, crossings, welds, repairs) never look at curves. After every sketch edit the curves are **settled** against the graph (`Sketch::settle_curves`):

1. A curve segment another edge split is taken into the chain: the vertices along the old segment, within twice the merge distance of it, in order (`Sketch::link`). The segment is still the curve's, as a run of pieces; the new vertex sits on a chord, not on the circle.
2. At a **junction** (a chain vertex where an edge that is not the curve's meets it) the curve **splits**: an open curve into two; a closed curve from its **second** junction on (one line ending on a circle leaves the circle whole, as in SketchUp; two make two arcs). A circle or an arc splits into arcs on the same circle (centre and radius kept, the angles read from the piece's points); a polygon or a polyline into polylines. A figure eight's lobes are closed pieces.
3. A curve that **lost** an edge or a point (erased by the plain `EraseSegment`, consumed by a push, a point merged away, an edge now claimed by an older curve it coincides with) becomes **polyline pieces** of what is left: never a chain with a hole in it, never a dangling reference. Pieces too short to be curves (a polyline of one edge) go.

The first piece keeps the curve's id; the others take new ones. A curve's point is never healed away (an erase that leaves it joining two collinear edges keeps it: the chain names it), and a repair merging a curve's point with a plain point keeps the curve's.

The **validator** (`Sketch::check`) holds: every curve's id below the counter; its chain of distinct live vertices joined by edges in order (and back round for a closed one); no edge in two curves; circles and polygons closed, arcs open, round kinds with a circle and polylines without; and every round curve's points on its circle within `CURVE_FIT` (four merge distances plus a part in 1e9 of its size, `tolerance.rs`), or on the segment joining its two neighbours (a chord point), or at an open end no further out than the radius; its start angle pointing at its first point; its points turning one way, by the stored sweep, a closed one once round (`curve::check_round`).

### Ops

- `Draw { curve: Option<CurveSpec> }`: the tools say what a stroke is (`{kind, centre}`). The kernel checks it (a circle or polygon closed with three points or more, an arc open with two or more, every point at one distance from the centre within two merge distances; else `NotACurve`, typed), draws the stroke with the insertion rules as before, then makes it a curve (`Sketch::add_curve`): its points found where the insertion put them, its radius the farthest drawn point, settled at once (a circle drawn across a line is two arcs at once). A stroke that did not land whole, or whose landed points do not fit the circle, stays plain edges, never a curve that lies. On a solid's face the spec is ignored: the stroke cuts or imprints plain edges (below).
- `EraseCurve { sketch, curve }`: every edge of the curve goes, junctions it leaves joining two straight edges heal, regions are re-detected. One undo step.
- `ExplodeCurve { sketch | object, curve }`: the curve goes, its edges stay. One undo step.
- `TransformSketch { curves }` (ADR-0012): whole curves by id; their points move and their edges are carried. A curve whose every edge is carried moves **as one**: within its plane its centre is mapped and its radius scaled when the map, projected into the plane, is a similarity (a move, a turn, a mirror, a uniform scale; `curve::similarity_scale`), and its angles are read again from its points; any other map (a non-uniform scale, a tilt projected into the plane) leaves it one curve, a polyline. Lifted off its plane it is made a curve again in the sketch it lands on (a new id); a copy is a new curve with a new id. A curve only some of whose points move is bent: it becomes a polyline. Outcomes say the (sketch, curve) pairs: `SketchMoved { curves }`, `SketchCopied { curves }`.
- `Offset`: a region's or a chain's round curves offset to curves of their kind: an offset loop with a closed source's point count, all at one distance from its centre, is a circle (or polygon) about the same centre; a run of an arc's points (or two fewer, cut to meet the lines beside it) at one distance from its centre is an arc. `Drawn { curve }` names the first.
- `Import { strokes: [{ curve }] }`: a file's stroke that is one curve (a DXF `CIRCLE` or `ARC`) comes in as a curve when its points land as drawn; the import notes count them ("3 curves came in as curves").

### The one deliberate divergence: a curve's point moved on its own is refused

SketchUp lets you move one point of a circle and bends the curve. Sawhorse refuses it, typed (`TransformError::CurvePoint`: "that point is on a curve: moving it alone would bend the curve. Select the whole curve, or use Edit > Explode Curve first"); the Move, Rotate and Scale tools refuse it before the gesture starts, with the same words (`xform::curvePointRefusal`), for a sketch's curves and for a solid's rims. A curve's points stay individually selectable (a click exactly on one selects that point: curve points are points), and moving an edge or a region that shares a point with a curve still bends the curve (it becomes a polyline, still one curve). The reason: a bent circle silently stops being a circle (its centre, radius and Centre inference are gone), and the commonest way to do it is a stray drag on a rim point while meaning to move the shape. Explode Curve is one click away for the rare deliberate case.

### Solids: rims (keyed like lane 15's edge flags)

Pushing a region into a solid (`Extrude`) gives the solid the **rims** of every curve whose every edge bounds the region: one on the base and one on the cap (`RimCurve`: kind, closed, segment count, and its edges in order, each named by the **pair of persistent face ids** it lies between: the cap, 1 or 2, and the side wall that edge raised; `Object::extrude` numbers the walls ring by ring from 3). As with ADR-0011's flags, a face-id pair follows its edge through moves, copies, mirrors, component definitions and instances, and pushes of other faces with no bookkeeping. After every edit (`Object::settle_curves`, run with `settle_edges`) a rim edge whose key no longer names exactly one edge drops out, and the rim splits there: circle and arc rims into arcs, polygon and polyline rims into polylines. A rim's circle is **read from its points** when asked for (`Object::curve_info`: three points spread along it, every point within `CURVE_FIT` of the circle and its plane), so a scaled solid's rim needs nothing, and a rim scaled out of round reads as a polyline. The validator holds that every key names one edge, the edges meet in order (round the loop for a closed rim), no edge is in two rims.

Pulling a circle or an arc also makes the walls between its consecutive segments **soft and smooth** (SketchUp: a pulled circle is a smooth cylinder). This was not lane 15's doing: the brief asked to verify that lane 15 already made them so, and it did not (lane 15's softening is by angle, on request); with curves the kernel knows which walls belong to one curve. A polygon's and a polyline's walls stay hard (a hexagonal prism has six faces). A solid pushed back flat onto its source sketch gives its base rims back to the sketch as curves.

A circle drawn **on a solid's face** imprints or cuts plain edges: its edges would share one face-id pair (the face and the imprint), so lane 15's keying cannot tell them apart, and no rim is kept (`docs/LIMITS.md`).

### Selection, the Eraser, the transform tools, inference (src/)

The view mesh names each edge segment's curve (`edge_curve`, an index into `curves`: owner, id, kind, closed, segments, centre, radius, sweep, normal); a pick of an edge carries its curve's id. The Select tool takes a curve's segment as the whole curve on hover and click (Shift adds, Ctrl toggles the whole curve; a double-click still selects the solid); a window takes a curve when all its segments are inside, a crossing box when it touches any; the status line says "Selected a circle (24 segments, radius 300 mm)", "an arc (12 segments, radius 1 1/2 in, 90 degrees)", "a polygon (6 sides, ...)" in the model's units. The Eraser erases a sketch's curve whole on a click or across a drag (one undo step); with Ctrl or Shift it flags every segment of a solid's rim. Edit > Explode Curve explodes the selected curves, one undo step (there is no viewport context menu; the Edit menu is the one place). Move, Rotate, Scale and Offset carry a selected curve as one (a mixed selection of curves, edges, regions and solids moves together, one undo step). An inference on a circle's, an arc's or a polygon's segment names its centre (the dot labelled "Centre"); moving there snaps to it, on sketches and on solid rims; the curves' exact centres join the shape-found arcs among plain edges.

### What `.skp` import keeps

Nothing new: openskp 1.3.0's parse output does not say which edges make up a SketchUp curve, in either file family (the classic 2013-2020 reader reads each `CEdge`'s curve pointer and each `CArcCurve`'s parameters and builds edges without them; the 2021+ reader's edges carry their vertices and flags only). Sawhorse never guesses curves from the edges' shape, so SketchUp's circles, arcs, polygons and welded curves come in as plain edges, and the import report says so in one line. `docs/skp-compat.md` has the detail; `docs/upstream/openskp-curves.md` asks for it upstream. `.skp` export is not shipped.

### Saved and hashed only when present

`.shz` (`docs/format.md`): a sketch's `curves` (id, kind, vertex indices, closed, segments, and centre / radius / start / sweep for a round kind) and `next_curve` (once a curve was ever made); a B-rep's `curves` (id, kind, closed, segments, keys). The model hash includes a sketch's curves and counter only when it ever had a curve, and a solid's rims only when it has any, so every older document and every golden keeps its hash. A file is refused when a curve breaks the validator's rules or names edges that are not there.

## What the property tests found (and the three kernel fixes)

The hostile property test (`crates/kernel/tests/curve_props.rs`) found three faults, none of them in the curve logic; each also failed without curves, each is fixed where it was, never by loosening a tolerance, and each is kept as a named test:

- **The tessellator's area check trusted the robust predicate's values** (seed 2026102300): a 9 km nonagon and its copy turned 1e-7 degrees bound 0.14 m2 slivers 1.5e-5 m wide bent round the corners; earcut's triangles were right (checked in exact arithmetic), but the check measured them with `robust::orient2d`, whose value is only sign-exact, and the ring as a fan of 5e6 m2 triangles either way: 1.2e-8 off, refused, and the view could not be built. Both sides of the check are now worked out to their own size: products split exactly (`two_product`, a fused multiply-add) and summed with Neumaier's compensated sum (`sawhorse_tessellate::ring_area2`). `a_sliver_bent_round_a_far_corner_triangulates`, `a_huge_nonagon_copied_a_hair_turned_still_draws`.
- **The self-intersection test called a valid solid self-intersecting** (seed 2026102301): a 9 km 67-gon's copy turned 1e-7 degrees leaves slivers whose tips are 1.3e-8 rad wide; pulled, the edge leaving a tip was tested from a point a merge distance along it, 1.3e-13 m off the other side's wall, under the 1e-12 m its coordinates round to, so it fell on the wrong side. An edge from a point of a face's plane meets that plane nowhere else unless it lies in it: such an edge is now tested only when its far end is within the planarity tolerance of the face (`intersect.rs`). `a_wedge_whose_tip_is_a_hair_wide_far_out_is_no_contact`, `a_huge_67_gon_copied_a_hair_turned_pushes_into_solids`.
- **An extruded sliver's faces were fitted, not built** (seed 2026102302): on a tilted plane 9 km out, a sliver's Newell normal was 1e-8 rad off and its own points read 5.6e-5 m off it. `Object::extrude` now gives every face the plane it is built in (the sketch's plane, it moved by the distance, each wall across its edge and the extrusion), origins at the ring's centroid as a fitted plane has them, so ordinary faces keep their triangles. `a_huge_circle_on_a_tilted_plane_copied_a_hair_turned_pushes_flat`.

No golden changed its hash.

## What the fuzzer found (four more fixes)

The op-sequence fuzzer's lane-23 intents found four faults, each kept as a named repro sequence (`crates/kernel/tests/repro/`, replayed on every run). The last two fail the same way on `main` with the curve moved as a region (lane 22's move), so they were older than curves; curves only made a tiny shape far out easier to reach:

- **A heal could lay an edge onto a point** (seed 20269003, `2026-10-19-erase-curve-heals-an-edge-onto-a-copy.json`): erasing a curve left a vertex joining two near-collinear edges, and healing it joined their far ends with one edge that passed within the merge distance of a copy's point, which the validator rightly refuses (a point lying on an edge it is not an end of). A heal now happens only when no other point is within the merge distance of the new edge and no edge properly crosses it; otherwise the two edges stay. This was not curve logic: any erase could reach it, a curve erase only reached it first.
- **Settling could keep a bent piece round** (seeds 20269104 and 20269107, `2026-10-19-tiny-triangle-curve-copied-a-hair-over-itself.json`): a 0.1 mm three-segment circle copied 0.15 mm over itself; a corner of the copy lay 1.1e-5 m from one of the original's chords, inside the twice-merge reach a split segment is linked through, so the original's piece ran out along the copy's edges and back, turning back on itself, and kept its circle; the validator refused it. A piece whose points no longer fit its circle under the validator's own rules (`check_round`) now settles as a polyline, so settling never leaves a curve the validator refuses.
- **A solid's volume was summed about the world origin** (seed 20269306, `2026-10-19-tiny-cylinder-6km-out-turned-measures-its-volume.json`): a 0.1 mm cylinder moved 6.9 km out and copied turned 45 degrees measured 3.5e-6 m3 by its triangles, 244 times its 1.4e-8, because each triangle's term is a product of 6.6 km coordinates; the section invariant caught it. `TriSolid::volume` (and the mesh importer's orientation test) now sum about the solid's own first corner, the same volume for a closed surface and exact to its own size. `a_tiny_cube_far_out_measures_its_volume`.
- **A section half's vector area was summed about the world origin** (seed 20269303, `2026-10-19-tiny-cap-9km-out-closes.json`): a 2.5e-5 m2 cap 9 km out read 3.1e-9 m2 open (Newell's sum over 9 km corners), and the watertightness invariant refused a correct cut. `KeptPart`'s Newell sum is now about each ring's own first corner.

## Consequences

- Every rule above has a kernel test (`crates/kernel/tests/curves.rs`), the hostile property test (`curve_props.rs`: circles and polygons of 3 to 96 segments at radii from 0.1 mm to 9 km, arcs of 0.001 to 359.999 degrees either way, lines ending on a curve's point and on a segment's middle, crossing a segment, crossing two curves at once, along a segment, erases of one segment and of a whole curve, explodes on sketches and solids, offsets of regions and chains, `transform_sketch` of whole curves, regions and lone points by moves, landings, turns, tilts, uniform and non-uniform scales, mirrors and lifts, copies and arrays, pushes and pushes back flat, on the ground and on tilted planes; after every op the validator's curve rules, `check_model`, undo and redo exact, the snapshot through JSON with the same curves; then `check_sequence`), and the op-sequence fuzzer's lane-23 intents (`CurveDraw`, `CurveLine`, `CurveErase`, `CurveExplode`, whole curves in `SketchMove`). See `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md` for the runs.
- The inference engine's cost is the same (a curve's index on each segment, the hint read from the nearest segment within reach): `crates/inference/tests/perf.rs` times it on 9,600-face models (`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`).
- Issue [#7](https://github.com/sawhorse3d/sawhorse/issues/7) (the selection goes stale when a moved region splits) gains a curve form, not fixed here: select the circle itself (its rim) and move it across a rectangle, and the circle splits into arcs where it crosses; the selection keeps the circle's id, which the first arc now carries (`SketchMoved.curves` names first pieces only), so it shrinks to that one arc, highlighted as such, until the next click. Selecting the region inside the circle and moving it behaves exactly as #7 describes.
- The plugin API names edges, not curves: a plugin sees a selected curve as nothing (`itemOfEntity`) and cannot learn a curve's id, though its batches may hold the curve ops and a `draw` with a `curve` like any op (`docs/LIMITS.md`).
- Clean room: the behaviour was taken from SketchUp as a user sees it (Entity Info, the Eraser, Explode Curve, the centre inference); no SketchUp, Hew or hew3d/openskp source was read. The installed openskp package (MIT) was read to establish what its parser exposes.
